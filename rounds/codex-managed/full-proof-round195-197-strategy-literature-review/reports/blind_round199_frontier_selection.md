# Statement-only Round-198 frontier selection

## 1. Result: frontier-selection proposition

Under the isolated statement and the hypotheses in Section 2, the eight frontiers rank as follows for a **single bounded Round-199 theorem-or-no-go task**:

1. exact three-piece physical \(P_2\) complement, with the literal sharp-code boundary as the smallest first gate;
2. M2 hard TOP;
3. smooth direct M1;
4. M2 unbalanced;
5. M2 balanced;
6. lower angular-radial M1;
7. disjoint physical \(P_1\);
8. the remaining original-\(t\) and large-common-factor hard-M1 ranges.

The unique recommendation is the following single objective.

> **Round-199 literal sharp-boundary gate.** On the exact original-\(t=1\) physical \(P_2\) packets
> \[
> \Omega:=\{\kappa<D_L,\ \min(Y,D_L)>H_B\mathfrak m\kappa\},
> \qquad D_L=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil,
> \]
> determine the restriction to \(P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\) while retaining the exact literal coefficient and all arithmetic, support, zero-extension, endpoint, and sharp-branch states before any absolute value, square, energy, or other positive-norm relaxation. The theorem exit is the uniform outer bound
> \[
> \bigl|\mathcal T_{P_2}(\Omega\cap P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}})\bigr|
> \ll_\varepsilon L^2X^\varepsilon,
> \]
> where \(\mathcal T_{P_2}\) denotes the already-defined literal original-\(t=1\) physical \(P_2\) contribution with no normalization change. The no-go exit is a legal literal-coefficient family in the same packet and allowed parameter range which proves that the required \(D_L\)-saving is unavailable on this boundary, or a sharply delimited impossibility theorem for a fully specified coefficient-preserving mechanism class. A no-go about an unsigned relaxation alone is not enough.

This is a strategy-selection result only. The symbol \(\mathcal T_{P_2}\) is referential because its summation formula is deliberately absent from the statement-only packet. Round 199 must freeze that formula and the allowed parameter range before mathematical work begins. A theorem exit promotes only \(\Omega\cap P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\), not \(P_{s\mathrm f}\), \(P_{g\mathrm f}\), all of \(P_2\), hard M1, M9, an endpoint statement, either bridge, or an exponent. A no-go exit promotes no estimate.

The target is

There are exactly two lawful routes in the isolated packet:

\[
d=g\alpha,\qquad d'=g\beta,\qquad D_L=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil.

The already target-safe \(P_2\) portions are \(\kappa\ge D_L\), the packets with
\(\kappa<D_L\) and \(\min(Y,D_L)\le H_B\mathfrak m\kappa\), and, inside the complementary packets \(\Omega\), the exact common-cell mask
\[
P_{\rm cc}=P_2\mathbf1_{(m,\beta)=1}

\]
Here \(C_{\rm lit}\) records literal arithmetic, support, zero-extension, and sharp-branch states, but contains no coefficient-value or nonvanishing hypothesis. There is no accepted nonemptiness, density, or mass assertion for \(P_{\rm cc}\). The exact residual is
\[
P_2\mathbf1_\Omega
=\bigl(P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\dot\cup P_{s\mathrm f}\dot\cup P_{g\mathrm f}\bigr)\mathbf1_\Omega.

The ranking uses the following lexicographic criteria: (i) an exact literal interface and target; (ii) a bounded kernel with a measurable missing factor; (iii) a genuinely new cancellation seam not already blocked by the stated mechanism obstruction; (iv) a clean theorem/no-go exit; and only then (v) downstream leverage. This ordering implements the protocol's smallest-proof-kernel rule. It prevents a very broad, high-leverage owner from outranking a sharply testable seam merely because completing the broad owner would be valuable.

The internal \(1/3\), external \(0.3144831759740614\ldots\), and desired \(1/4\) exponents remain distinct. Any sub-\(1/3\) result that does not attain its frozen quarter-route interface remains in a graded intermediate lane and cannot be reported as the quarter theorem.

## 3. Proof or derivation of the ranking

### 3.1 Dependency and separation argument

The two lawful routes share complete M2 and downstream connectors, but have different M1 owners. Thus no direct-M1 sector can be silently transferred to the angular-radial route, and no angular-radial layer completes direct M1. M2 hard TOP, balanced, and unbalanced are separate parents within complete M2. Endpoint uniformity and both bridges remain independent connectors. Therefore the correct leverage of every item below is its exact owner effect, not its imagined effect on the final exponent.

The recent \(P_2\) result has an exact disjoint residual. Even complete control of that residual would leave \(P_1\), the rest of original \(t=1\), all original \(t\ge2\) small-common-factor incidences, and the large-common-factor near-resonant complement. This limits downstream leverage, but the literal boundary still ranks first because it is the only candidate combining an exact packet, an exact target, a named literal submask, a quantified missing factor, and a one-seam promotion boundary.

### 3.2 Rank 1: exact three-piece \(P_2\) complement

- **Exact target.** Outer \(O_\varepsilon(L^2X^\varepsilon)\) on \(\Omega\cap(P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\dot\cup P_{s\mathrm f}\dot\cup P_{g\mathrm f})\). The recommended first gate asks for that bound only on \(\Omega\cap P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\).
- **Current positive capacity.** An aligned literal ratio face may be crossed by every lower-close pair and retain geometric positive capacity \(D_LL^2X^\varepsilon\). This is a capacity of the positive/geometric relaxation, not a lower bound for the literal signed operator. No separate capacities for \(P_{s\mathrm f}\) and \(P_{g\mathrm f}\) are stated.
- **Fixed-power deficit.** The boundary relaxation misses a factor \(D_L=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil\), hence a fixed \(L^{1/2}\)-scale saving. No numerical deficit is supplied for the other two pieces.
- **Literal coefficient interface.** The original physical coefficient must be kept together with \(\chi_4(\alpha m)\), gcd data, lower-allocation state, literal support and zero-extension, and the sharp-branch code. Because \(C_{\rm lit}\) contains no coefficient-value or nonvanishing condition, the missing theorem must use the actual coefficient formula in addition to the code; the code alone cannot supply cancellation.
- **Strongest obstruction.** The aligned sharp face defeats pure face-crossing geometry by retaining the factor \(D_L\). The full four-corner cross-coprime rectangle forces \(\kappa=1\), so it cannot cover all \(2\le\kappa<D_L\). Neither fact is a lower bound for the literal operator.
- **Downstream owner leverage.** A boundary theorem removes exactly one piece of the three-piece \(P_2\) residual. Even a complete three-piece theorem removes only \(P_2\) on \(\Omega\); it does not complete hard M1.
- **Genuinely new theorem.** A coefficient-sensitive sharp-boundary cancellation theorem gaining \(D_L\), uniform in the exact packet variables, or a literal signed obstruction in the same interface. Reusing allocation-swap geometry or the cross-coprime rectangle is not new.
- **False controls.** Replacing literal coefficients by absolute values or adversarial phases must recover the \(D_LL^2\) obstruction rather than the target. One must also reject: treating aligned capacity as an operator lower bound; extrapolating a \(\kappa=1\) rectangle to \(2\le\kappa<D_L\); and inferring nonemptiness or mass from the \(P_{\rm cc}\) upper bound.
- **Stop rule.** Stop with no theorem if the proof takes a positive norm before identifying a coefficient identity that yields the \(D_L\)-saving, if it changes support or sharp-branch states, or if it controls only \(P_{s\mathrm f}\) or \(P_{g\mathrm f}\) while claiming the boundary. A successful theorem is quarantined to \(\Omega\cap P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\).

### 3.3 Rank 2: M2 hard TOP

- **Exact target.** \(L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon\).
- **Current positive capacity.** The diagonal and exact-product sectors are target-safe. On the unequal-product near-cell rows, coefficient-uniform row positivity misses a factor \(L\), so its stated scale is target times \(L\), namely \(L^{5/2}X^\varepsilon\).
- **Fixed-power deficit.** Exactly one factor \(L\) on the open row channel.
- **Literal coefficient interface.** The actual signed unequal-product near-cell row, before coefficient-uniform positivity. The isolated packet does not give the coefficient formula, so it must be frozen in any subsequent brief.
- **Strongest obstruction.** The row representation returns to the original scalar concentration problem; uniform positivity erases the only possible coefficient gain and loses \(L\).
- **Downstream owner leverage.** It could complete the remaining hard-TOP channel, but balanced and unbalanced M2 and all downstream connectors remain.
- **Genuinely new theorem.** A literal coefficient-sensitive row cancellation or non-scalar operator estimate which gains the full factor \(L\) without importing diagonal or exact-product structure into unequal products.
- **False controls.** The theorem must fail after unsigned/adversarial replacement if its saving is genuinely sign-based. Safe diagonal and exact-product channels cannot be used as controls purporting to prove the unequal-product channel.
- **Stop rule.** Stop if the estimate self-returns to scalar concentration, leaves any fixed \(L^\delta\) loss, or proves only the already safe channels.

This ranks below the sharp-boundary gate because its literal formula is absent and the needed gain is larger, although its M2-owner leverage is greater.

### 3.4 Rank 3: smooth direct M1

- **Exact target.** \(X^{1/4+\varepsilon}\) at the first critical smooth block, uniformly through the full smooth owner.
- **Current positive capacity.** Accepted menu capacity \(X^{1/3+o(1)}\).
- **Fixed-power deficit.** \(X^{1/12-o(1)}\), since \(1/3-1/4=1/12\).
- **Literal coefficient interface.** Every Vaaler taper, sign, profile, floor, star, endpoint, and support crossing remains literal.
- **Strongest obstruction.** A fixed power, not a logarithm or endpoint constant, separates the available menu bound from the target; simplifying any listed feature changes the interface.
- **Downstream owner leverage.** A uniform theorem could close the smooth portion of direct M1, but hard M1, complete M2, endpoints, bridges, and final assembly remain.
- **Genuinely new theorem.** A full-feature signed smooth-block estimate supplying the entire \(X^{1/12-o(1)}\) gain and then uniformizing across the smooth owner.
- **False controls.** The same assertion with absolute or adversarial coefficients, or after freezing/dropping taper, floors, stars, endpoints, or support crossings, is not the literal theorem and must not be implied.
- **Stop rule.** Stop if the result remains \(X^{1/4+\delta}\) for fixed \(\delta>0\), applies only to the first profile without the stated uniform continuation, or removes any literal feature.

This ranks below hard TOP because the missing power is a direct \(X^{1/12}\) gap and the theorem must simultaneously survive a larger list of crossings.

### 3.5 Rank 4: M2 unbalanced

- **Exact target.** \(M^{3/4}X^\varepsilon\).
- **Current positive capacity.** Every stated scalar route has scale equal to the target times an unresolved boundary factor, or self-returns. The isolated packet does not state that factor's size, so no sharper capacity formula is justified.
- **Fixed-power deficit.** Not numerically identifiable from the isolated statement. The exact known deficit is the required boundary factor; it must not be silently converted into an \(M\)-power.
- **Literal coefficient interface.** A signed vector/matrix formulation with the original coefficients and boundary truncation intact.
- **Strongest obstruction.** Scalar inverse-residue, reciprocity, and complete-frequency transformations either lose the boundary factor or return to the starting scalar problem.
- **Downstream owner leverage.** A target theorem could close the unbalanced M2 owner, but hard TOP, balanced M2, and all connectors remain.
- **Genuinely new theorem.** The explicitly missing literal signed vector/matrix theorem at \(M^{3/4}X^\varepsilon\), with a boundary-aware operator norm that is not obtained by scalarization.
- **False controls.** Unsigned/adversarial matrices and complete-frequency models without the literal boundary must not inherit the signed target estimate. Any scalar corollary that has already lost the boundary factor is a failed control.
- **Stop rule.** Stop on scalarization, boundary loss, self-return, or any unspecified residual boundary factor.

This ranks below the first three because the needed boundary factor is not quantified in the packet, despite being the clearest genuinely vectorial high-leverage seam.

- **False controls.** A fixed-mask, paired-interior-only, collar-only, unsigned, or adversarial estimate cannot be promoted to the total active-M1 theorem. The cross owner cannot be inferred from the radial owner.
- **Stop rule.** Stop if the calculation returns to the same hard-defect wave, closes only \(D=d=L=1\), omits any layer, or borrows the independent cross owner.

This ranks below the M2 candidates because its full owner objective is multi-layer and its exact local target power is not stated, notwithstanding its high route leverage.

- **Exact target.** Outer \(O_\varepsilon(L^2X^\varepsilon)\) separately for the rest of original \(t=1\), every original \(t\ge2\) small-common-factor incidence, and the large-common-factor near-resonant complement.

- **Stop rule.** Stop if one range is promoted as the union, if a hidden \(t\)- or gcd-sum costs a fixed power, or if the coefficient/support interface is not stated separately for each range.

This ranks last because it is the least atomic item and has no quantitative capacity or shared literal formula in the statement-only packet.

### 3.10 Why the recommended gate is uniquely first

The literal boundary gate is narrower than the full three-piece residual, so it obeys the smallest-kernel rule. It has a measurable factor \(D_L\) to gain, a known geometric obstruction that a new proof must bypass, and an exact sector-level promotion boundary. The other two pieces of the same residual rank lower as immediate Round-199 subobjectives because their capacities and distinct coefficient obstructions are not stated. Every other frontier ranks lower for at least one concrete reason: hard TOP has a larger \(L\)-loss and no formula; smooth M1 has a fixed \(X^{1/12}\) gap and many simultaneous crossings; unbalanced M2 has an unquantified boundary loss; balanced M2 has two independent seams; angular-radial M1 is a multi-layer total theorem with a self-return; \(P_1\) lacks a frozen formula and capacity; and the remaining hard ranges are a non-atomic collection.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first mathematical doubt is whether the actual literal coefficient has an identity across the aligned sharp face that can gain the full factor \(D_L\) before positivity. The isolated statement establishes only that geometry by itself can retain \(D_L\) positive capacity. It neither supplies the coefficient formula nor proves nonvanishing, sign variation, orthogonality, or cancellation on the boundary.

The first specification gap is earlier still: the blind packet does not define \(\mathcal T_{P_2}\), \(P_{\partial\mathrm{lit}}\), \(P_{s\mathrm f}\), or \(P_{g\mathrm f}\) by literal summation formulas, and it does not state the allowed \(L\)-versus-\(X\) range. Consequently the displayed Round-199 objective is exact only as an owner restriction of the pre-existing literal operator; it is not a self-contained analytic lemma until those definitions are placed in the Round-199 brief.

Several competing deficits are likewise intentionally reported as unknown rather than guessed: the lower angular-radial target power, balanced positive capacity, unbalanced boundary-factor size, \(P_1\) capacity, and capacities of the remaining hard ranges. Finally, statement-only isolation supplies no primary sources, so this report makes no claim that literature through 2026-08-31 does or does not contain a matching theorem.
