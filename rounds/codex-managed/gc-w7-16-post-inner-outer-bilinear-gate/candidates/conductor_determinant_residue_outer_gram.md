## 1. Result

Two exact facts constrain the Round-130 interface.

First, on the top reduced shell the numerator increment is not a second
independent long oscillatory variable.  Reassemble the Möbius pieces to
the physical primitive inner ray, then fix one primitive outer ray
\((a,b)\) and write \(a'=a+p\).

\[
 n=ab'-a'b.
\]

Then

\[
 b'={b(a+p)+n\over a},
 \qquad n\equiv-bp\pmod a.
 \tag{130.C1}
\]

Because \((a,b)=1\), every residue \(n\pmod {|a|}\) selects exactly one
residue \(p\pmod {|a|}\).  On the literal top shell, assume the integer
\(p\)-support is a union of \(O(1)\) intervals of span \(O(|a|)\), with
\(|a|\asymp L\).  Then each determinant \(n\) has only \(O(1)\)
admissible \(p\)'s.  The reciprocal progressions are interlaced residue
classes in the determinant coordinate, not two independent opportunities
for square-root cancellation.

Second, for the fully completed random-cell operator \(T=G\), completing
the corresponding outer square function produces the ray-space pullback
\(T^*T=G^2\).  The literal resolved post-inner family instead has an
incidence map \(H\) and exact energy

\[
 \|H\mathbf 1\|_2^2=\mathbf 1^*H^*H\mathbf 1.
\]

Equivalently, after physical reassembly the literal one-sided shell row is
an operator \(K_{B,+}b\), with exact energy
\(b^*K_{B,+}^*K_{B,+}b\).  Identifying either resolved formulation with
\(b^*G^2b\) requires a separate vector-level completion connector,
including all cross pieces and owners.  Either
positive energy is a sufficient but generally strictly stronger problem
than the scalar correlation.  Hence a support-only outer-energy proof
cannot be inferred from the scalar target.

These facts do not refute a genuinely signed actual-coefficient bilinear
estimate.  They reject only independent-\(p\) heuristics and automatic
outer \(L^2\) completion.

## 2. Exact determinant-residue chart

At the top shell,

\[
 a,a'\asymp L,\qquad b,b'\asymp D,
 \qquad a'=a+p,\quad b'=b+q,
\]

and

\[
 n=aq-bp.
 \tag{130.C2}
\]

For fixed \(p\), the determinant strip makes \(q\) run through an
interval of length

\[
 Q_*={D^2\over WL}.
\]

Consequently \(n\) runs in one residue class modulo \(a\), through an
interval of length \(\asymp |a|Q_*\).  As \(p\) runs through \(O(1)\)
intervals of span \(O(|a|)\), the congruences in (130.C1) interlace these
classes with bounded multiplicity.  Their literal determinant intervals,
half-open endpoints, and tapers must still be retained; the congruence
alone does not identify one common contiguous determinant interval.  The
number of physical \((p,q)\) atoms is unchanged.  The auxiliary
\(\rho,\eta\) and threshold labels are algebraic summands that reconstruct
the physical primitive coefficient; they are not extra determinant
representations.  Character and owner data remain attached to each atom.

The phase becomes exactly

\[
 {cn\over\kappa_i bb'}
 ={can\over\kappa_i b\{b(a+p)+n\}}.
 \tag{130.C3}
\]

M1's denominator character is the exact quarter twist in \(b'\); M2's
numerator character is the exact parity twist in \(p\).  Under (130.C1)
these become residue twists of the determinant sequence.  They are not
deleted, but neither supplies an independent length-\(L\) variable.

Thus a valid continuation must prove cancellation between the interlaced
residue classes in (130.C3), with the actual profiles and characters.  A
Cauchy gain assigned separately to the \(p\)-count has no algebraic
premise.

## 3. Exact outer Gram

Let \(\mathscr R\) be the complete reduced-ray set in one fixed block,
let

\[
 \lambda_r={a\over\kappa_i b},\qquad
 b_r=A_i(r)e(c\lambda_r),
\]

and define the exact triangular Gram matrix

\[
 G_{rs}=(1-W|\lambda_r-\lambda_s|)_+.
 \tag{130.C4}
\]

The random-cell identity gives the complete positive cluster energy

\[
 \mathcal C_i=b^*Gb.
 \tag{130.C5}
\]

For the specifically defined completed random-cell operator \(T=G\), put
\(F^{\rm comp}=Gb\).  Its outer square energy is

\[
 \sum_r|F_r|^2=b^*G^2b.
 \tag{130.C6}
\]

Equation (130.C6) is exact for this completed operator.  Since \(G\) is
positive and not generally a projection, the spectral implication
available without actual-vector information is

\[
 b^*G^2b\le\|G\|\,b^*Gb,
 \tag{130.C7}
\]

and the factor \(\|G\|\) is its spectral capacity.  A comparison with a
local weighted ray-density count requires a separate Schur/geometric
estimate.
The centre phase is only a diagonal unitary modulation and cannot reduce
the spectrum of \(G\).

The Round-130 one-sided \(F_i(r)\) is instead the literal value
\(H\mathbf 1\).  After reassembling the auxiliary labels this is the
physical one-sided row \(K_{B,+}b\), so its ray-space energy is exactly
\(b^*K_{B,+}^*K_{B,+}b\).  Identifying it with a shell- and
orientation-resolved piece of \(Gb\) requires a proved completion
connector.  Such a connector must assign every cross-piece term, diagonal,
taper, character, and owner.  Without it the exact atom-space energy is
\(\mathbf 1^*H^*H\mathbf 1\), equivalently the preceding resolved
ray-space form, not automatically (130.C6).  In either formulation the
proposed target energy is sufficient, not scalar-equivalent.

## 4. False controls and known returns

A top eigenvector of \(G\), followed by the inverse centre-phase
modulation, saturates the spectral ratio in (130.C7).  Without a separate
delocalization check it need not satisfy the literal \(L^\infty\), profile,
or threshold hypotheses, so this is only a coefficient-blind spectral
control.  The support- and norm-matched incidence controls in the blind
and hostile reports separately prove that the displayed marginal facts do
not imply a target outer square function.

Reversing the order of summation does not itself create a gain.  For fixed
\(b'\), the \(p\)-interval has length \(T=\min(L,D/W)\).  The direct
post-inner product-window ledger in the Round-130 discovery report returns
the crude scale \(D^2/L\) at the top shell, while exact inversion to the
accepted complete original-variable theorem returns
\(D^2/L^2+WD/L=Y^{37/48+o(1)}\).  Both are weaker than Round 129's
\(DK_B\).  Applying reciprocal transforms in
both \(p\) and \(q\) has the accepted unit-order Hessian determinant and
repays the first dual saving after an aliaswise modulus.

These are method controls, not lower bounds for the literal coefficient
vector.

## 5. Capacity

Round 129 proves

\[
 |\mathfrak O_i|\ll DK_B Y^\varepsilon,
 \qquad K_B=Y^{11/48+o(1)},
\]

while the target is \(D\).  Cauchy with
\(\sum_r|A_i(r)|^2\ll D/L\) would close the target if the exact resolved
row obeyed

\[
 \sum_r|F_i(r)|^2\ll LDY^\varepsilon.
 \tag{130.C8}
\]

The exact incidence formula and the conditional completed formula
(130.C6) show why (130.C8) is a new actual-vector spectral theorem, not a
consequence of the scalar target or the ray energy.  Even a strict power
improvement in (130.C8) is meaningful only after its one-sided pieces and
owners are reassembled without positive completion loss.

## 6. First doubtful step

The first genuinely open step is an actual-coefficient theorem excluding
the phase-aligned directions of the literal incidence Gram \(H^*H\), or
after a proved completion connector, the high-eigenvalue directions of
the triangular operator.  It must retain the residue twists in (130.C3).
Neither the common lift character already consumed in Round 129 nor the
pointwise and energy bounds on \(A_i\) state such a theorem.

The determinant-residue chart leaves open cancellation across complete
residue classes.  A future proof could still exploit the exact M1/M2
character and sampled profiles there; it may not count \(p\) as an
independent oscillatory dimension or replace the scalar by a positive
energy without proving the required connector and the energy estimate.
