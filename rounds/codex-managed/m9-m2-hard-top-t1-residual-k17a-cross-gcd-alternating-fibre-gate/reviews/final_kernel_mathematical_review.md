## 4. Hyperbola count and strict owner theorem

Fix \(\kappa\) and put \(Y=L/\kappa\).  Positivity of the two gaps makes
the inward cross product smaller than both supported endpoint products,
so

\[
 uv\ll Y^2.
\]

The shift condition gives \(n\ll Y\).  A primitive row advances through
the product shell in steps of \(2\kappa uv\), hence contains at most

\[
 O\!\left(1+{L^2\over\kappa uv}\right)
\]

sites.  Therefore, for either orientation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_\kappa
 &\ll X^\varepsilon
 \sum_{uv\ll Y^2}\sum_{n\ll Y}
 \left(1+{L^2\over\kappa uv}\right)\\
 &\ll X^\varepsilon\left(
 Y^3\log(2Y)+{L^2Y\over\kappa}\log^2(2Y)
 \right)
 \ll {L^3\over\kappa^2}X^\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]

The logarithms are absorbed by the usual auxiliary-\(\varepsilon\)
renaming.  Both orientations change only the constant.  Squarefree,
selector, parity, original-gcd, endpoint, and non-polylogarithmic
conditions delete atoms, while the Fejer factor and endpoint coefficients
are bounded.  Hence

\[
 \sum_{\kappa\ge K}C_\kappa
 \ll {L^3\over K}X^\varepsilon.
\]

Taking \(K=\delta L\) proves (176.K15) for every fixed
\(0<\delta<1/2\).  The bound controls the modulus of the complete complex
subaggregate and therefore its outer real part.  Its exact complement is
\(\kappa_*<\delta L\).  If \(\delta\) varies, the kernel correctly retains
the \(\delta^{-1}\) loss.  The proof uses the product-hyperbola count, so
it does not depend on an unjustified global \(T\ll1+\kappa\) row claim.

## 5. Route-capacity scope and diagnostics

\[

(O(1+2Jn/L)) half-lattice modes, one saddle has size

\[

the rowwise positive-transform self-return without claiming a lower bound

over anchors gives (O(L^3)).  The kernel explicitly says that this does
not execute Poisson on the discontinuous literal selector and does not
exclude signed dual cancellation.

The remaining route capacities are also correctly scoped.  Positive
