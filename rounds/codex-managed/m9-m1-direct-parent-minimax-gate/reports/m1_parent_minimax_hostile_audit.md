## 1. Result

**Separate-parent menu-frontier lemma, with a mandatory no-lower-bound
qualification.**  After the accepted bottom and Fejer owners are separated
and the terminal, full second-derivative, and TTY main owners are removed in
priority order, the remaining direct M1 labels split literally into the
unique hard profile containing

\[
y=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor
\]

and all other, smooth profiles.  Relative to the complete accepted direct
block menu, the worst power capacity of **each** open physical parent is
exactly (1/3):

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak c_{\rm hard}=\mathfrak c_{\rm smooth}=\frac13.}       \tag{1.1}
\]

This is a statement about the minimum of available upper-bound formulas,
not a lower bound for either actual reciprocal sum.  Literal asymptotic
witnesses exist.  The hard profile (D_0\asymp y) and the first smooth
profile (D_1\asymp y/2) each possess an un-clipped dyadic frequency label
(L\asymp X^{1/6}).  Their logarithmic coordinates tend to
((\delta,\ell)=(1/2,1/6)), they lie in the residual set
(\mathcal U_1), and the TTY exponent tends to

\[
\frac{770}{1923}=\frac13+\frac{43}{641}>\frac13.             \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus TTY does not lower either witness, while the frequency-first and full
second-derivative rows meet at (1/3).

For the hard transformed block, the same capacity is exactly the accepted
normalization ledger

\[
\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L}:\qquad
L^2\ \hbox{(coefficient-blind capacity)}
\quad\hbox{versus}\quad
L^{3/2}\ \hbox{(required target)},                         \tag{1.3}
\]

so the missing factor at (L\asymp X^{1/6}) is
(L^{1/2}=X^{1/12+o(1)}).  This too is a capacity deficit, not a signed
lower bound.

No accepted adjacent-profile identity shrinks either complete physical
parent.  The raw denominator partition is an additive, nonnegative
one-count identity; the hard and first-smooth coefficients have different
profiles and different Vaaler heights, and only the hard profile contains

## 3. Proof or derivation

**Owner calculation.**  On (\Omega), frequency-first reaches the quarter
target only when (a\le1/4), which is exactly the terminal boundary.
The dominant second-derivative exponent ((1-a)/2) reaches the quarter
target only when (a\ge1/2); since (a\le\delta\le1/2), this is only
((\delta,\ell)=(1/2,0)).  TTY reaches the target exactly on
(178\ell+1638\delta\le463).  Removing the terminal/TTY overlap according
to priority leaves precisely (2.3).  The accepted profile certificate then
splits every literal label of (2.3) exactly once by whether its support
contains (y).  Neither a power exponent nor a transformed conductor
label can replace that physical test.

**Universal upper menu frontier.**  Since (1/4\le a\le1/2),

\[
\min\left(a,{1-a\over2}\right)\le\frac13,           \tag{3.1}
\]

with equality only at (a=1/3).  The trivial exponent cannot improve this
upper frontier because (\delta\ge a).  Adding TTY can only decrease the
minimum, never increase it.  Hence

\[
\mathfrak c_{\rm hard}\le\frac13,qquad
\mathfrak c_{\rm sm}\le\frac13.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

**Literal hard and smooth witnesses.**  Use the actual scales

\[
D_0\asymp y,qquad D_1\asymp y/2,
\]

where (D_0) is the unique hard label and (D_1) is the first smooth
label.  Both are active for all sufficiently large (X), and the smooth
profile is a full fixed (C_c^\infty) rescaling.  Their heights satisfy

\[
H_j=\lfloor D_jX^{-1/4}\rfloor\asymp X^{1/4}.
\]

In the accepted dyadic frequency partition choose an integer (r_j\ge1)
so that the full, nonterminal shell scale

\[
L_j=2^{-r_j}H_j
\]

lies in

\[
X^{1/6}\le L_j<2X^{1/6}.                            \tag{3.3}
\]

Such (r_j) exists because (H_j/X^{1/6}\asymp X^{1/12}\to\infty).
Moreover (L_j\to\infty), so the literal integer frequency shell is
nonempty and is neither the clipped (r=0) terminal shell nor the
bounded-frequency endpoint.  Floors change only constant factors.  Thus

\[
\delta_j=\frac12+O\!\left({1\over\log X}\right),
\qquad
\ell_j=\frac16+O\!\left({1\over\log X}\right),
\qquad
a_j=\frac13+O\!\left({1\over\log X}\right).         \tag{3.4}
\]

The strict residual inequalities have fixed slack at the limit:

\[
\frac16<\frac12-\frac14,
\qquad
178\cdot\frac16+1638\cdot\frac12
=\frac{2546}{3}>463.                                \tag{3.5}
\]

The labels are not ((1/2,0)).  Hence both literal families lie in
(\mathcal U_1) for all sufficiently large (X).  At their limiting
point,

\[
a={1-a\over2}=\frac13,qquad
{3\delta-1-\ell\over2}=\frac16,qquad
\delta=\frac12,                                    \tag{3.6}
\]

and TTY has the value (1.2).  By continuity, the minimum in (2.5) tends to
(1/3) on each family.  This reverses both inequalities in (3.2) at the
level of the formal limsup and proves (1.1).  Equivalently, with
(R=D/L\asymp X^{1/3}), the two elementary numerical rows are

\[
1+R\asymp X^{1/3},
\qquad
1+\sqrt{X/R}+D\sqrt{R/X}\asymp X^{1/3};            \tag{3.7}
\]

the last term in the second row is only (X^{1/6+o(1)}).

**Hard-cone normalization.**  Put (q=X/y^2).  On a hard shell
(h\asymp L), the proved one-sided transform has stationary part

\[
-{2e(1/8)X^{1/4}\over\pi}
\sum_{h\asymp L}{\Phi(h/(H+1))\over h^{3/4}}
\sum_{\substack{4h<n<16h\\n\ {\rm odd}}}
{\chi _4(n)W(\sqrt{4qh/n})\over n^{3/4}}
e(\sqrt{Xhn}),                                      \tag{3.8}
\]

up to the already target-safe boundary and transform errors and the exact
conjugate-frequency pairing.  If

\[
A_L(h,n)=
\Phi(h/(H+1))
\left({L\over h}\right)^{3/4}
\left({L\over n}\right)^{3/4}
W(\sqrt{4qh/n}),                                   \tag{3.9}
\]

then (3.8) is exactly a fixed constant times

\[
X^{1/4}L^{-3/2}\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L},
\quad
\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L}=
\sum_{h,n}\chi _4(n)A_L(h,n)e(\sqrt{Xhn}),         \tag{3.10}
\]

with all literal support restrictions, floors, and stars kept in the sum
and symbol.  There are (\asymp L^2) admissible lattice positions and the
normalized symbol has bounded size; this is the (L^2) coefficient-blind
ledger.  The physical quarter target is equivalent to

\[
\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L}\ll_\varepsilon
L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon.                              \tag{3.11}
\]

At (3.3), restoring the coefficient-blind capacity in (3.10) gives
(X^{1/4}L^{1/2}=X^{1/3+o(1)}), and the ratio between (1.3) is
(L^{1/2}=X^{1/12+o(1)}).  The factor is therefore neither lost nor
duplicated by the hard transform.

**Adjacent profiles and route implications.**  The exact denominator
partition gives an additive identity, but not a cancellation identity.
The adjacent hard and first-smooth blocks have different (w_D), heights
(H_0\asymp2H_1), and tapers
(\Phi(h/(H_j+1))).  Only the hard profile has the endpoint sample (d=y)

The accepted coupled-scale telescope obstruction also records that raw

the two stationary constants have the same sign, their Vaaler factors sit

It is neither a cancellation identity nor an identity between the hard M1
profile and the first smooth M1 profile.

The accepted compact-radial, endpoint-prefix, low-conductor, and

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first invalid step in a stronger interpretation of this result would be

\[
\text{best available row has capacity }X^{1/3+o(1)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|B_1(D,L;X)|\gg X^{1/3-o(1)}.                       \tag{4.1}
\]

Nothing in the menu gives (4.1).  The character and reciprocal phase could
produce cancellation not detected by any listed row.  Likewise, the
coefficient-blind (L^2) cone ledger is not a signed lower bound for
(\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L}).

After that semantic guardrail, the first genuinely unproved analytic steps
are independent and exactly the two graph blockers:

\[
\mathcal T_{{\rm M1},L}\ll_\varepsilon L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon
\quad\text{on every residual hard shell},           \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
B_1(D,L;X)\ll_\varepsilon X^{1/4+\varepsilon}
\quad\text{on every literal smooth label in }\mathcal U_1.   \tag{4.3}
\]

Neither has priority over the other, and proving one leaves the other
open.  Any proposed adjacent-profile shortcut must first provide an exact
coefficientwise identity preserving both scale-dependent heights, tapers,
profiles, endpoint stars, support crossings, signs, and real-(X)
conventions; it must then prove a capacity gain for the combined object.
No such connector is present.  A canonical or GAR estimate cannot fill
this gap without a new inverse-localization theorem, and a global circle
bound cannot fill it without an isolation inequality.

The TTY source fit used here is inherited from its already accepted source
node and fixed-profile transfer.  No new primary-source extension to an
arbitrary coefficient, canonical Gram, or global aggregate is asserted.
