## 3. Proof and applicability derivation

### 3.1 Why the pointwise conclusion is correctly normalized

Li--Yang define the area variable by \(m^2+n^2\le X\), so their exponent is directly \(\theta_*\) in \(X\). Huxley's published \(131/208\) is in maximum radius of curvature \(R\); replacing \(R\) by \(\sqrt X\) gives \(131/416\). Gao also defines the area variable through \(\sum_{n\le x}r(n)=\pi x+\cdots\), so the \(131/208\) sentence in Gao's introduction cannot be transferred literally. Gao's theorem inserts the independent restriction \(P(n)\le y\), and removing that restriction is not a limiting case covered uniformly by its hypotheses. Therefore Gao neither supersedes nor contradicts Li--Yang.

The primary search found later work on smooth numbers, averages, variants, and lower-bound/extreme-value questions, but no later indexed theorem with the quantifier “for every real \(X\)” for the unrestricted classical \(R(X)\). This establishes the dated source-index conclusion, not an assertion about unindexed manuscripts.

### 3.2 Blomer--Pascadi versus the Round-169 product collar

There is no legal parameter map at the first step. Equations (170.BP1)--(170.BP4) start with an already formed

\[
S_{\rm src}(am,n;c)=\sum_{x\bmod c}^*e((amx+n\overline x)/c)
\]

at one fixed integral modulus \(c\), a unit \(a\), two interval variables, and factorized coefficients. The collar (170.L2) is an equality/inequality involving products \(k\ell\) near the moving real centre \(XQR\). It supplies neither a modular inverse, a modulus, nor a congruence. Thus the first failed hypothesis is **the absence of an exact Kloosterman transform**, before any exponent comparison.

Even conditionally granting a separate transform for each \((Q,R)\), the resulting modulus, coefficient support, and test weight would depend on \((Q,R)\). Applying a fixed-modulus theorem blockwise places absolute values before

\[
\sum_{Q,R}{g(Q,R)\over QR}(\cdots),
\]

destroying the only possible cross-block cancellation. Theorem 1.6 cannot restore it: (170.BP5) is a positive square over exceptional Maaß forms at one level/cusp, not a full Kuznetsov identity or a signed varying-weight bound. Hard cardinal cells, the real centre, common-prime and 2-adic branches, holomorphic/regular Maaß/Eisenstein pieces, zero frequencies, main terms, and endpoints remain unpriced.

Hence the new \(c^{-1/32}\) critical saving never reaches the collar. By the floor-aware identity (170.L3), the capacity-to-target factor is \(H/L+O(L^{-1})\), and the required relative saving is \(L/H\), exactly as in Round 169.

### 3.3 Exact delta-mass test against the UNBAL matrix

Fix one pair \((g,n)\) and temporarily assume \((N_0,n)=1\). In Blomer--Pascadi set

\[
c=n,\qquad a=N_0\pmod n,\qquad
\mathcal I=\{1\},\quad M=1,\qquad
\mathcal J=\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad N=n,
\qquad \alpha_1=1,\quad \beta_h=\widehat\gamma_{g,n}(h),
\tag{170.U1}
\]

and extend \(\beta_n=0\). The source and project conventions agree only after the explicit inversion/symmetry step

\[
S_{\rm proj}(N_0,h;n)
=S_{\rm src}(N_0,h;n)
=S_{\rm src}(h,N_0;n),
\tag{170.U1a}
\]

because \(x\mapsto\overline x\) is a bijection on the reduced residue classes. Thus (170.U1) produces \(S_{\rm src}(N_0,h;n)\), and (170.U1a) identifies it with the project row. Moreover, the source joint condition \((m,h,n)=1\) becomes \((1,h,n)=1\), so it is automatic for **every** \(h\); the initial-interval gcd-removal clause is not needed. Theorem 5.7's condition \((m,c)=1\) is automatic for the same delta row. Therefore the delta-mass coefficient and arbitrary \(\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\) are legal at one fixed modulus on the unit-center subfamily. The first literal source hypothesis that fails for the full project family is \(a\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\): the project allows arbitrary \((N_0,n)\), as is also visible in its switched-cusp identity. A gcd decomposition would be a new reduction, not Theorem 5.5 as stated.

On the coprime-center subfamily, substitute \((M,N,c)=(1,n,n)\) into (170.BP3). The five contributions are respectively

\[
n^{-1/32+o(1)},\qquad 1,
\qquad n^{2/15+o(1)},\qquad n^{1/9+o(1)},\qquad 1.
\]

Therefore

\[
H(1,n,n)\asymp n^{2/15+o(1)},
\qquad
\left|\sum_{h<n}\widehat\gamma_{g,n}(h)S_{\rm proj}(N_0,h;n)\right|
\ll n^{17/15+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2.
\tag{170.U2}
\]

Swapping the two Kloosterman arguments and using \(H(n,1,n)\) gives the same obstructing \(n^{2/15}\) and constant terms. Theorem 1.1 with its common length parameter forced to \(N=n\) is worse:

\[
n^{1+o(1)}(n^{1/32}+n^{1/8}+n^{5/18})
=n^{23/18+o(1)}.
\tag{170.U3}
\]

The authors' dedicated unbalanced Theorem 5.7 is the best of these direct delta specializations. At \((M,N,c)=(1,n,n)\), its bracket is

\[
n^{-1/4}+1+n^{-1/4}\asymp1,
\]

and hence

\[
\left|\sum_{h<n}\widehat\gamma_{g,n}(h)S_{\rm proj}(N_0,h;n)\right|
\ll n^{1+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2.
\tag{170.U4}
\]

There is **no power saving** in (170.U4), so it cannot supply the required \(X^{\mu(a)}\) boundary gain.

Nor can Blomer--Pascadi Theorem 1.1 be rescued by chopping the \(h\)-range into \(n^{1/2}\) translated blocks of length \(\sqrt n\), which that theorem does permit. No frequency deletion is involved because the joint gcd is automatic at \(m=1\). Each block costs \(n^{1-1/32+o(1)}\|\beta_B\|_2\); triangle plus Cauchy over the \(n^{1/2}\) blocks gives

\[
n^{1-1/32+1/4+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2
=n^{39/32+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2,
\tag{170.U5}
\]

again worse than (170.U4).

Finally, (170.U1) works only **one modulus at a time**. The project vector changes with the modulus:

\[
\beta_h^{(n,g)}=\widehat\gamma_{g,n}(h),
\]

and the required object keeps the outer \(\chi_4(g)\chi_4(n)\) sum, moving \(g,n,j\) support, short phase, and hard weights before every positive norm. Summing (170.U4) over \(g,n\) by triangle erases those signs. Blomer--Pascadi has no modulus-average theorem here. Pascadi's Corollary 1.4 does average moduli, but it puts \(|\cdot|\) at each modulus, requires two fixed sequences of square-root length, and therefore does not accept \(\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\).

Theorem 1.6 is likewise not a substitute. The switched-cusp identity

\[
S_{\infty0}^{\chi_4}(4N_0,h;2n)=\chi_4(n)S_{\rm proj}(N_0,h;n)
\]

puts the moduli \(2n\) into a Kloosterman family, but a Kuznetsov application would need one coefficient vector in \(h\) independent of the modulus and its complete holomorphic, regular/exceptional Maaß, Eisenstein, and diagonal/main ledger. Here the vector itself is \(\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\), the weight moves with \(n,g\), and (170.BP5) treats only a positive exceptional piece for a fixed level/cusp setup. Thus the first family-level failure is the absence of a vector-valued varying-modulus theorem; positivity and omitted spectra fail next.

### 3.4 Pascadi v2 against the same two interfaces

For the product collar, Pascadi v2 fails at the same pre-exponent point: no exact fixed-modulus Kloosterman form has been derived, and Corollary 1.4 would move the absolute value inside the outer modulus sum even if one were.

For UNBAL, the full \(h<n\) length violates \(M,N\ll n^{1/2+o(1)}\). Unlike Blomer--Pascadi Theorem 1.1, Pascadi's (170.P1) is stated only for initial ranges, not arbitrary translated intervals. On the unit-center subfamily it licenses the first initial range \(1\le h\le\sqrt n\); its joint gcd condition is automatic for the delta row \(m=1\). It does **not** license chopping and recombining the complete row.

For scale diagnosis only, if one hypothetically added a translated-interval extension not present in the source, each \(\sqrt n\)-block would cost

\[
n^{1-1/700+o(1)}\|\beta_B\|_2.
\]

Formal triangle plus Cauchy over \(n^{1/2}\) blocks would then cost

\[
n^{5/4-1/700+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2,
\tag{170.U6\text{-hyp}}
\]

which is worse than (170.U4), but (170.U6-hyp) is **not a theorem-backed full-row estimate**. Only the stronger \(1/276\) alternative (170.P2) imposes \((h,n)=1\), so it does not recover the complete row. Corollary 1.6 is only a positive exceptional-spectrum square at one factorizable level and length at most square root. Thus v2 supplies no improvement to (170.U4) and no preservation of the outer signed family.
