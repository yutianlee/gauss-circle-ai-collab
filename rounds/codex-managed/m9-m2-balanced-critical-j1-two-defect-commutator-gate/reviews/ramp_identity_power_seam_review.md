## 1. Result

**Verdict: REPAIR.**

The central ramp calculation in
`reports/two_defect_capacity_boundary_hostile_audit.md` is correct.  With
the report's conventions, both signs and both shifts in (171.H4) are exact;
the fixed-\(p\) character identity is exact; the nonaxial coordinate inverse has
multiplicity one; the two axial sectors are absolutely target-safe; and the
sharp-gate face count is correctly at target scale before multiplication by
the \(q\)-ramp.  The lower bound for a coefficientwise \(q\)-primitive is
also exact and shows that the ramp cannot be replaced by uniformly bounded
coefficients on a length-\(L\) run.

One material algebraic seam is missing.  Pure endpoint exchanges commute,
and the bare translations \(T_p,T_q\) commute, but natural
**multiplication/shift commutators do not commute**.  In particular, for
\[
 U=q(2h+p)=\Delta+\rho,\qquad
 V=p(2k+q)=\Delta-\rho,
\]
the commutators of \(M_U,M_V\) with \(T_p,T_q\) are nonzero.  They do not
rescue the estimate: after exact normalization they reduce to the same
Weyl shift identities and expose a coordinate multiplier of size
\(\Theta(L)\), or an axial/diagonal singular divisor.  Nevertheless, they
must be displayed before concluding that the first identity gate is a zero
commutator.  Without this repair, the phrase
"coordinate-generated first-difference route" is broader than the proved
no-go.

After the repair, the lawful conclusion remains a scoped method no-go:
the pure-swap/pure-translation commutator and their coefficient-uniform
positive-variation closures do not provide the missing factor \(L\).  No
bound for the physical remainder, and no no-go for a new signed nonlocal
actual-symbol theorem, follows.

## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses audited

Fix the report's persistent critical \(j=1\) literal block, with
\(h,h',k,k'\asymp L\), \(h,h'\) odd, and all weights zero extended off the
joint positive literal support.  Put
\[
 p=h'-h\in2\mathbb Z,\quad q=k'-k\in\mathbb Z,
\]
\[
 \Delta=h'k'-hk=hq+kp+pq,\qquad
 \rho=hk'-h'k=hq-kp.
\]
Then
\[
 U:=\Delta+\rho=qH,\qquad H:=2h+p=h+h',
\]
\[
 V:=\Delta-\rho=pK,\qquad K:=2k+q=k+k'.
\tag{171.R1}
\]
On literal support \(H,K\asymp L\) and are positive.  Define
\[
 (T_pF)(p,q)=F(p-2,q),\qquad
 (T_qF)(p,q)=F(p,q-1),
\]
and \(\nabla_p=I-T_p\), \(\nabla_q=I-T_q\).  Let
\(M_g\) denote multiplication by \(g(p,q)\), and use
\([A,B]=AB-BA\).

The reviewed claim that is green is
\[
 \sum_{p,q}\epsilon_pF(p,q)
 ={1\over2}\sum_{p,q}\epsilon_p\nabla_pF(p,q)
 =-{1\over2}\sum_{p,q}\epsilon_p(q-q_0)
       \nabla_p\nabla_qF(p,q),
\tag{171.R2}
\]
where \(\epsilon_p=(-1)^{p/2}\), all sums are finite, and \(q_0\) is any
real number.  The repaired commutator claim must say only that the two
endpoint exchanges and the two bare differences commute.  It must not say
that every multiplication/shift operator generated by (171.R1) has zero
commutator.

For rigor, the zero-extension convention should be stated piecewise:
\(F_{h,k}(p,q)=0\) unless both \((h,k)\) and
\((h+p,k+q)\) belong to the positive literal support; the square-root phase
is evaluated only in the latter case.  Multiplying an undefined square root
by a zero weight is not itself a definition.

## 3. Proof and equation-by-equation audit

### 3.1 Signs and indices in (171.H4)

Because \(\epsilon_{p+2}=-\epsilon_p\), finite reindexing gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_p\epsilon_p\nabla_pF(p,q)
 &=\sum_p\epsilon_pF(p,q)
   -\sum_p\epsilon_pF(p-2,q)\\
 &=\sum_p\epsilon_pF(p,q)
   -\sum_r\epsilon_{r+2}F(r,q)\\
 &=2\sum_p\epsilon_pF(p,q).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{171.R3}
\]
For every finitely supported \(H\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_q(q-q_0)\nabla_qH(q)
 &=\sum_q(q-q_0)H(q)
   -\sum_r(r+1-q_0)H(r)\\
 &=-\sum_qH(q).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{171.R4}
\]
Since \(T_pT_q=T_qT_p\), (171.R3)--(171.R4) give exactly
(171.R2), including the minus sign and the factor \(1/2\).  The four-corner
expansion
\[
 F(p,q)-F(p-2,q)-F(p,q-1)+F(p-2,q-1)
\]
is therefore correct.

The character calculation is also exact.  If \(p=2s\) and \(h\) is odd,
then
\[
 \chi_4(h)\chi_4(h+p)
 =(-1)^{(h-1)/2+(h+2s-1)/2}=(-1)^s=\epsilon_p.
\tag{171.R5}
\]
It is constant on a fixed-\(p\) fibre and supplies (171.R3), not an
\(L\)-saving.

### 3.2 Coordinate inverse, divisibility, and multiplicity

The ordered-pair map
\[
 (h,k,h',k')\longleftrightarrow(h,k,p=h'-h,q=k'-k)
\]
is a bijection of multiplicity one.  Off the axes, with
\(u=\Delta+\rho\) and \(v=\Delta-\rho\),
\[
 h={1\over2}\left({u\over q}-p\right),\qquad
 k={1\over2}\left({v\over p}-q\right)
\tag{171.R6}
\]
is correct.  Thus \(q\mid u\), \(p\mid v\),
\(u/q\equiv p\pmod2\), and \(v/p\equiv q\pmod2\) are the integer
conditions.  For an **exact character-support chart**, the report should
also state explicitly
\[
 p\in2\mathbb Z,\qquad {u\over q}-p\equiv2\pmod4,
\tag{171.R7}
\]
the latter being equivalent to reconstructed \(h\) (and hence \(h'\))
being odd.  This condition is currently recoverable from (171.H5)'s
literal support test, but (171.H7) alone is not the complete parity ledger.
If \((u,v)\) are treated as independent coordinates rather than defined
from integer \((\Delta,\rho)\), one must additionally retain
\(u\equiv v\pmod2\).

For fixed nonaxial \((\Delta,\rho)\), \(q\) is a signed divisor of \(u\)
and \(p\) a signed divisor of \(v\), so the number of representations is
\(O(\tau(|u|)\tau(|v|))\ll X^\varepsilon\), after the parity and support
tests.  This is an upper multiplicity, not a saving after summing the
\(O(L^4)\) defect range.

### 3.3 Axes and sharp faces

For \(p=0\), \(\Delta=\rho=hq\); for \(q=0\),
\(\Delta=pk\) and \(\rho=-pk\).  Both can be strictly double-far.
Each sector has three variables of length \(O(L)\), bounded literal weights,
and \(|e(\cdot)-1|\le2\), hence is
\(O(L^3X^\varepsilon)\) absolutely.  Their intersection is excluded by
the far gates.  Equation (171.H9) is therefore correct.

The increments in (171.H18) are also exact:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \Delta(p+2,q)-\Delta(p,q)=2(k+q)=2k',&
 \rho(p+2,q)-\rho(p,q)=-2k,\\
 \Delta(p,q+1)-\Delta(p,q)=h+p=h',&
 \rho(p,q+1)-\rho(p,q)=h.
 \end{array}
\tag{171.R8}
\]
They are \(\asymp L\).  If a sharp gate changes across one step, one of
the old or new defects lies in an \(O(L)\)-width collar about \(\pm L\).
The accepted corridor estimate
\(O(L^2(U+1)X^\varepsilon)\), at \(U\asymp L\), gives
\(O(L^3X^\varepsilon)\) before the ramp.  A literal one-coordinate support
face also has at most three free length-\(L\) variables.  Multiplication by
\(|q-q_0|\asymp L\) can therefore restore \(L^4X^\varepsilon\) capacity.
This is an upper-capacity obstruction, not a lower bound for the actual
symbol.

### 3.4 The primitive lower bound

If
\[
 \sum_{q\in I}f(q)=\sum_qc(q)(f(q)-f(q-1))
\]
for every sequence supported on an interval \(I=\{a,\ldots,b\}\) of
length \(N\), comparison of the coefficient of \(f(r)\) gives
\(c(r)-c(r+1)=1\) for every \(r\in I\).  Hence
\(c(a)-c(b+1)=N\), and the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \max_q|c(q)|\ge N/2.
\tag{171.R9}
\]
This verifies (171.H17), including the constant.  Removing \(q=0\) splits
the run and creates new endpoints; it does not furnish a bounded primitive
on any resulting component that still has length \(\asymp L\).

### 3.5 The missed nonzero multiplication/shift commutators

For every multiplier \(g\),
\[
 [T_p,M_g]=M_{T_pg-g}T_p,\qquad
 [\nabla_p,M_g]=M_{g-T_pg}T_p,
\]
and analogously in \(q\).  Applying this to (171.R1) gives the exact table
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &M_U&M_V\\ \hline
 [\nabla_p,\,\cdot]&2qT_p&2K T_p\\
 [\nabla_q,\,\cdot]&H T_q&pT_q.
 \end{array}
\tag{171.R10}
\]
All four entries are nonzero in general.  The corresponding shift
commutators have the opposite signs.  Thus the zero result for
\([\nabla_p,\nabla_q]\) and for the endpoint exchanges is not a
classification of all operators generated by \(U,V,T_p,T_q\).

These commutators are nevertheless Weyl tautologies, not a hidden saving.
Indeed,
\[
 H^{-1}[\nabla_q,M_U]=[\nabla_q,M_q]=T_q,
 \qquad
 (2K)^{-1}[\nabla_p,M_V]={1\over2}[\nabla_p,M_p]=T_p.
\tag{171.R11}
\]
On summing the first identity, the total difference
\(\sum_q\nabla_q(qF)\) vanishes, leaving exactly
\(\sum_qF=-\sum_qq\nabla_qF\), namely the ramp identity (171.H14).
The second identity introduces a multiplier \(p=O(L)\); with the actual
alternating character it is weaker than the bounded identity (171.R3).
The alternative inversions
\[
 (2q)^{-1}[\nabla_p,M_U]=T_p,\qquad
 p^{-1}[\nabla_q,M_V]=T_q
\tag{171.R12}
\]
are singular on the already separated \(q=0\) and \(p=0\) axes and, after
expansion, leave the size-\(L\) multipliers \(H\) and \(K\).

There is also a genuinely two-direction nonzero commutator.  If
\(A=M_U\nabla_p\) and \(B=M_V\nabla_q\), direct expansion gives
\[
 [A,B]
 =HK\bigl(2qT_p\nabla_q-pT_q\nabla_p\bigr).
\tag{171.R13}
\]
Normalizing by the harmless positive factor \(HK\asymp L^2\) restores
the ramp coefficients \(p,q=O(L)\); it does not yield an inverse power of
\(L\).

For the ordered multiplication-shift pair
\(A_0=M_UT_p\), \(B_0=M_VT_q\), one instead obtains
\[
 [A_0,B_0]
 =HK(p-2q)T_pT_q.
\tag{171.R14}
\]
This is nonzero away from \(p=2q\).  Inverting it reconstructs a shift and
is singular on that diagonal.  More explicitly, after division by
\(HK(p-2q)\), the two ordered terms carry the coefficients
\[
 {q(p-2)\over p-2q},\qquad {p(q-1)\over p-2q}.
\tag{171.R15}
\]
They are generically of size at least \(L\) when \(|p|,|q|\asymp L\) and
\(|p-2q|\asymp L\), and can be of size \(L^2\) on the first nonzero
parallel lattice lines.  The exact line \(p=2q\) itself has only three free
variables and is absolutely \(O(L^3X^\varepsilon)\), but taking absolute
values after (171.R15) restores at least the missing factor \(L\) on its
nearby complement.  Keeping the two large terms signed merely reconstructs
the original shift tautologically.  Therefore these missed nonzero
commutators do not validate the target, but their existence requires the
scope repair in Section 1.

### 3.6 Fixed-\(Q\) scope

The formulas in (171.H20) agree with the accepted Round-136 alias chart.
With
\[
 c={\lambda\over R}\sqrt{h/k},\quad A=k'/k,
 \quad Q={\mu^2h\over d^2k}=Xc^2,
\]
and real stationary centre \(h'=Xk'/\lambda^2=hA/c^2\), direct
substitution gives
\[
 \rho=hk'{c^2-1\over c^2},\qquad
 \Delta=hk{A^2-c^2\over c^2}.
\tag{171.R16}
\]
For \(A=1\), \(\Delta=-\rho\), and
\(|c^2-1|\asymp C/L\) with a sufficiently large fixed \(C\) places the
real stationary ruling beyond both collars.  This verifies that phase-free
subtraction and the two gates do not delete the accepted fixed-\(Q\)
obstruction.  It must remain explicitly scoped to the transformed alias
bulk: a \(p\)- or \(q\)-difference need not preserve \(Q\), and the ruling
does not by itself prove failure of every signed commutator estimate.  It
shows that the existing positive broad--narrow theorem cannot be used as
the missing complement estimate.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first required repair is the transition from the correct statements
\[
 [\mathscr D_h,\mathscr D_k]=0,\qquad
 [\nabla_p,\nabla_q]=0
\]
to the broader narrative that the coordinate-generated commutator gate
fails because the commutator is zero.  Equations (171.R10), (171.R13), and
(171.R14) are exact counterexamples to an unrestricted zero-commutator
reading.

They do not overturn the capacity no-go.  Their normalized forms either
are exactly the \(q\)-ramp already proved in (171.H14), introduce a size-
\(L\) multiplier, divide by an axial coordinate, or divide by the new
singular factor \(p-2q\).  What remains unproved is any signed estimate
which keeps the cancellation between the large ordered terms and returns
an \(L^{-1}\) gain for the complete actual symbol.  Consequently the report
may reject the pure-swap, pure-translation, and positive normalized
multiplication/shift placements, but it may not claim a classification of
all coordinate commutators or a no-go for such an additional signed
theorem.

A secondary exactness repair is to add the mod-4 odd-character condition
(171.R7), rather than call (171.H7) alone the complete parity ledger, and to
define the square-root summand piecewise under zero extension.
