**Result: `source_level_no_go` for a direct transfer to the Round-131 determinant correlation, together with an exact repaired source card.** Li--Yang's literal standard sum, its two conditions on (F), the Bombieri--Iwaniec parameter package, the displayed double-large-sieve consequence, and both spacing problems can be recorded exactly. The single-wave phase identity

\[
 \frac{hT}{M}F(m/M)=\frac{ca}{\kappa_i b}
 \quad\text{for}\quad
 (h,m,H,M,T,F)=(a,b,L_{\rm proj},D,c/\kappa_i,z^{-1})

is exact on a positive dyadic sign stratum. It is not a map of the joint scalar: Li--Yang's (S) has separable bounded-variation weights in ((h,m)), whereas the project coefficient of ((a,b),(a',b')) is joint through (n=ab'-a'b>0), the determinant taper, and the physical owners. \[
S=\sum_{H\le h\le2H}g(h/H)
  \sum_{M\le m\le2M}G(m/M)

\tag{LY 4.1}

where (T,M) are large positive parameters, (H\ge1), (F\in C^3[1,2]) is real, and (g,G) have bounded variation on ([1,2]). Fixed real constants (C_1,\dots,C_5\ge2) are used. On all (z\in[1,2]), the two separate conditions are

\tag{LY 4.3}

These are local TeX lines 763--780. No joint ((h,m;h',m')) weight occurs in the definition.

### Bombieri--Iwaniec parameters and arc ranges

Here (N) is the length of a short interval in the original (m)-sum. It is not a determinant. Li--Yang define

\[
R\sim\left(\frac{M^3}{NT}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
L_{\rm sp}\sim\frac{HQ}{R^2}\ge1,\qquad
K_{\rm sp}\sim\frac{NQ}{R^2}\ge1,
\]

\[
\eta\sim\left(\frac QR\right)^2(K_{\rm sp}L_{\rm sp})^{-1}
      \sim\frac{R^2}{NH},
\tag{LY 4.12}
\]

with

\[
R\le Q\le3H\le\frac{3}{64C_2}N,
\qquad
L_{\rm sp}\le K_{\rm sp}\le\eta^{-1}\le K_{\rm sp}L_{\rm sp}.
\tag{LY 4.13--4.14}
\]

These are lines 873--889. In the quoted Bourgain--Watt consequence, the actual maximum is over (R\le Q\lesssim Q_2), where

\[
Q_2=R(H/R)^{39/119}\bigl(\log(2H/R)\bigr)^{-3/4}.
\tag{LY Lemma 4.3}
\]

The source stresses that (R\le H), hence (L_{\rm sp}\ge1) and (\eta K_{\rm sp}\le1), cannot be inferred formally in every case; the exceptional one-term regime is delegated to Huxley's elementary treatment (lines 1013--1025).

The original (m)-range is cut into intervals (I_j) of length (N). A Dirichlet approximant (a_{\rm BI}/r_{\rm BI}) approximates the linear Taylor coefficient and labels (I_{a/r}). After grouping, major arcs are treated separately; minor denominators are reduced to (R\le r\le H) and then dyadically localized to (r\sim Q), (a\sim A). See lines 891--921.

### Second spacing: all four coordinates

For a reduced derivative approximant (a/r), let (m_{a/r}) be the nearest integer to the inverse-image of (a/r) under the function (m\mapsto (T/M^2)F'(m/M)). Define

\[
\vec x_{a/r}=\left(\frac{\bar a}{r},\frac{\bar a c_{\rm BI}}r,
\frac1{\sqrt{\mu r^3}},\frac{\kappa_{\rm BI}}{\sqrt{\mu r^3}}\right),
\tag{LY 4.20}
\]

where

\[
a\bar a\equiv1\pmod r,
\quad \mu=\frac12\frac{T}{M^3}F''(m_{a/r}/M),
\quad \nu=\frac{(T/M^2)F'(m_{a/r}/M)-a/r}{2\mu},\ |\nu|\le1,
\]

\[
c_{\rm BI}=\left\lfloor r\frac TMF(m_{a/r}/M)-\mu\nu^2\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\kappa_{\rm BI}=\left\{r\frac TMF(m_{a/r}/M)-\mu\nu^2\right\}\in[0,1).
\]

For (a,a_1\sim A), (r,r_1\sim Q), the second spacing problem counts pairs satisfying

\[
\left\|\frac{\bar a}{r}-\frac{\bar a_1}{r_1}\right\|\lesssim(K_{\rm sp}L_{\rm sp})^{-1},
\qquad
\left\|\frac{\bar a c_{\rm BI}}r-\frac{\bar a_1c_{{\rm BI},1}}{r_1}\right\|\lesssim L_{\rm sp}^{-1},
\]

\[
\left|\frac1{\sqrt{\mu r^3}}-\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_1r_1^3}}\right|
\lesssim(L_{\rm sp}\sqrt{K_{\rm sp}})^{-1},
\qquad
\left|\frac{\kappa_{\rm BI}}{\sqrt{\mu r^3}}-
\frac{\kappa_{{\rm BI},1}}{\sqrt{\mu_1r_1^3}}\right|
\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{K_{\rm sp}}}{L_{\rm sp}}.
\tag{LY second spacing}
\]

Li--Yang then replace these by four schematic tolerances (\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4), including

\[
\left\|\bar a/r-\bar a_1/r_1\right\|\le\Delta_1,
\quad
\left\|\bar a c_{\rm BI}/r-\bar a_1c_{{\rm BI},1}/r_1\right\|\le\Delta_2,
\]

\[
|\mu_1r_1^3/(\mu r^3)-1|\le\Delta_3,
\quad |\kappa_{\rm BI}-\kappa_{{\rm BI},1}|\le\Delta_4,
\]

but explicitly do not specify the (\Delta_j) in this paper. These formulas are local lines 1029--1064. The subsequent (SL_2(\mathbb Z)) matrix relation and short-real-interval argument are only sketched (lines 1064--1096).

## 3. Proof or derivation

### Algebraic audit of (4.6), (4.8), printed (4.9), and (5.23)

Put (\lambda=969/14000). For the Case-A value in (4.8),

\[
N_A\asymp H^{-16/25}M^{41/25}T^{-49/100}(\log T)^\lambda.
\]

Direct division, with no numerical approximation, gives

\[
\frac{N_A^{6-q}}{H^{2q-6}(M^3/T)^{4-q}}
\asymp
\left(\frac MH\right)^{(34q-54)/25}
T^{(106-51q)/100}(\log T)^{\lambda(6-q)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since (4\le q\le9/2<6), the equivalent root form of Lemma 4.1's condition is

\[
\left(\frac HM\right)^{(34q-54)/(25(6-q))}
\ll T^{(106-51q)/(100(6-q))}(\log T)^\lambda.
\tag{3.2}
\]

By contrast, the source prints at local lines 829--832

\[
H^{(2q-6)/(6-q)+16/25}M^{34/25}
\ll T^{51/100}(\log T)^\lambda.
\tag{printed LY 4.9}
\]

Equations (3.2) and printed (4.9) have different (H,M,T,q) exponents and are not algebraically equivalent. A sharp symbolic control is the campaign scale (M=T^{1/2}), (H=T^{1/6}). For every (q\ge4), the (T)-power on the left of printed (4.9) is at least

\[
\frac16\left(1+\frac{16}{25}\right)+\frac12\frac{34}{25}
=\frac{143}{150}>\frac{51}{100},
\]

so printed (4.9) fails by a fixed power. Yet (3.1) below holds by a fixed power on the same scale. This proves non-equivalence, rather than merely suggesting a typographical difference.

Now use the accepted correction to (5.18), namely (H=MT^x), not the literal (H=MT^{-x}). The main (T)-exponent in (3.1) is

\[
E(x,q)=-\frac{34q-54}{25}x+\frac{106-51q}{100}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

On the final range, (164x+49<0), (14x-1<0), and (q>4). Cross-multiplying Li--Yang's (5.23) with the required reversal gives

\[
\frac{(14x-1)/50}{(164x+49)/100}<\frac{q-2}{q-4}
\iff
2(14x-1)(q-4)>(164x+49)(q-2)
\]

\[
\iff -136qx+216x+106-51q>0
\iff E(x,q)>0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus (5.23) is exactly the strict positivity test for the power of (T) in the *actual* (4.6)+(4.8) ratio. Because the logarithmic factor in (3.1) is favorable, it is sufficient for (4.6). It is not a full logical equivalence at a zero-power boundary, and it is not equivalent to printed (4.9). At (x=-3/8,q=4), where (5.23) has a (q-4) denominator, (3.3) can be checked directly and is positive.

### Why the source does not imply the determinant estimate

The source transformation begins with one separably weighted (S), cuts its (m)-sum into short intervals, introduces derivative approximants, and bounds the minor contribution by

\[
\sum_{I_{a/r}\in\mathcal C(A,Q)}|\text{first-spacing inner sum}|.
\]

Hölder and the double large sieve then produce (G_q) and a positive pair count (B(A,Q;V)). This direction can prove an upper bound for the source (S); it does not supply an inverse representation of an arbitrary signed pair form. The project scalar has a fixed physical centre in both phases and a coefficient (B_i((a,b),(a',b'))) that retains (n>0), (1-Wn/(\kappa_i bb')), stars, cells, threshold strata, Möbius/Stieltjes profiles, and quarter or (\chi_4) carriers. None is present in the source hypotheses. Separating it into two (S)'s would discard precisely the joint owner, while taking an absolute-value or spacing energy changes the requested directionality. This is the first exact project-to-source normal-form mismatch.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

Round 95 repairs only the final circle range by proving (H/R\ge(N/H)^{(q-4)/4}); it does not certify Proposition 3.1 in full generality.

\[
H>M^{-27/23}T^{53/92}(\log T)^{2907/12880},

not merely the printed power inequality. Round 95 proves a fixed-power margin only on the final application range. Thus general Theorem 4.2 remains over-stated.
