## 3. Proof or derivation

### Direct \(N\)-condition and full small-cap applicability

Inserting

\[
N_A=H^{-16/25}M^{41/25}T^{-49/100}(\log T)^\lambda
\]

into the original Lemma-4.1 condition gives

\[
\frac{N_A^{6-q}}
{H^{2q-6}(M^3/T)^{4-q}}
\asymp
\left(\frac MH\right)^{(34q-54)/25}
T^{(106-51q)/100}(\log T)^{\lambda(6-q)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At \(H/M=T^{-1/3}\), its \(T\)-power is

\[
\frac{17(6-q)}{300}>0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the actual source gate has fixed-power slack. Printed Li--Yang (4.9) fails, as the source report, conductor candidate, and blind report independently show.

At the same scale,

\[
\frac{L_{\rm BI}}{K_{\rm BI}}=\frac HN,
\qquad
\frac{\eta}{(H/N)^2}
=\frac{M^3}{H^3T}\asymp1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since \(H/N<1\) and

\[
\frac{q-2}{q-4}>2\qquad(q<6),
\]

the printed first-spacing inequality

\[
\left(\frac{L_{\rm BI}}{K_{\rm BI}}\right)^{(q-2)/(q-4)}
\le\eta
\]

holds with fixed-power slack. The omitted Guth--Maldague condition is equivalently

\[
\frac HR\ge\left(\frac NH\right)^{(q-4)/4}.
\]

The left and right \(Y\)-powers are \(17/600\) and
\(17(q-4)/1200\), leaving

\[
\frac{17(6-q)}{1200}>0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The logarithmic margin is likewise favorable:
\(\lambda/2-\lambda(q-4)/4=\lambda(6-q)/4>0\).
Therefore the optimized \(q\) passes the complete repaired small-cap
range, not merely the printed Case-A clauses.

### Moving-wedge powers and their precise scope

For fixed outer \(r=(a,b)\),

\[
\frac ab-\frac{a'}{b'}=\frac{n}{bb'},\qquad
0<n\lesssim\frac{bb'}W.
\]

More precisely than conductor (133.W2),

\[
a'=\frac ab\,b'-\frac nb,
\qquad
0<\frac nb\lesssim\frac DW.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The interval is one-sided below its moving centre; the symmetric
\(O(D/W)\) notation preserves its length but not its orientation.
The available numerator length is

\[
H_0=D/W=Y^{3/48}.
\]

The centre has slope \(a/b\asymp L/D\). Requiring its drift on a
denominator cell of length \(M_0\) to be \(O(H_0)\) gives

\[
\frac LD M_0\lesssim H_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_0\lesssim\frac{D^2}{WL}=Y^{19/48}.
\]

Consequently a full denominator shell needs at least the crude cell count

\[
\frac D{M_0}\gtrsim\frac{WL}{D}=Y^{5/48}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus all powers (133.W3)--(133.W5) pass.

The sentence in the moving-wedge artifact that a source rectangle must
have interval length comparable to its scale is too strong. A bounded-
variation cutoff \(g(h/H)\) or \(G(m/M)\) can select a much shorter
subinterval of \([H,2H]\) or \([M,2M]\) with \(O(1)\) variation. The real
issues are:

1. the wedge boundary moves with \(b'\) and is not a product cutoff;
2. rebasing \(a'=a_0+u\) produces the high-frequency reciprocal factor
   \(e(-ca_0/(\kappa_i b'))\), which is not a fixed-BV amplitude;
3. retaining the absolute source scales \(H=L,M=D\) gives the source
   bound no automatic \(H_0\) or \(M_0\) saving;
4. an exact rectangle/projective decomposition, including the taper and
   all owners, has not been proved.

Even the maximally optimistic calculation is insufficient. Suppose, only
for capacity testing, that every one of the \(Y^{5/48}\) cells were one
legal source \(S\) at the full scales \(H=L,M=D\), with no further loss.
Then summing the one-wave estimates costs

\[
Y^{5/48}Y^{1/6+\Phi(-1/3)+\varepsilon}
=Y^{13/48+\Phi(-1/3)+\varepsilon}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

This already exceeds \(Y^{27/48}\), because

\[
\left(\frac{13}{48}+\Phi(-1/3)\right)-\frac{27}{48}
=\Phi(-1/3)-\frac7{24}
=\frac{10\sqrt{170}-117}{600}>0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The inequality is exact since \(17000>117^2\). This calculation still
omits the outer-ray summation and owner norms, so it is diagnostic only
and is more favorable than any currently justified rowwise connector.

If instead one formally sets the source height to \(H_0\), then

\[
H_0/M=Y^{-7/16}<Y^{-3/8},
\]

and the optimized \(q>4\) final-range result is unavailable. The source's
elementary small-height estimate is a different route and supplies no
unpriced moving-wedge pair theorem.

### Arithmetic-carrier audit

The M1 denominator-quarter carrier does not force the BV loss asserted at
discovery lines 188--194 and in its coefficient table. Split
\[
b=4u+r,\qquad r\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]
Then
\[
e(\pm b/4)=e(\pm r/4)
\]
on that residue class. Put \(M'=D/4\), up to an \(O(1)\) endpoint
adjustment, and
\[
F_r(z)=\frac{1}{4z+r/M'}.
\]
For \(z=u/M'\),
\[
\frac{a(c/\kappa_i)}{M'}F_r(u/M')
=\frac{ac}{\kappa_i(4u+r)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
Uniformly in \(r\) and large \(D\),
\[
F_r'=-\frac4{(4z+r/M')^2},\quad
F_r''=\frac{32}{(4z+r/M')^3},\quad
F_r'''=-\frac{384}{(4z+r/M')^4},
\]
\[
F_r'F_r'''-3(F_r'')^2
=-\frac{1536}{(4z+r/M')^6}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Thus both source derivative conditions hold with fixed constants on the
relevant compact interval. Any already separated denominator profile
\(G(b/D)\) becomes
\(G(u/M'+r/(4M'))\), with the same uniform BV bound. Four residue
classes and \(O(1)\) endpoint
pieces absorb the M1 carrier at \(O(1)\) cost. Treating
\(e(\pm b/4)\) as one rapidly oscillating continuous \(G(b/D)\) indeed
has variation \(\asymp D\), but that is an avoidable representation, not
a necessary projective-BV cost.

For M2, the exact finite Fourier identity
\[
\chi _4(a)=\frac{e(a/4)-e(-a/4)}{2i}
\tag{3.11}
\]
gives two source phases. If
\[
F_\sigma(z)=F(z)+\sigma\frac{M}{4T},\qquad \sigma=\pm1,
\]
then
\[
\frac{hT}{M}F_\sigma(m/M)
=\frac{hT}{M}F(m/M)+\sigma\frac h4.
\tag{3.12}
\]
All derivatives and the determinant
\(F'F'''-3(F'')^2\) are unchanged. Hence the numerator character also
costs only \(O(1)\) phase pieces. For a two-wave correlation one obtains
only \(O(1)\) more combinations. These observations do not absorb
primitive lifts, Möbius/Stieltjes profiles, determinant ownership, or the
joint taper.

### Why one wave cannot imply the pair bound

A legal separated black-box reduction would need, for example,

\[
A_i(r)B_i(r,r')
=\sum_j u_j(r)v_j(r')
\tag{3.13}
\]

with every determinant selector, taper, star, cell, threshold,
Möbius/Stieltjes profile, quarter or \(\chi_4\) carrier, sign, and owner
retained, and with a proved projective norm small enough after applying
the source theorem to both factors. No such identity or inequality is
present.

Fixing \(r\) does not convert the problem to one source sum: it leaves a
moving, outer-dependent wedge in \(r'\), followed by an outer sum. Taking
absolute values replaces the literal signed scalar by a positive count;
the double-large-sieve inequality in Li--Yang goes from a source minor-arc
sum to positive spacing norms and is not a reverse operator bound for an
arbitrary pair coefficient. The source derivative approximants
\(a_{\rm BI}/r_{\rm BI}\) are also created after short-interval
localization and have \(r_{\rm BI}\lesssim Y^{1/6}\), while project
denominators have size \(Y^{1/2}\). The determinant condition on ordinary
ratios supplies none of the four modular-inverse/real second-spacing
conditions. These are independent obstructions.

The blind aligned-packet control is consistent with this conclusion:
at \(b'=b\) and \(c=\kappa_i b^2\), the geometric phase is \(1\), leaving
the full \(D/W=Y^{3/48}\) determinant-shift capacity. It is not a physical
lower bound, but it rules out a phase-only saving.

The same conclusion must be scoped carefully. Equations (2.3) and
(3.13) obstruct a factor-first use of the final one-wave theorem. They
do not exclude deriving a new joint inequality from the underlying DLS
before separating the two waves. The present source-level no-go for such
a route comes instead from the missing approximant-dependent coefficient
map, the positive arcwise absolute-value placement, and the absent
four-coordinate owner-preserving connector. It is a no-go for the
audited source interface, not for all conceivable Bombieri--Iwaniec
arguments.
