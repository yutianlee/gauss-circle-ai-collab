## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses

Fix \(X\geq2\), \(L\geq2\), \(\sigma\in\{\pm1\}\), \(B>0\), and a nonempty dyadic block \(Y<h\leq2Y\) with \(Y>H_B\), exactly as in the packet.  Only (B187.1)--(B187.13), including all literal support predicates hidden in the one fixed coefficient \(A_{L,X}^{\sigma}\), are used.  Choose fixed support constants \(0<c<C_0\) such that every nonzero \(A(m,d)\) has
\[
 cL\leq m,d\leq C_0L.
\tag{2.1}
\]
All divisor-logarithms and the product of the two pointwise coefficient envelopes will be denoted by \(\mathcal X_*\); this is the same permitted generic subpolynomial-loss convention under which the packet states the positive capacity as \(O(YL^2\mathcal X)\).

For an outer row \(\mathfrak f=(\kappa,g,h,U,v)\), define
\[
 C_{\mathfrak f,\omega}
 :=\sum_{t\in I_{\mathfrak f,\omega}}(-1)^t
 B_{\mathfrak f,\omega}^{\sigma}(t).
\tag{2.2}
\]
For \(U>1\), put \(a_{\mathfrak f}=[\bar vh]_U\).  Then the exact orientation trace is
\[
 (-1)^{S_{0,+}}C_{\mathfrak f,+}
 +(-1)^{S_{0,-}}C_{\mathfrak f,-}
 =E_U(a_{\mathfrak f})
 \bigl(C_{\mathfrak f,+}-C_{\mathfrak f,-}\bigr).
\tag{2.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr T_Y=\Re\Bigg\{&
 \sum_{\substack{\kappa,g,h,v>0\\
                   \kappa,g\ {\mathrm{odd}},\ (g,v)=1\\
                   Y<h\leq2Y,\ 0<2\kappa gh<R_0}}
 \bigl(C_{(\kappa,g,h,1,v),+}+C_{(\kappa,g,h,1,v),-}\bigr)\\
&+\sum_{\substack{\kappa,g,h,U,v>0\\
                   U>1,\ \kappa,g,U\ {\mathrm{odd}}\\
                   (gU,v)=1,\ (U,h)=1\\
                   Y<h\leq2Y,\ 0<2\kappa gh<R_0}}
 {1\over U}\sum_{k\bmod U}\widehat E_U(k)
 e\!\left({k\bar vh\over U}\right)
 \bigl(C_{\mathfrak f,+}-C_{\mathfrak f,-}\bigr)
 \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]
There is one real part in (2.4), exactly as in (B187.12).

More generally, for any family \(\mathcal K=(\mathcal K_U)_{U>1,\ U\ {\mathrm{odd}}}\) of mode sets, define
\[
 \beta_U(\mathcal K):={1\over U}
       \sum_{k\in\mathcal K_U}|\widehat E_U(k)|,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W(\mathcal K):=
 \sup_{V\geq1}\sum_{\substack{V\leq U\leq C_1V\\U>1\ {\mathrm{odd}}}}
       \beta_U(\mathcal K),
\tag{2.5}
\]
where \(C_1>1\) depends only on the ratio \(C_0/c\).  The coefficient-free packet lemma proved below is
\[
 \left|\Re\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak f:\,U>1\\Y<h\leq2Y}}
 {1\over U}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_U}\widehat E_U(k)
 e\!\left({k\bar vh\over U}\right)
 (C_{\mathfrak f,+}-C_{\mathfrak f,-})\right|
 \ll L^2\mathcal X_*\,\mathfrak W(\mathcal K).
\tag{2.6}
\]
Thus every packet with subpolynomial \(\mathfrak W\) is target-safe.  This is the full class certified by the available absolute-capacity argument; (2.6) does not assert that packets outside this class cannot cancel by a new property of the literal coefficients.

For the explicit packet in Section 1, set
\[
 \mathcal K_U(P)=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathbb Z/U\mathbb Z,&1<U\leq4P,\\
  \{k\bmod U:|k|_U\leq P\},&U>4P.
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.7}
\]
It satisfies \(\mathfrak W(\mathcal K(P))\ll P\log(2P)\).  Let \(\mathscr S_{Y,P}\) be the \(U=1\) line of (2.4) plus the \(\mathcal K_U(P)\) part of its second line.  Then
\[
 |\mathscr S_{Y,P}|\ll_{B,C,\varepsilon}L^2X^\varepsilon.
\tag{2.8}
\]
The exact complement is
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathscr R_{Y,P}:=\Re
 \sum_{\substack{\kappa,g,h,U,v>0\\
                  U>4P,\ \kappa,g,U\ {\mathrm{odd}}\\
                  (gU,v)=1,\ (U,h)=1\\
                  Y<h\leq2Y,\ 0<2\kappa gh<R_0}}
 {1\over U}
 \sum_{\substack{k\bmod U\\P<|k|_U\leq(U-1)/2}}
 \widehat E_U(k)e\!\left({k\bar vh\over U}\right)
 \left[
 \sum_{t\in I_{\mathfrak f,+}}(-1)^tB_{\mathfrak f,+}^{\sigma}(t)
 -\sum_{t\in I_{\mathfrak f,-}}(-1)^tB_{\mathfrak f,-}^{\sigma}(t)
 \right].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.9}
\]
Exactly, \(\mathscr T_Y=\mathscr S_{Y,P}+\mathscr R_{Y,P}\).  The desired one-sided inequality follows from the still-unproved one-sided bound \(\mathscr R_{Y,P}\ll L^2X^\varepsilon\).

## 3. Proof or derivation

**Primitive carrier and endpoint products.**  Put
\[
 D_0=\kappa gU,\qquad D_t=\kappa gU+2s_{t,\omega}
       =g(\kappa U+2S_{t,\omega}),
\qquad M_0=\kappa v,\qquad M_t=\kappa v+2w_{t,\omega}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The definitions of \(S_0,w_0\) give the two exact Bezout relations
\[
 vS_{t,+}-Uw_{t,+}=h,
 \qquad
 Uw_{t,-}-vS_{t,-}=h.
\tag{3.2}
\]
They imply
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\omega&N_{\mathfrak f,t}^{\omega}&N_{\mathfrak f,t}^{\omega}+r&
\text{positive determinant}\\ \hline
+&D_0M_t&D_tM_0&D_tM_0-D_0M_t=r\\
-&D_tM_0&D_0M_t&D_0M_t-D_tM_0=r.
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Thus the two literal endpoint arguments are precisely
\[
 (m,d)=(M_t,D_0),\qquad (m,d)=(M_0,D_t),
\tag{3.4}
\]
and the orientations exchange which one is lower and which one is upper.  Since \(\kappa,g,U\) are odd, both \(D_0,D_t\) are odd, so the odd-divisor condition in \(\lambda\) is exact.  On the carrier where both endpoint products are squarefree, (B187.9)--(B187.11) equal
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{\mathfrak f,+}^{\sigma}(t)
={}&\left(1-{r\over R_0}\right)
 \rho_{D_tM_0}(D_t)\rho_{D_0M_t}(D_0)\\
&\quad\times A(M_0,D_t)\overline{A(M_t,D_0)}
 e\!\left(\sigma\sqrt X(\sqrt{D_tM_0}-\sqrt{D_0M_t})\right),\\
B_{\mathfrak f,-}^{\sigma}(t)
={}&\left(1-{r\over R_0}\right)
 \rho_{D_0M_t}(D_0)\rho_{D_tM_0}(D_t)\\
&\quad\times A(M_t,D_0)\overline{A(M_0,D_t)}
 e\!\left(\sigma\sqrt X(\sqrt{D_0M_t}-\sqrt{D_tM_0})\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]
If either endpoint product is not squarefree, the relevant \(\lambda\), hence \(B\), is zero before any \(\rho_N\) is invoked; thus (3.5) never extends \(\rho_N\) beyond its stated squarefree domain.  Each occurrence of \(A\) retains, rather than relaxes, the original shell, height, cone, profile, floor, star, half-weight, hard-sample, crossing, endpoint, sign, squarefree, coprimality, and zero-extension fields.  Formula (3.5) uses
\[
 {r\over\sqrt{N+r}+\sqrt N}=\sqrt{N+r}-\sqrt N.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Positivity is exact.  Since \(U,v>0\),
\[
 I_{\mathfrak f,\omega}
 =\left\{t\in\mathbb Z:t>-{S_{0,\omega}\over U},\ 
                         t>-{w_{0,\omega}\over v}\right\};
\tag{3.7}
\]
it is an integer terminal ray before the literal support cuts it to a finite set.  For \(U>1\), \((U,h)=(U,v)=1\) makes both \(S_{0,+}\) and \(S_{0,-}\) lie in \(\{1,\ldots,U-1\}\), so the first inequality in (3.7) is \(t\geq0\).  For \(U=1\),
\[
 I_{(\kappa,g,h,1,v),+}=\{t\in\mathbb Z:t>0,\ vt>h\},
 \qquad
 I_{(\kappa,g,h,1,v),-}=\{t\in\mathbb Z:t>0\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
No square root is evaluated outside these positive sets.  Moreover, because \(U\) is odd,
\[
 (-1)^{S_{0,\omega}}(-1)^t
 =(-1)^{S_{0,\omega}+Ut}=(-1)^{S_{t,\omega}},
\tag{3.9}
\]
so the parity sign has no missing \(U\)-dependent factor.

**Multiplicity-one scope.**  Multiplicity one holds for a nonzero ordered endpoint configuration, not for formal zero rows.  Indeed, from its two factor pairs identify \(D_0<D_t\) and \(M_0<M_t\).  Squarefreeness of \(D_tM_0\), together with \(\kappa\mid M_0\), shows \((\kappa,S_{t,\omega})=1\); otherwise an odd prime dividing both would divide both \(D_t\) and \(M_0\).  Also \((U,S_{t,\omega})=1\) follows from (3.2), \((U,h)=1\), and \((U,v)=1\).  Hence
\[
 g=(D_0,D_t),\qquad
 \kappa=(D_0,M_0),\qquad
 U={D_0\over\kappa g},\qquad v={M_0\over\kappa},
\tag{3.10}
\]
and then
\[
 S={D_t-D_0\over2g},\qquad w={M_t-M_0\over2},\qquad
 h=|vS-Uw|.
\tag{3.11}
\]
The sign of \(vS-Uw\) determines the orientation, and the canonical residue \(S_0\) then gives the unique \(t=(S-S_0)/U\).  The allocation-independent selection rule makes \(\rho_N\) a function of the endpoint \(N\), rather than of a representation, and therefore introduces no second allocation multiplicity.

**Positive capacity and the missing scale.**  A nonzero term has all four quantities in (3.1) in the interval (2.1).  For fixed \(\kappa,g,h\), this gives
\[
 \#U\ll {L\over\kappa g},\qquad
 \#v\ll {L\over\kappa},\qquad
 \#t\ll 1+{L\over gU}\ll\kappa.
\tag{3.12}
\]
The coprimality, parity, squarefree, selector, and all literal masks only reduce these counts.  Also
\[
 \kappa g<{R_0\over2h}\ll {L\over Y}.
\tag{3.13}
\]
Therefore the number of nonzero summands in both orientations is
\[
 \ll L^2\sum_{Y<h\leq2Y}
       \sum_{\kappa g<R_0/(2h)}{1\over\kappa g}
 \ll YL^2(1+\log(2L))^2.
\tag{3.14}
\]
Since \(0<1-r/R_0<1\), \(\rho\in\{0,1\}\), and each term contains two pointwise-bounded coefficients, (3.14) is exactly the advertised
\[
 O(YL^2\mathcal X)
\tag{3.15}
\]
positive capacity after the allowed loss is relabelled as \(\mathcal X_*\).  The target has no \(Y\), so the missing scale is the full factor \(Y\) (up to allowed losses).  In a terminal truncated dyadic block, the allowed \(h\)'s form a subset of \((Y,2Y]\), while every surviving \(h\asymp Y\) still gives (3.13).  Thus (3.14) remains valid with \(Y\), and truncation alone cannot be credited as a uniform gain: for admissible small \(\kappa g\), a terminal block may still contain \(\asymp Y\) values.

**Fourier normalization and anchors.**  With the inversion convention in (B187.13), the forward transform is
\[
 \widehat E_U(k)=\sum_{a=0}^{U-1}(-1)^a e(-ka/U).
\tag{3.16}
\]
The ratio is \(-e(-k/U)\).  Since \(U\) is odd, its \(U\)-th power is \(-1\), and therefore
\[
 \widehat E_U(k)
 ={1-(-e(-k/U))^U\over1+e(-k/U)}
 ={2\over1+e(-k/U)}.
\tag{3.17}
\]
The denominator never vanishes for odd \(U\).  Finite Fourier inversion proves (B187.13), and in particular \(\widehat E_U(0)=1\).  Also
\[
 |\widehat E_U(k)|={1\over|\cos(\pi k/U)|}.
\tag{3.18}
\]
The plus anchor is immediate from \(S_{0,+}=[\bar vh]_U\).  If \(a=[\bar vh]_U\), then \(a\neq0\), \(S_{0,-}=U-a\), and oddness of \(U\) gives
\[
 (-1)^{S_{0,-}}=(-1)^{U-a}=-(-1)^a=-E_U(a),
\tag{3.19}
\]
which proves (2.3).  For \(U=1\), both anchors equal \(1\), so the orientation sum is \(C_++C_-\), not the trace difference; this is why it must remain separate.

**Pricing the zero, low, and nonresonant modes.**  The zero mode is exactly
\[
 \mathscr Z_Y=\Re
 \sum_{\substack{\kappa,g,h,U,v>0\\
                  U>1,\ \kappa,g,U\ {\mathrm{odd}}\\
                  (gU,v)=1,\ (U,h)=1\\
                  Y<h\leq2Y,\ 0<2\kappa gh<R_0}}
 {1\over U}
 \left[
 \sum_{t\in I_{\mathfrak f,+}}(-1)^tB_{\mathfrak f,+}^{\sigma}(t)
 -\sum_{t\in I_{\mathfrak f,-}}(-1)^tB_{\mathfrak f,-}^{\sigma}(t)
 \right].
\tag{3.20}
\]
Here \(U\asymp L/(\kappa g)\) on support, so
\[
 \sum_{U\asymp L/(\kappa g)}{1\over U}\ll1.
\tag{3.21}
\]
After the \(v,t\) counts in (3.12), the price per \(\kappa,g,h\) is \(O(L\mathcal X_*)\).  The elementary divisor count
\[
 \sum_{Y<h\leq2Y}\#\{(\kappa,g):\kappa g<R_0/(2h)\}
 \ll\sum_{Y<h\leq2Y}{L\over h}\log(2L)
 \ll L\log(2L)
\tag{3.22}
\]
therefore gives
\[
 |\mathscr Z_Y|\ll L^2\mathcal X_*.
\tag{3.23}
\]
This modulus is legitimate because the zero sector has now been proved target-safe with all outer labels present.

The same calculation proves (2.6): replace (3.21) by the defining bound \(\mathfrak W(\mathcal K)\), while retaining the \(h,\kappa,g,v,t\) counts in (3.22).  To price (2.7), (3.18) first gives
\[
 {1\over U}\sum_{k\bmod U}|\widehat E_U(k)|\ll\log(2U).
\tag{3.24}
\]
For \(U>4P\) and \(|k|_U\leq P\), one has \(|\cos(\pi k/U)|\geq2^{-1/2}\), whence
\[
 \beta_U(\mathcal K(P))\ll {P\over U}.
\tag{3.25}
\]
For \(U\leq4P\), (3.24) applies to all modes.  Summing either case over any fixed-ratio \(U\)-interval gives
\[
 \mathfrak W(\mathcal K(P))\ll P\log(2P),
\tag{3.26}
\]
which proves (2.8) for polylogarithmic \(P\).

For \(U=1\), support forces \(\kappa g\asymp L\), whereas \(2\kappa gh<R_0\) forces \(h=O(1)\).  There are \(O(L\log(2L))\) possible \((\kappa,g)\), and the \(v,t\) product in (3.12) is \(O(L)\), so its whole contribution is also \(O(L^2\mathcal X_*)\).  This remains valid when the high-height condition makes the \(U=1\) sector empty.

Finally, a “nonresonant” family containing \(M_U\asymp U\) modes is not target-safe by capacity.  Even when \(|\cos(\pi k/U)|\geq\delta>0\), (3.18) gives \(\beta_U\asymp_\delta M_U/U\asymp1\); a support interval contains \(\asymp L/(\kappa g)\) choices of \(U\).  Substitution into (2.6)'s counting before the \(\mathfrak W\) shortcut recovers (3.14), namely \(O(YL^2\mathcal X_*)\).  The modes nearest \(U/2\) make the absolute Fourier mass logarithmically larger, not smaller.  Positive Fourier recombination or an alias-energy identity therefore cannot replace cancellation in (2.9).

Outcome: both orientations, high-height positivity convention, endpoint order, and determinant sign agree with (3.3).

2. **Selector and zero-extension control.**  If a pair \(\{p,q\}\) is selected, the four possibilities for \((\mathbf1_{p\mid d},\mathbf1_{q\mid d})\) give

Outcome: an envelope-only alternating-sum or phase-oscillation argument is false.  This control is not a lower bound for the physical coefficient and does not assert that there are capacity-many physical survivors; it identifies the literal coefficient-correlation property that a valid proof still has to supply.
