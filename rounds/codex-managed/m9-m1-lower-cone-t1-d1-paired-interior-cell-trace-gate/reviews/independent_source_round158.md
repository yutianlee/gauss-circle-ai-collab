## 2. Primary theorem statements and legal interfaces

The cited DFI Lemma 6.1 is an exact pointwise match for one
\(K(m,n;c)\), with \(c\equiv0\pmod4\), and gives the stated
\((m,n,c)^{1/2}c^{1/2}\tau(c)\) bound. It supplies neither a moving
Abel sum nor a fixed-modulus spectral average. The quoted DFI spectral
formula has materially different smooth-test-function and
modulus-average hypotheses, so its rejection is correct.

Müllner's complete and incomplete quadratic estimates accept arbitrary
integer coefficients, modulus and interval endpoints, with the stated
gcd factor; they are unweighted estimates. Passing to a boundary
coefficient by Abel summation requires the diagonal BV hypothesis
(158.SA54), which is expressly not supplied. Vaaler's interval
majorant/minorant mechanism is likewise legal only for a fixed
interval; it does not supply control of the moving endpoints or a
root-indexed complex coefficient.

The Bourgain and Tao--Trudgian--Yang exponent-pair cards give
unweighted interval estimates for a model phase after the source
parameter, model-phase derivative class, and \(T\geq L\) conditions
are checked. For \(h\sqrt{N\ell}\) with \(\ell\asymp M\), those
conditions hold in the deliberately easier unweighted model. They do
not insert arbitrary coefficients. The Shparlinski--Xiao, character,
moment, floor, and limiting-distribution exclusions identify explicit
prime/composite-modulus, independent-root, weight, interval, or
effectivity mismatches and are correctly non-imports.

## 3. Exact first coefficient and moving-mask mismatch

The source report correctly rewrites the strict object as a sum over
\(\ell\) with nearest root \(k(\ell)\), literal strict residual
selector, quotient sign \(\chi_4(\ell)\), and coefficient
\(F_{k(\ell)-1}(k(\ell))\) or \(F_{-k(\ell)}(k(\ell))\). The
coefficient is sampled at the boundary argument just below or above
\(\ell\), not at \(\ell\). Thus fixed-modulus root theorems that sum
an independently occurring root with only an outer coefficient cannot
be inserted without a theorem covering this root-dependent sampling
and the one-sided strict selector.

This is the first exact mismatch. It precedes all power comparisons:
the project assumptions provide only pointwise atom control, not
diagonal variation in \(k\) or \(\ell\). The source-favorable BV grant
(158.SA54) is therefore properly labelled illegal for the literal
trace.

One wording requires correction. The residual factors
\[
 e(\sqrt{k^2-k+1}-k),\qquad e(\sqrt{k^2+k}-k)
\]
are not algebraically a fixed shift of the Vaaler frequency. They are
near fixed phases on the top block, but their remaining
\(k\)-dependence belongs in the coefficient and needs its own BV or
joint estimate. This strengthens, rather than removes, the stated
first mismatch.

## 4. Completion capacity and its scope

Under the explicit extra BV grant, insertion of the exact
\(4N\)-projector leaves incomplete quadratic sums with modulus
\(q=4N\). Müllner plus Abel summation gives
\[
 \left|\mathcal S_++\mathcal S_-\right|
 \ll W\sqrt N\,X^\varepsilon,\qquad W=M^{-3/4}.
\]
The gcd and harmonic denominator ledger in (158.SA55)--(158.SA58)
has the correct normalization up to harmless constants from \(q=4N\).
After removing \(W\), this is raw capacity \(N^{1/2}X^\varepsilon\).

Comparison with the actual raw requirement
\(M^{3/4}X^\varepsilon\) gives \(M\ge N^{2/3}\), outside the frozen
\(M\le N^{1/2}\) cone. This is an upper bound for an easier,
coefficient-BV model. It is neither a lower bound for the literal
sum nor a proof that a different completion with joint coefficient
cancellation cannot work.

## 5. Fourier cutoff and exponent-pair algebra

For a signed complex trace, the Vaaler \(L^1\)-excess costs
\(M/J\) after absolute restoration, so target feasibility forces
\(J\ge M^{1/4}\). With fixed endpoints and an additionally granted
unit-BV coefficient, an exponent pair \((\kappa,\lambda)\) yields
\[
 E(J)\ll \frac MJ+
 N^{\kappa/2}M^{\lambda-\kappa/2}J^\kappa X^\varepsilon.
\]
At the mandatory cutoff, the target condition is exactly
\[
 M\ge N^{\,2\kappa/(\kappa+3-4\lambda)}
\]
when the denominator is positive.

For Bourgain's \((13/84,55/84)\), this is
\(M\ge N^{26/45}\). For the source-legal transformed pair
\((195/796,235/398)\), the oscillatory term at \(J=M^{1/4}\) is
\[
 N^{195/1592}M^{1685/3184},
\]
and comparison with \(M^{3/4}\) is precisely
\[
 M^{703}\ge N^{390}.
\]
Thus \(M\ge N^{390/703}>N^{1/2}\). The algebra and the stated
390/703 threshold are correct. The comparison may only be called a
capacity failure among the audited model placements; it is not an
optimality theorem for exponent pairs or for all discrepancy methods.
