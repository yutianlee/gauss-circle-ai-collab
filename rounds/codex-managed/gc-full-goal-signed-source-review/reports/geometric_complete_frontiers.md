## 3. Derivation and complete restoration

### 3.1 Exact product map and actual vectors

Use only the accepted flat inverse-selector kernel:
\[
 X=N_0+\xi,\quad N_0=\lfloor X\rfloor,\quad0\le\xi<1,\qquad
 R=X/D,\quad K=XL/D^2,\quad\Delta=R/K=D/L.
\]
For \(r=gn,k=gj\), characters force \(g,n\) odd, \(gn\asymp R\), \(j\asymp J=K/g\), and \((j,n)=1\). The literal zero-extended row is
\[
 b_{g,n}(j)=\frac{q_L(4Xj/(gn^2))}{gj}e(\xi j/n),\qquad
 \|b_{g,n}\|_2\ll X^\varepsilon(gK)^{-1/2},\quad
 \|b_{g,n}\|_1\ll X^\varepsilon/g.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(q_L\) retains its inherited moving mask and endpoints; it has not been flattened. Since \(J/n\asymp\Delta^{-1}<1\), supported \(j\)'s have distinct residues.

Use the inverse-first, equivalent symmetric convention
\[
 S(m,h;n)=\sum_{x\bmod n}^{*}e((m\bar x+hx)/n),\qquad
 \widehat\gamma(h)=n^{-1}\sum_jb_j e(-h\bar j/n).
\]
Finite orthogonality gives
\[
 U_{g,n}:=\sum_{h\bmod n}\widehat\gamma(h)S(N_0,h;n)
       =\sum_jb_j e(N_0j/n).
 \tag{2}
\]
Substitute \(h=jv\bmod n\) for each unit \(j\), then \(x=\bar j u\):
\[
 S(N_0,jv;n)=S(N_0j,v;n),\qquad
 U_{g,n}=\frac1n\sum_jb_j\sum_{v\bmod n}e(-v/n)S(N_0j,v;n).
 \tag{3}
\]
The coefficients truly factor for fixed \(g,n\). The full \(v\)-norm is \(\sqrt n\), and an interval of \(V\) entries has norm \(\sqrt V\). The factor \(1/n\), additive coefficient \(e(-v/n)\), and real-centre factor in (1) are compulsory. Summing \(v\) first returns (2) exactly; completion has supplied an identity, not a saving.

The flat physical wave is \(-i/(2\pi)\) times
\[
 \mathcal R=\sum_{g,n}\chi_4(g)\chi_4(n)
 W(X/(gnD))\,U_{g,n},
 \tag{4}
\]
plus already owned flat errors. Original \(h=0\) is exactly \(v=0\), because \(j\) is a unit. Its global Ramanujan contribution is the inherited \(O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)\), removed once. All other residues, including nonunit \(v\), remain. Both characters in (4) are actual; taking their absolute values is the positive certificate being tested.

### 3.3 Pascadi: short and longer intervals

For \((N_0,n)=1\), use \(c=n,a=N_0\) in (P). Since \(j\) is a unit, \((j,v,n)=1\) holds even for nonunit \(v\). Its moving mask is legal inside the arbitrary \(j\)-vector. Bad rows are not declared eligible.

At interval length \(\sqrt n\), the full \(v\)-partition has sum of norms \(n^{3/4}\). If \(J>\sqrt n\), splitting \(j\) costs at most \(\sqrt J/n^{1/4}\) in its norm sum. Restoring \(1/n\), the display is
\[
 n^{3/4+o(1)}F_n\|b\|_2\max(1,\sqrt J/n^{1/4}).
 \tag{7}
\]
Because \(f\ge d\) and \(\min(n,d^2)\le d\sqrt n\), \(F_n\ge n^{-1/12}\). Thus even the favorable balanced-\(pq\) or prime-square case costs \(n^{2/3+o(1)}\|b\|_2\) for \(J\le\sqrt n\), compared with \(n^{1/2}\|b\|_2\) in (5): a certificate loss \(n^{1/6}\). Other factorizations may be worse.

The full-range theorem does not rescue this partition. At \(M=n,N=J\), its extra factor is \((dJ+f+f/d^2)^{1/6}\ge1\). More generally divide the complete \(v\)-range into comparable intervals of length \(V\), retaining the \(j\)-vector. The first term of (P), after norms, interval summation and \(1/n\), contributes the following ratio of certificate expressions to \(\sqrt n\|b\|_2\):
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (dJ)^{1/6},&V\ge J,\\
 d^{1/6}J^{1/2}V^{-1/3},&V<J.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{8}
\]
Both are at least one. End intervals alter only constants. This prices the tested full-frequency triangle partition at every \(V\), not all possible signed uses of the theorem or its proof. Capping by (5), including all bad rows, returns (6).

### 3.4 FKMS: legal orientation and geometry

For a prime row \(n=p,\ p\nmid N_0\), put \(K_{N_0}(t)=p^{-1/2}S(N_0,t;p)\). It is a nonzero dilation of the normalized classical Kloosterman trace. The exact product is \(K_{N_0}(jv)\), with \(b=c=1\); the entire inverse-selector coefficient is not being declared one sheaf.

Assign long \(v\) to the theorem's \(M\)-variable and short \(j\) to its \(N\)-variable. Thus completion is not itself a length-hypothesis failure. A mandatory dyadic \(v\)-interval has \(V\asymp p\), norm \(\asymp\sqrt p\). At \(M\asymp p,N=J\), restoring \(p^{-1/2}\) in (3), (F) gives
\[
 p^\varepsilon\sqrt{pJ}\|b\|_2
 \left[p^{-1}+
 \left(p^{-1/4+7/(4l)}/J\right)^{1/l}\right]^{1/2}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The second-term ratio to (5) equals
\[
 J^{(l-1)/(2l)}p^{-1/(8l)+7/(8l^2)}
 \ge c_l p^{(5l+1)/(8l^2)}
 \quad\text{if }J\ge10p^{3/(2l)}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Every eligible fixed \(l\) leaves a power loss; ineligible \(J\) has no application of (F). Remaining \(v\)-intervals must also be summed. Taking absolute values after the partition cannot cure the mandatory interval's cost. Arbitrary translations are not free: \(K(j(v_0+v))\) is not simply \(K(jv)\) with product coefficients. Equation (9) uses actual dyadic intervals.

When \(p\mid N_0\), the nonzero-frequency sum degenerates to a Ramanujan sum; the gallant claim is not available. Composite and prime-power \(n\) lie outside (F). CRT would need a separate coefficient/interval/alias ledger, not a claim that prime-field eligibility tensorizes. These complements retain (5) or better inherited estimates. The actual \(g,n\) in (4) are odd, so no 2-adic rows are omitted there; this says nothing about 2-adic branches on the other frontiers.

The theorem controls its own exceptional geometry. No bad locus is deleted from (F), nor is a squarefree selector, growing-conductor family or rank-one phase assumed gallant by resemblance. Fixed \(l\) and bounded Kloosterman complexity suffice for the legal row; growing \(l\) or complexity with \(X\) has no constant ledger.
