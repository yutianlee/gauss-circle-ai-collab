\[
 A_d^{(j)}=\sum_{s\ge1}b_{ds^2}^{(j)},\qquad

\[

K1 takes identical sequences. K2 assumes the additional common envelope \(b_n\le C\tau(n)n^{-3/4}\), with fixed \(C\ge0\). K3 takes identical sequences and the exact tail condition \(\max_j n_j>U\).

## 3. Independent proof checks

### 3.1. Radical induction

The simultaneous induction at kernel lines 44--56 is valid. At the preceding stage, the rational squareclasses becoming squares in \(K_{r-1}\) are exactly those generated by \(p_1,\ldots,p_{r-1}\). Since the new prime \(p_r\) has odd valuation at \(p_r\), it cannot belong to an old squareclass. Thus \(X^2-p_r\) is irreducible over \(K_{r-1}\), the extension is quadratic, and the stated basis doubles.

If \(q\in\mathbb Q^\times\) and \((u+v\sqrt{p_r})^2=q\), comparison in that quadratic basis gives \(2uv=0\). A field has no zero divisors, so either \(v=0\) and \(q=u^2\), or \(u=0\) and \(q/p_r=v^2\). Both remaining numbers are rational, and the preceding induction hypothesis applies. Conversely, the squareclass description visibly constructs a square root in the field. This proves both assertions without circularly assuming the basis or radical independence. Negative rational \(q\) cause no exception: the field is embedded in the reals and the described squareclasses are positive.

Every squarefree \(d\) made from these finitely many primes is a distinct basis product; \(d=1\) is the empty product. Therefore exact radical cancellation must occur separately in each squarefree class. A class occurring once cannot cancel, because its integer multiplier is a positive \(s\). This proves the no-singleton condition used by K1.

### 3.2. K1 and the mixed multiplicities

For a fixed ordered tuple, its partition by equal squarefree part is unique. Once a partition and sign vector are fixed, dropping each block's integer relation and dropping the distinctness condition between different block values of \(d\) produces an upper bound, because every summand is nonnegative.

For a block \(I\) of size \(h\ge2\), the enlarged block sum factors exactly as

\[
 \sum_{d\ \mathrm{squarefree}}\prod_{j\in I} A_d^{(j)}.
\]

Hölder with \(h\) equal exponents bounds this by \(\prod_{j\in I}\|A^{(j)}\|_h\). For any sequence and \(h\ge2\), \(\|A\|_h\le\|A\|_2\). For example, after dividing by its nonzero \(\ell^2\) norm, each term is at most one, so its \(h\)th power is at most its square. Thus the block bound is \(\prod_{j\in I}S_j^{1/2}\).

Each label belongs to exactly one block, so multiplying the block bounds includes each norm precisely once. The at most \(B_k\) partitions and exactly \(2^k\) sign vectors produce the asserted constant. There is no missing permutation factor: the partitions are partitions of the already labeled positions, and each \(s_j\) is summed independently. Repeated indices, unequal supports, and unbalanced sign vectors are all present. Allowing two blocks to choose the same \(d\) merely overcounts in the permitted direction. A zero \(S_j\) forces that nonnegative sequence to vanish, as the kernel states.

### 3.3. K2 and its zeta constant

The primewise inequality \(a+b+1\le(a+1)(b+1)\) proves divisor submultiplicativity without a coprimality assumption. At a prime exponent \(e\),

\[
 \binom{e+2}{2}-(2e+1)=\frac{e(e-1)}2\ge0,
\]

including \(e=0,1\). Consequently \(\tau(s^2)\le\tau_3(s)\), exactly as required at kernel lines 82--89, and

\[
 A_d\le C\tau(d)d^{-3/4}
 \sum_{s\ge1}\tau_3(s)s^{-3/2}
 =C\tau(d)d^{-3/4}\zeta(3/2)^3.
\]

For squarefree \(d\), \(\tau(d)^2=4^{\omega(d)}=\tau_4(d)\). Thus

\[
 S\le C^2\zeta(3/2)^6
 \sum_{d\ \mathrm{squarefree}}\tau_4(d)d^{-3/2}
 \le C^2\zeta(3/2)^{10}.
\]

Inserting this in K1 gives \(2^kB_kC^k\zeta(3/2)^{5k}\). The exponents 10 and \(5k\) are correct. The positive Dirichlet-series products converge at \(3/2>1\), so their rearrangement requires no asymptotic theorem.

### 3.4. K3, infinite limits, and uniformity

Since \(S<\infty\), every \(A_d\) is finite. For fixed \(d\), the nonnegative series tail \(\sum_{ds^2>U}b_{ds^2}\) tends to zero. Its square is dominated by \(A_d^2\), and \(\sum_d A_d^2=S\). Dominated convergence therefore proves \(S_{>U}\to0\).

The indicator of \(\max_j n_j>U\) is at most \(\sum_{j=1}^k1_{n_j>U}\). For its \(j\)th summand apply the mixed K1 bound with that coordinate replaced by \(b_n1_{n>U}\). Its class norm is \(S_{>U}^{1/2}\); the other \(k-1\) norms are \(S^{1/2}\). Summation over the \(k\) coordinates proves K3 with exactly its printed factor \(k2^kB_k\). Overlapping tail events are counted multiple times only in an upper bound.

For a family dominated by one fixed divisor envelope \(B_n\), both \(S(b)\le S(B)\) and \(S_{>U}(b)\le S_{>U}(B)\). K2 proves \(S(B)<\infty\), and the preceding dominated convergence applies to that single envelope. This proves the stated family-uniform convergence; no unasserted uniformity in varying \(C\) or envelope is needed. Monotone convergence extends the nonnegative finite-support K1 statement. Absolute tail control then extends the signed exact coefficient.
