## 3. Derivation, source applications and full-goal comparison

**First new source interface: a weighted parabola, including the trace.**
Kim--Ko, [arXiv:2511.04503v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04503v2),
20 December2025, Theorem1.1 and definitions(2)--(4): for `2<=p<=4`,
Fourier support within S^(-1) of `(t,t^2), |t|<=1`, and `0<=H<=1`,

    ||f||_(Lp(H)) <= C_epsilon S^epsilon
      (max_U kappa_p,H(U)+S^(-100)) ||(sum_theta|f_theta|^2)^(1/2)||_p,
    kappa_p,H(U)=max_(V subset U)
      [H(V)/|V|]^(1/4)[H(U)/|U|]^(1/p-1/4).                   (F6)

Here theta has dimensions S^(-1/2) by S^(-1); at each
`S^(-1/2)<=s<=1`, V has dimensions s^(-1) by s^(-2) and U has
dimensions Ss by S, with the corresponding dual orientations. The
version replaces the earlier polylogarithmic claim by S^epsilon.
This paragraph and(F6) are the imported statement; the following is our
application calculation, not a result claimed in that source.

Take an actual block `N<=n<2N`, with any partial endpoints zero-extended,
and `S_N(R)=sum a_n(T)e(R sqrt n)`. Define

    xi_n=sqrt(n/(4N)),
    P_N(x,y)=sum a_n(T)e(x xi_n+y xi_n^2).                     (F7)

Then `S_N(R)=P_N(2sqrt(N)R,0)` exactly: these are actual parabola
frequencies, not a Taylor approximation. Localize R to windows of length
comparable to sqrt N, centered at R_j. Set S comparable to N and multiply
the translated P_N by a fixed Schwartz cutoff `chi(x/S,y/S)` with small
compact Fourier support and lower bound on the needed central box. Its
coefficients retain `a_n(T)e(R_j sqrt n)`. A fixed enlargement of S and
the source neighborhood accommodates the thickening. Fixed bounded
coordinate rescalings are harmless. The cap cover can be chosen in the
xi coordinate, so each cap contains O(sqrt N) integers, including all
actual multiplicities through r2(n).

Let `A_N=(1+sqrt(N/T))^(-A)` for any chosen fixed large A. The divisor
bound gives `|a_n|<<_eta N^(-3/4+eta) A_N`. Each cap function is therefore
bounded by `N^(-1/4+eta)A_N` times a Schwartz envelope at scale S; there
are O(sqrt N) caps. The cap projections have uniformly bounded scaled
convolution L1 norms. Consequently

    integral (sum_theta|f_theta|^2)^2 dxdy
                                <<_eta N^(2+eta) A_N^4.       (F8)

No arithmetic cancellation was used in this particular cap-norm bound.
Fixed y-bandwidth supplies a reproducing kernel and thus
`|f(x,0)|^4<<integral W(y)|f(x,y)|^4dy`, with a fixed rapidly decreasing
nonnegative W. Use H equal to a constant-normalized W(y) times the
indicator of the central x interval. The constant rescaling of W is
paid. For p=4, the second factor in kappa is 1. Every density is bounded
by1, while some unit-scale V meeting the central line has density bounded
below by a positive constant. Thus the actual maximum in(F6) is
comparable to1; dimensional language about a thin line cannot erase it.

Equations(F6)--(F8), the trace, and `dx=2sqrt(N)dR` give local fourth
moment `<<N^(3/2+epsilon)A_N^4`. There are `O(1+T/sqrt N)` windows. On
every effective block `N<=T^(3/2)` the full number is `O(T/sqrt N)`, so

    M4(H_N) <= M4(S_N) << T^(1+epsilon)N A_N^4.               (F9)

Partial end windows, translated centers and real T obey the same bound.
Summing the fourth roots over dyadic N and the singleton gives the
complete certificate `M4(B_T)<<T^(2+epsilon)`: indeed the geometric sum
of `N^(1/4)A_N` is O(T^(1/4)); the infinite Schwartz tail is summable.
It is weaker than accepted `M4(B_T)<<T^(1+epsilon)`. Combining(F9)'s
complete bound with the actual full supremum only gives
`M_p(B_T)<<T^(2+(p-4)beta+epsilon)` for p>=4, also worse than the accepted
family. This scopes the no-gain conclusion to(F8) and(F6), not to every
possible coefficient-sensitive estimate of the wave envelopes. Applying
(F6) directly at p=10 or12 violates its order hypothesis.

**Second new source interface: multiplicative minor-arc decomposition.**
Granville--Lamzouri, [arXiv:2604.02306v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02306v1),
2 April2026, Theorem1.1, first range: fix `0<eta<1/10`, `(a,q)=1`,
`|alpha-a/q|<=1/(qx)`,

    (log x)^(2+eta)<=q<=min(x^(1-eta),x/(log x)^(3+eta)),
    2<=M<<min(q^(1-eta/4)/(log x)^2,
                 exp((1/2-eta)sqrt(log x loglog x))).

For multiplicative `|f|<=1` its conclusion is

    sum_(n<=x)f(n)e(alpha n)
     =sum_(m<=M)f(m)sum_(x/M<p<=x/m, p prime)f(p)e(alpha mp)
       +O(x/(sqrt M log x)).                                 (F10)

Only this stated range is audited; no inference is made from the separate
larger-q clause. This is a decomposition, not a small bound for its prime
sum. The following tests and prices are our own.

The original r2(n)/4 is multiplicative but not 1-bounded: for a prime
1 modulo4 it equals2. Dividing all values by a large global constant does
not preserve multiplicativity. Opening `r2=4(1*chi4)` lawfully exposes
f=chi4; the remaining weights must stay outside(F10). In(F3), for a
fixed cofactor k, the d phase is `R sqrt(kd)`. For d near d0~D its
linear slope is `alpha=R sqrt(k)/(2sqrt(d0))`; its quadratic remainder
on a length ell interval is of order

    T sqrt(N) ell^2/D^2.                                    (F11)

To use a bounded-variation residual phase after linearization requires
`ell << D/(T sqrt N)^(1/2)`. If this is below one, no useful integer
interval is obtained. Otherwise the number of pieces is at least at
the power scale `(T sqrt N)^(1/2)`. This is not a permissible replacement
of x in(F10) by ell: shifting d=d0+h changes chi4(d0+h), which is in
general not a multiplicative function of h. Subtracting two prefix
formulas instead retains ambient x~D and the corresponding error.
No theorem here prices all those pieces, all k, or all anchored pairs.

There is an independent actual exceptional configuration. Exactly,

    chi4(d)=[e(d/4)-e(-d/4)]/(2i).

For alpha=1/4, `chi4(d)e(alpha d)=i` on odd d and zero on even d,
so its sum over a long interval is `i ell/2+O(1)`. Thus character
cancellation cannot be claimed uniformly through slopes near the
modulus-four major arcs. R is real and can meet these slopes. This
does not assert that an entire actual high-value configuration is
resonant, but it forbids deleting that complement by(F10).

Even if one grants an unproved short-interval version and favorable
minor-arc conditions on every piece, the largest permitted M in(F10)
is x^o(1). Its error is x^(1-o(1)), not x^(1-c) for fixed c>0. The prime
sum is still unestimated. Optimistically assigning each ell piece an
ell^(1-o(1)) cost and summing all D/ell pieces and K cofactors yields
`N^(-3/4)KD T^o(1)=N^(1/4)T^o(1)`, the same power as counting pairs.
For M1 a corresponding L^2 subpower price does not supply L^(3/2);
for UNBAL an LK subpower price does not supply (LK)^(3/4). These are
costs of this sufficient scalar use, not lower bounds for signed sums.
The joint masks and anchored sums would need a new signed theorem.
The scalar major-arc control does not select a new unpriced partition as
the next objective. If this decomposition is used inside the divisor
route, the full signed quantity still required is Oanch in(F4), at the
power(F5): every major-major, major-minor and minor-minor contribution,
both anchored signs, exact-zero minus terms, cofactor endpoints and all
rectangles must remain. There is no separately proved target-sized
major-arc complement in this report. The recommended distinct-class
objective(F1) does not discard such configurations at all.

**One complete selection and a genuinely sufficient family.**
For(F1), the accepted216 identity pays the entire head and repeated

    T 2^(-12) sum_(eps_j=+-1) sum_(distinct squarefree d_j>Q;s_j>=1)

A simple explicit full-goal extension criterion is the following
conditional family, offered to make availability precise, not as a
newly proved arithmetic input:

    For every fixed even p>=4, with Q_p=floor(T^(1/p)),
    I_p(T;Q_p) <= C_(p,epsilon)T^(5/4+epsilon), every real T>=2. (F13)

Together with the elementary class bounds already proved in216 and the
accepted second moment, (F13) implies `M_p(B_T)<<T^(5/4+epsilon)` for
every fixed even p. Here is the complete induction, independent of any
optimized one-pass calculation. Assume all smaller even full moments.
The actual head bound gives `||L_Qp||_infty<<Q_p^(1/4)` and hence
`M_r(L_Qp)<<T Q_p^(r/4)<=T^(5/4)` for r<=p. For odd r<=p-2 use Holder
between the surrounding smaller even full moments and the measure O(T);
for r=0,1 use the measure and second moment. Thus
`M_r(Z_Qp)<<T^(5/4+epsilon)` for every r<=p-2, by `Z=B-L`.

The exact set-partition identity at order p has nonsingleton terms
`Z^r product_i p_hi`, with hi>=2 and r+sum hi=p. The elementary bound
`sum_(d>Q) A_d^h<<Q^(1-3h/4+eta)` makes each such term at most

    T^(5/4+epsilon) Q_p^(b-3(p-r)/4),
    b<=floor((p-r)/2), so the Q exponent is <=-(p-r)/4<0.

There are finitely many partitions for each fixed p. Absolute convergence
justifies the infinite identity. Consequently the absolute repeated-class
error is O(T^(5/4+epsilon)), and the one-sided(F13) bounds the nonnegative
M_p(Z). The paid head and the power inequality then bound M_p(B). This
closes the induction. Constants may depend on p and all input epsilons
can be chosen before T tends to infinity.

Restoring physical amplitude T^(p/2), squared-radius Jacobian T, and
sharp-error moment T^(2+p/2) yields the accepted pointwise bridge

    Theta(p)=[p/4+(1+max(1,E(p)))/2]/(p+1).

For E(p)=5/4 it is `1/4+7/[8(p+1)]`, which tends to1/4. No p depending
on T is needed. Thus(F13) is an actual sufficient full-quarter family.
It requires new distinct estimates at EVERY displayed p AND its smaller
cutoff T^(1/p). There is no deduction of(F13) from(F1), no claim that the
physical lower moments follow for arbitrary masks, and no reuse of the
old beta-ceiling as though it were the improved inductive hypothesis.
Even a bound only for an unspecified unbounded subsequence of I_p would
not justify this particular induction without its intermediate orders.
The coarse criterion is not asserted optimal; the isolated task owns the
