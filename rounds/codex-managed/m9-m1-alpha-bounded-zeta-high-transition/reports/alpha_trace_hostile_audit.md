# Hostile audit of the alpha-bounded zeta-high trace

## 1. Result

The proposed pure-cosine physicalization is not a proof of the alpha trace. There is an exact but sharply scoped distributional return:

\[
 {1\over 2\pi i}\int_{(c)}X_\zeta(A)\zeta(A)Y^{-A}\,dA
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{n\geq1}\delta(Y-n),&c<0,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle\sum_{n\geq1}\delta(Y-n)-1
 =2\sum_{h\geq1}\cos(2\pi hY),&c>0,
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{49.1}
\]

where the identities are understood after pairing with a compactly supported smooth test function on \((0,\infty)\). The difference between the two lines is exactly the residue \(-1\) at \(A=0\). It is not a second endpoint term.

The actual alpha share is not the unmasked left side of (49.1). Its hierarchical weight is

\[
 \Theta_\alpha(\alpha,\beta)=(1-\psi(\beta))\psi(\alpha),       \tag{49.2}
\]

so multiplication in the high \(A\)-height produces a logarithmic-Mellin convolution of the comb and creates nonzero Cauchy--Pompeiu/strip-edge connectors. At \(A=0\), \(\beta=0\), and \(\Theta_\alpha\), all its first connector derivatives, and its mixed connector derivative vanish because \(\psi\equiv1\) near zero. Thus the alpha share owns no new \(A=0\) residue; that residue is already in the accepted unmasked/R1 ledger. Using the pure comb for the alpha share, or attaching another R1 subtraction to it, is false ownership.

Even the unmasked comb gives no saving. Its integer lattice is coherent for the unsigned high coefficients and, after the remaining low-factor inversion, can only return a projection of the original active M1 angular operator. Only the complete sum of all mask shares and connectors can return the unmasked operator. Hence this is an equivalence-hard return/no-go result, not the required normalized \(O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)\) estimate.

## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses

Assume the accepted finite reflected vector identity and put

\[
 A=s-z/2,\qquad B=s+z/2,\qquad
 \beta=\Im A,\quad \alpha=\Im B.
\]

Let \(\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) be the fixed hierarchical cutoff, with \(\psi=1\) on a neighbourhood of zero. Work first on the common finite \((u,v,s)\) antecedent, after the endpoint and recombined R1 modules have been routed globally but before any outside-height limit. Retain the terminal \(R_{1,v}(1-s)\) density, actual scale profiles, \(H_j+1\) floors, stars, characters, collision convention, and the single external factor \(X^{1/4}\).

On this antecedent the alpha arithmetic factor is exactly

\[
 X_\zeta(A)\zeta(A)L(1-B,\chi_4)=\zeta(1-A)L(1-B,\chi_4).       \tag{49.3}
\]

The connector-completed finite alpha operator means the terminal integral weighted by (49.2), every upper-minus-lower outside side, the \(u=0\) top, \(v=0\) height residue and their joint corner, and every first and mixed Cauchy--Green connector generated by (49.2). In diagonal coordinates

\[
 \alpha=t+{\mu+\nu\over2},\qquad
 \beta=t-{\mu+\nu\over2},
\]

an outside \(u\)- or \(v\)-height derivative acts on the mask as

\[
 D={1\over2}(\partial_\alpha-\partial_\beta).
\]

Consequently the required first and product connectors contain, with the orientation factors dictated by the finite Cauchy--Green formula,

\[
 D\Theta_\alpha={1\over2}\bigl((1-\psi(\beta))\psi'(\alpha)
                    +\psi'(\beta)\psi(\alpha)\bigr),           \tag{49.4}
\]

\[
 D^2\Theta_\alpha={1\over4}\bigl((1-\psi(\beta))\psi''(\alpha)
 +2\psi'(\beta)\psi'(\alpha)-\psi''(\beta)\psi(\alpha)\bigr). \tag{49.5}
\]

No assertion below suppresses these connectors or the finite sides. The physical target is \(O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)\) before, and \(O_\varepsilon(X^{1/4+\varepsilon})\) after, the one external normalization.

## 3. Proof or derivation

The branch orientation follows immediately from the accepted one-factor identity: bounded \(\alpha=\Im B\) leaves \(\zeta(A)\) at high height and therefore leaves unsigned coefficients \(h^{-A}\). There is no \(\chi_4(h)\) in this high series.

For (49.1), let \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)\). On a line \(c<0\), the series

\[
 \zeta(1-A)=\sum_{n\geq1}n^{A-1}
\]

is absolutely convergent. Pairing the inverse Mellin integral with \(\varphi\), interchanging the now absolutely convergent sum, and applying Mellin inversion gives \(\sum_{n\geq1}\varphi(n)\). Moving the \(A\)-line from \(c<0\) to \(c>0\) crosses only \(A=0\), because \(A=1\) is removable in the recombined product. Since

\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{A=0}\zeta(1-A)=-1,                       \tag{49.6}
\]

the right-line inverse is the positive Dirac comb minus the constant function one. Finally,

\[
 X_\zeta(A)=2(2\pi)^{-A}\Gamma(A)\cos(\pi A/2),
\]

whose inverse Mellin kernel is \(2\cos(2\pi Y)\); Poisson summation against \(\varphi\) gives the last equality in (49.1). This proof also shows why directly expanding \(\zeta(A)\) and directly invoking the cosine inversion is not an absolute interchange: their ordinary convergence strips do not overlap. The equality is distributional and residue-sensitive.

Now insert (49.2). Multiplication by \(1-\psi(\beta)\) in Mellin height is a high-pass multiplier, hence becomes convolution in \(\log Y\) after inversion. It does not preserve each delta mass of (49.1). Moreover the multiplier is nonholomorphic as a function of \(A\). A finite horizontal displacement therefore gives the appropriate Cauchy--Pompeiu area term containing (49.4), and two outside transfers give (49.5), in addition to the oriented sides and axes. A sharp mask gives the corresponding two strip-edge integrals. Thus the pure Dirac comb is available only after unmasking, or after summing a complete mask partition whose first and mixed derivatives cancel.

At \(A=0\), one has \(\beta=0\). Flatness of \(\psi\) near zero gives

\[
 \Theta_\alpha= D\Theta_\alpha=D^2\Theta_\alpha=0.             \tag{49.7}
\]

So shifting the connector-completed alpha share across \(A=0\) produces no alpha residue. The \(-1\) in the unmasked right-line formula is the same \(A=0\) share already reconciled with R1; assigning it to alpha and then retaining the accepted R1 module counts it twice.

The hierarchy satisfies

\[
 \psi(\beta)+(1-\psi(\beta))\psi(\alpha)
 +(1-\psi(\beta))(1-\psi(\alpha))=1.                           \tag{49.8}
\]

Only after all three shares are placed on the same finite antecedent do their first and mixed connector densities cancel and the unmasked comb return. The alpha share alone still has the bounded-\(B\) projection \(\psi(\alpha)L(1-B,\chi_4)\). Therefore its lattice is not literally the original M1 sum. Removing the \(B\)-mask and carrying out the remaining inversion can only return the original active M1 terminal operator, since (49.3) is an identity and (49.8) is one. In the accepted graph this is the GAR angular-divisor aggregate with globally routed boundary modules kept outside. Calling it merely “GAR minus the upper endpoint” is incomplete: the lower endpoint and the recombined R1 arithmetic module also have separate owners. A row-by-row equality between the projected alpha lattice and the displayed GAR coefficient \(C_X^*(n)\) has not been proved here; the rigorous conclusion is an equivalence-hard return, not a new estimate.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first unproved step is commuting the masked distributional inversion with the signed hard-top limit and the outside-height limit. The top variable is

\[
 \mu=\alpha-\beta-\nu,
\]

so the \(u^{-1}\) factor couples the high \(\beta\)-height to the bounded-\(\alpha\) mask and to the \(v\)-height. Its physical limit must be formed on the original finite box as a delta-plus-principal-value distribution before absolute values. No accepted result shows that the resulting actual profile, with floors, stars, radial \(R_1\), connectors, sides, axes and corner, is a uniform test family for (49.1), nor that the infinite cosine series may be exchanged with this Plemelj limit.

Even granting that exchange, the first sharp absolute-capacity obstruction remains. On the terminal line \(\Re s=c'>1+\Re z/2\),

\[
 |X_\zeta(A)|\asymp (1+|\beta|)^{c'-\Re z/2-1/2}.
\]

The hard top supplies only one inverse high-height factor, so integration to height \(U\) has capacity

\[
 U^{c'-1/2-\Re z/2},                                          \tag{49.9}
\]

whose exponent is greater than \(1/2\). The signed connector-completed operator must recover this entire positive power uniformly. Neither the comb identity nor the accepted radial side removal supplies it. The noncompact factor \(1-\psi(\beta)\) also means that compact-beta exact side annihilation is unavailable; the finite outside \(u,v,s\) sides remain until a separate signed reconciliation is proved.

## 5. Control tests and outcomes

- **Branch orientation — pass.** Bounded \(\alpha\) leaves the high unsigned zeta series; character cancellation belongs only to the low \(B\)-factor and cannot be transferred coefficientwise to \(h\).
- **Common antecedent and mask connectors — proposed side-free formula fails.** Equations (49.4)--(49.5) are nonzero on the transition collars. With horizontal sides oriented left-to-right, every finite transfer retains upper minus lower sides, connectors, axes, and the corner.
- **Pole and residue ledger — pass only with zero alpha ownership.** \(A=1\) is removable. The actual arithmetic pole is \(A=0\), with residue \(-1\); (49.7) assigns none of it to alpha. The artificial radial pole \(\rho=0\) is separate.
- **Signed height order — open.** Taking absolute values or the infinite \(h\)-sum before the symmetric top principal value loses the only possible high-height cancellation. No uniform interchange theorem is available.
- **Integer-cosine resonance — falsifies coefficientwise cancellation.** For every integer \(Y=N\) and every \(H\geq1\),
  \[
    \sum_{h=1}^{H}\cos(2\pi hN)=H.                             \tag{49.10}
  \]
  Thus the cosine kernel concentrates the high series on a lattice rather than cancelling it.
- **Unsigned coefficient control — fails.** The adversary \(a_h=1\) is already the sign pattern of the zeta coefficients and attains (49.10). This is a falsifier of an \(h\)-wise saving, not a lower bound for the fully coupled low-factor/radial operator.
- **Radial and external normalization — no hidden gain.** The accepted endpoint and R1 modules are routed once; the remaining terminal \(R_1\), profiles, floors, stars and scale sums must stay in the alpha operator. Any normalized loss \(X^\delta\) becomes \(X^{1/4+\delta}\) after the single external factor.
- **Outside-height limit — open.** The accepted renormalized radial-side theorem removes only its declared radial remainder under nested exhaustion. It neither removes the alpha outside-height faces nor overcomes (49.9).
- **Downstream scope — no implication.** A comb/projection identity proves neither the alpha estimate nor the swept operator, post-FE vector kernel, M9-M1, M9, or the Gauss-circle target.

No numerical test was used. No external source was invoked: the only source-like ingredient is the elementary Mellin/Poisson distribution identity proved above, and no literature theorem is claimed to apply to the masked physical operator.
