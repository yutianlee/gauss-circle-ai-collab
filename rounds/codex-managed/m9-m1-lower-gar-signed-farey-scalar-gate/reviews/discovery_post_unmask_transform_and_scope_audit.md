## 2. Exact full-scalar partition and adjusted-carrier packing

At every literal reduced sample put

\[
 c_{a,b}={L_\chi(y/b)\over a}
 V_{\rm low}(4R^2a^2/b^2),\qquad
 \phi_{a,b}={Na\over b}+{b-1\over4}\pmod1.
\tag{138.U2}
\]

The sample cutoff is one because \(ya/b\geq1\), and
\(\chi_4(b)=e((b-1)/4)\), so

\[
 \mathcal F_N=\sum_{a,b}c_{a,b}e(\phi_{a,b}),\qquad
 |c_{a,b}|\ll a^{-1},\qquad a\ll b/R.
\tag{138.U3}
\]

This identity is full-scalar for the integerized cone
\(\mathcal B_{\rm flat}^{(N)}(X)\).  It is not a termwise identity with
the original \(X\)-phase cone \(\mathcal B_{\rm flat}^{+}(X)\); the
accepted Round-121 seam is
\(\mathcal B_{\rm flat}^{+}(X)=\mathcal B_{\rm flat}^{(N)}(X)+O(R)\),
which is target-safe.  The link to the \((d,h)\)-cone also uses the
literal certified small positive arc \(h<d\); the numerical inequality
\(h/d<C/R\) alone gives that containment only asymptotically.  The strict
Farey reduction itself is an identity for the frozen integerized scalar
(138.B4) for every real \(X\), with \(N=\lfloor X\rfloor\),
\(y=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\), and the conjugate frequency sign retained.

Let

\[
 H_b=\sum_a|c_{a,b}|\ll\log(2+b/R).
\tag{138.U4}
\]

The whole same-denominator owner is the nonnegative row square

\[
 \mathcal S_{\rm row}
 =\sum_b\left|\sum_ac_{a,b}e(\phi_{a,b})\right|^2
 \leq\sum_bH_b^2\ll y\log^2(2X).
\tag{138.U5}
\]

It contains both the \(O(y)\) equality diagonal and every
same-denominator unequal-numerator pair.  No modulus is taken on any
surviving row.

For the unadjusted phase \(\theta=Na/b\), write

\[
 g=(N,b),\qquad q=b/g.
\]

Then \(g\mid N\), \((N/g,q)=1\), and the standing odd-denominator
condition forces both \(q\) and \(g\) to be odd.  A fixed reduced phase
\(u/q\) forces

\[
 b=qg,\qquad q,g\ \text{odd},\qquad
 a\equiv u(N/g)^{-1}\pmod q.
\tag{138.U6}
\]

The primitive and profile conditions only shorten this progression.
Including \(q=1\), which contains every divisor denominator \(b\mid N\),
one phase fibre has mass

\[
 M_\theta\ll\tau(N)\log(2X).
\tag{138.U7}
\]

A physical \(\phi\)-fibre is the union of at most two restricted
\(\theta\)-fibres, one unshifted for \(b\equiv1\pmod4\) and one shifted
by \(1/2\) for \(b\equiv3\pmod4\).  Hence

\[
 M_\phi^{\max}\leq2M_\theta^{\max},\qquad
 \sum_zM_\phi(z)\ll y\log(2X).
\tag{138.U8}
\]

Every distinct \(\phi\)-value is a reduced rational with denominator at
most \(2y\).  Its circular separation from another distinct value is at
least \((4y^2)^{-1}\).  A circular arc of radius \(\delta\) therefore
contains at most

\[
 1+8\delta y^2
\tag{138.U9}
\]

distinct carrier values.  Equations (138.U7)--(138.U9) give the explicit
ordered-pair packing bound

\[
 \sum_{\|z-z'\|\leq\delta}M_\phi(z)M_\phi(z')
 \ll y\tau(N)\log^2(2X)(1+8\delta y^2).
\tag{138.U10}
\]

Thus \(\delta=y^{-2}\), as in both the discovery report and the current
clean conductor candidate, is target-safe.  More generally, for a
requested final \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing fixed
\(0<\eta<\varepsilon/4\) and \(\delta=X^\eta/y^2\) would still be
target-safe after assigning separate \(X^{\varepsilon/4}\) allowances to
\(\tau(N)\) and the logarithms, but the current candidate makes no need
of that optional enlargement.

The hostile exact-collision partition is a coarser valid reduction.  The
current conductor candidate and the discovery report both delete the
union of the ordinary and physical \(y^{-2}\)-collars.  For the ordinary
\(\theta\)-collar the denominator bound is \(y\), so its packing constant
is \(1+2\delta y^2\).  Taking the union costs only another absolute
constant.  Therefore the common conductor/discovery identity

\[
 |\mathcal F_N|^2=\mathfrak S_N+O_\varepsilon(yX^\varepsilon)
\tag{138.U11}
\]

is a valid full-scalar strict reduction and is the correct canonical
candidate statement.

## 3. Determinant fibres and fourth-power packet scope

For \(b=Gr\), \(b'=Gs\), \((r,s)=1\), one has

\[
 \Delta=G\delta,\qquad \delta=as-a'r,
 \qquad {N\Delta\over bb'}={N\delta\over Grs}.
\tag{138.U12}
\]

All solutions on one fibre are

\[
 a=a_0+rt,\qquad a'=a'_0+st,
\tag{138.U13}
\]

with \(O(1+G/R)\) allowed values after imposing both literal supports and
both primitive conditions.  The determinant phase is constant in \(t\),
and

\[
 \chi_4(Gr)\chi_4(Gs)=\chi_4(rs),
\tag{138.U14}
\]

so the common factor supplies no character oscillation either.

The partial fraction

\[
 {1\over aa'}={1\over\delta}\left({s\over a'}-{r\over a}\right)
\tag{138.U15}
\]

does not give a uniform \(1/|\delta|\) gain.  If
\(a_{\min},a'_{\min}\) and \(A,A'\) are the positive endpoints of the
fibre, the canonical safe estimate is

\[
 \sum_t{1\over a_ta'_t}
 \ll {1\over|\delta|}
 \left{
 {r\over a_{\min}}+{s\over a'_{\min}}
 +\log\!\left(2+{A\over a_{\min}}\right)
 +\log\!\left(2+{A'\over a'_{\min}}\right)
 \right}.
\tag{138.U16}
\]

The discovery report's coarser bound with numerator
\(r+s+\log(2+G/R)\) is valid because \(r+s\) absorbs the omitted
\(\log r,\log s\) endpoint aspects.  To see that the aspect cannot be
removed at the reciprocal-kernel level, take any odd \(S>1\), put
\(r=1,s=S,a=a'=1,\delta=S-1\), and choose odd \(G\gg R\) with
\(GS\leq y\).  With \(G\asymp R^{3/2}\), \(S\asymp R^{1/2}\), the
one-point kernel remains one while \(\log(2X)/(S-1)\to0\).  This is an
algebraic counterexample requiring no profile lower envelope, not a
physical lower bound.
Most safely, \(\delta\) merely partitions one fixed denominator block,
so

\[
 \sum_{\delta\ne0}|\mathcal A_{G,r,s,\delta}|
 \leq H_{Gr}H_{Gs}\ll\log^2(2X).
\tag{138.U17}
\]

Summing (138.U17) over denominator pairs returns \(y^{2+o(1)}\), not the
target \(y^{1+o(1)}\).  Determinant fibres are therefore a correct exact
dictionary and a correct obstruction to fibrewise modulus, but are not
part of the proof of (138.U11).

At a fourth-power centre \(X=N=M^4\), \(R=M\), \(y=M^2\), with \(M\)
odd, the direct Farey packet

\[
 a=1,\qquad b_u=y-4u,\qquad 1\leq u\leq cM,
\tag{138.U18}
\]

has \(b_u\equiv1\pmod4\), \(L_\chi(y/b_u)=1\), and

\[
 {N\over b_u}=y+4u+{16u^2\over y-4u}.
\tag{138.U19}
\]

For small fixed \(c\), the fractional parts occupy one short sector and
are distinct.  Their consecutive separations are \(\gg y^{-1}\), hence
far larger than the microscopic collar.  If the literal normalization
\(V_{\rm low}=1\) near zero is invoked, each coefficient in (138.U18) is
exactly one and the packet has internal cross-pair capacity \(\asymp R^2\),
exactly the square target.  Without that explicit normalization, the
word “exactly” must be replaced by the literal profile value, and a lower
capacity assertion requires a fixed nonzero lower envelope.  This packet
is not a lower bound for \(\mathcal F_N\), because all exterior terms may
cancel it.

The discovery report's \(R^{3/2}\) half-integer tube capacity is a
different control.  It arises only after exactly unaggregating the lifts,
pairing adjacent odd \(d\)'s, partitioning the paired kernel into
stationary tubes, and then summing tube moduli.  It is physical rather
than adversarial, but it is neither a signed lower bound nor an additional
full-scalar deletion.  The two fourth-power tests are therefore
consistent: (138.U18) gives target-scale capacity directly inside the
Farey survivor, while the paired stationary packets show why a later
tube-wise modulus costs \(R^{1/2}\) beyond the scalar target.

## 4. Character-Poisson normalization and hard endpoint ownership

For fixed \(h\), put

\[
 a_h(x)={1\over h}V_{\rm low}(4R^2h^2/x^2).
\]

Using

\[
 \chi_4(d)={e(d/4)-e(-d/4)\over2i}
\tag{138.U20}
\]

and symmetric Poisson summation of the hard interval gives the complete
formula

\[
 \begin{split}
 \sum_{d\leq y}\chi_4(d)a_h(d)e(Nh/d)
 ={}&{1\over2}\chi_4(y)a_h(y)e(Nh/y)\\
 &+{1\over2i}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\sigma
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\sum_{|k|\leq K}
 \int_0^y a_h(x)
 e\!\left({Nh\over x}+(\sigma/4-k)x\right)dx.
 \end{split}
\tag{138.U21}
\]

The half-endpoint term is required because the symmetric Fourier series
assigns half weight at the jump.  Its complete \(h\)-sum is
\(O(\log(2X))\), since the literal profile leaves only \(h\ll R\).

For an interior stationary mode let

\[
 r=\sigma-4k>0.
\]

Then \(r\) is odd, \(r\equiv\sigma\pmod4\), and
\(\sigma=\chi_4(r)\).  The phase satisfies

\[
 x_*=2\sqrt{Nh/r},\qquad
 \Phi(x_*)=\sqrt{Nhr},\qquad
 \Phi''(x_*)={r^{3/2}\over4(Nh)^{1/2}}.
\tag{138.U22}
\]

Multiplying the branch factor \(\chi_4(r)/(2i)\), the Gaussian factor
\(e(1/8)\), the reciprocal curvature
\(2(Nh)^{1/4}r^{-3/4}\), and the already included \(1/h\), gives

\[
 e(-1/8)N^{1/4}\chi_4(r)(hr)^{-3/4}
 V_{\rm low}(R^2hr/N)e(\sqrt{Nhr}).
\tag{138.U23}
\]

This agrees exactly with the blind and discovery conventions by
(138.U1).  Both congruence branches and the literal profile argument are
correct.

For a fixed smooth interior \(d/y\)-window, \(r\asymp h\), and the
principal modewise capacity is \(R^{3/2}\).  Across all possible
stationary windows, the hard endpoint and lower profile support give

\[
 r\gg h,\qquad hr\ll N/R^2\asymp y,
\]

and hence the slightly larger complete-principal ledger

\[
 N^{1/4}\sum_{h\ll R}h^{-3/4}
 \sum_{h\ll r\ll y/h}r^{-3/4}
 \ll R^{3/2}\log(2X).
\tag{138.U24}
\]

The clean family (138.U23) is not the complete scalar.  The exact owners
outside it are:

- the explicit hard endpoint in (138.U21);
- the bounded incomplete-Fresnel transition when \(x_*\) crosses
  \(d=y\), rather than a sharp stationary indicator;
- every \(r\leq0\) and other nonstationary Poisson integral;
- stationary points entering or leaving either end of a smooth block;
- the lower profile/support crossings near \(d\asymp Rh\);
- all stationary remainders outside a certified uniform interior range;
- the complement of any inserted smooth interior cutoff;
- small heights, both odd-lattice branches, the zero class, the floors
  \(N,y\), simultaneous dependence on \(R\), and the conjugate scalar
  sign;
- if one starts from the Farey rather than the \((d,h)\) form, the exact
  staircase \(L_\chi(y/b)\), primitive-numerator restrictions, and their
  lift reassembly.

The discovery report explicitly retained these owners and claimed only
an interior-principal formula.  The hostile complete formula (138.U21)
confirms that this is the correct scope.

## 5. Principal radical bound and Legendre self-return

Write uniquely

\[
 N=Ds^2,\qquad D=\operatorname{sf}(N)\ \text{squarefree}.
\tag{138.U25}
\]

Inside the stationary principal family,

\[
 e(\sqrt{Nhr})=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Nhr\ \text{is a square}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 hr=Dt^2.
\tag{138.U26}
\]

Thus there is one squarefree-radical channel, not one independent channel
for every divisor.  If \(A_{h,r}\) denotes either a compact interior
weight or a correctly uniformized incomplete-Fresnel endpoint weight,
then \(|A_{h,r}|\ll1\).  The profile forces \(Dt^2\ll N/R^2\asymp R^2\),
and the number of factorizations \(hr=Dt^2\) is at most \(\tau(Dt^2)\).
Consequently its entire principal absolute mass is

\[
 \begin{split}
 &N^{1/4}\sum_{Dt^2\ll R^2}(Dt^2)^{-3/4}\tau(Dt^2)\\
 &\hspace{20mm}\ll_\varepsilon
 RD^{-3/4}X^\varepsilon
 \sum_{t\geq1}t^{-3/2}
 \ll_\varepsilon RX^\varepsilon.
 \end{split}
\tag{138.U27}
\]

For \(D=1\) this contains the exact square channel at fourth-power
centres.  It is target-safe as an upper control and may saturate the
target through individual modes when the literal profile is nonzero near
zero.  It is not a lower bound and does not control near radicals,
nonsquare principal modes, hard endpoints, transitions, nonstationary
modes, or remainders.  Therefore it is not part of the full-scalar strict
reduction.

The phase self-return is exact.  For \(A=Nh\), the first stationary
Legendre step sends

\[
 {A\over x}+\rho x
 \quad\longmapsto\quad 2\sqrt{A\rho}.
\]

The reverse phase \(2\sqrt{A\rho}-\rho x\) has stationary point
\(\rho=A/x^2\) and stationary value \(A/x\).  Thus a second stationary
step returns the original reciprocal phase.  Grouping \(n=hr\) in
(138.U23) likewise produces an angularly truncated divisor-character
coefficient multiplying

\[
 N^{1/4}n^{-3/4}V_{\rm low}(R^2n/N)e(\sqrt{Nn}),
\tag{138.U28}
\]

the lower Hardy--Legendre radial form already under investigation.  This
certifies phase and structural self-return.  It does not certify a full
owner-complete asymptotic identity unless every owner listed after
(138.U24) is also transformed and reassembled.
