# Conductor candidate: adjacent-\(q\) transport and maximal alternation

Campaign: m9-m2-polynomial-q-maximal-alternation

## Candidate theorem

For the literal complete actual fixed-\(a\) row,

\[
 \sup_{I\subset[D,2D)}
 \left|\sum_{q\in I}(-1)^qF_a(q)\right|
 \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon {L^2\over A}. \tag{105.C1}
\]

It suffices to prove a transported variation estimate of the same scale,
but (105.C1) may also follow from joint oscillation even if total
variation fails.

## Exact Gram implication

Take \(H\asymp D\). The maximal theorem bounds each zero-extended window
by \(X^\varepsilon L^2/A\). There are \(O(A)\) bases and \(O(D)\)
windows, so

\[
 \mathcal G_H^{\rm act}
 \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon {DL^4\over A}
 \asymp X^\varepsilon {H^2L^4\over AD}.
\]

Thus the candidate closes the complete polynomial primitive-ray Gram,
not merely one fixed row.

At fixed \(k\), the complete centered phase obeys

\[
 \partial_q e(gk(y-r_{q,k})^2)
 =-r_{q,k}'\partial_y e(gk(y-r_{q,k})^2).
\]

The saddle moves monotonically through the physical interval. A lawful
proof would transport the entire actual coefficient from \(q\) to
\(q+1\), align the moving integer \(k\)-samples, and keep the complete
metric phase

\[
 {q\over2}+\left(\nu-{g\over2}\right){\Lambda_q\over k}.
\]

Primitivity must be expanded exactly, not smoothed. All prior owner
complements must remain one-count.

## False analogue

The coefficient array \(F_a(q)=(-1)^qL^2/A\) saturates the maximal sum
and violates (105.C1) by a factor \(D\). Hence pointwise row size,
formal parity, and coefficient-uniform transport cannot prove the
candidate.

This is not an actual-symbol counterexample. A hostile task must decide
whether the literal centered coefficient can realize enough of this
coherence to obstruct (105.C1).
