### 2.2 Squarefree and smooth Kloosterman sums

Primary source: Xuancheng Shao, Igor E. Shparlinski and Laurence P. Wijaya,
[*Sums of Kloosterman sums over square-free and smooth integers*,
arXiv:2411.12113v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12113), submitted
2024-11-18; [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12113v1).

For prime (p) and integer (d\ge2), the paper defines the normalized
finite-field hyper-Kloosterman trace

\[
 {\cal K}_{d,p}(n)=p^{-(d-1)/2}
 \sum_{x_1\cdots x_d\equiv n\ (p)}e_p(x_1+\cdots+x_d)
\]

and

\[
 Q_{d,p}(N)=\sum_{n\le N}|\mu(n)|{\cal K}_{d,p}(n).
\]

Theorem 1.1 assumes (d\ge2), an even positive integer (ell), a prime
(p), and (p^{1/2+2/\ell}\le N\le p), and gives

\[
 |Q_{d,p}(N)|\le
 N^{1/2}p^{1/4}
 \left(\frac{p^{1/2+2/\ell}}N\right)^{1/[2(4\ell-3)]}p^{o(1)}.
\tag{182.4}
\]

In particular, (N\ge p^{1/2+\varepsilon}) gives
(Q_{d,p}(N)\ll N^{1/2}p^{1/4+o(1)}); at (N=p), (ell=8), the printed
specialization is (p^{3/4-1/232+o(1)}).

This last exponent is numerically sufficient for (182.3) only after the
fictional identifications (N=p) and (p\asymp L^2), unit/normalized
coefficient, and no
(t)- or endpoint cost:

\[
 p^{3/4-1/232+o(1)}\asymp L^{3/2-1/116+o(1)}.
\]

The first failed hypothesis is not power but kernel/modulus. There is no
prime finite-field modulus (p) in (182.3), and
(C_{L,X}^{\sigma}(st^2)e(\sigma t\sqrt{Xs})) is not
({\cal K}_{d,p}(s)). Lemma 2.3 permits bounded outer weights in a Type-I
sum ({\cal K}_{d,p}(m^rn)), but still has one fixed prime, finite-field
trace, interval conditions, and no literal joint coefficient; it does not
repair that failure.

For (y)-smooth (friable) integers, Theorem 1.3 assumes (p) prime,
(N\ge p^{1/2}), (y\ge\log N), and states

\[
 |R_{d,p}(N,y)|\le
 \Psi(N,y)y^{1/2}p^\beta N^{-\gamma+o(1)},
 \quad
 \beta=\frac1{4(1+\alpha(N,y))},\quad
 \gamma=\frac{\alpha(N,y)^2}{2(1+\alpha(N,y))}.
\tag{182.5}
\]

Its first project failure is again the finite-field Kloosterman kernel.
Independently, its “smooth” hypothesis (P(n)\le y) is unrelated to the
smooth partition/profile defining the direct smooth-`M1` parent. Therefore
Theorem 1.3 supplies neither the hard small-(t) theorem nor the smooth
direct-`M1` theorem.

Primary source: Peng Gao,
[*On the Gauss circle problem over smooth numbers*,
arXiv:2604.23918v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23918). Theorems 1.1--1.2
give saddle-point asymptotics for the count
(\Psi_G(x,y)=\sum_{n\le x,\,P(n)\le y}r(n)) in specified friability
ranges, including \((\log\log y)^2\le u=\log x/\log y\) in Theorem 1.1
and \((\log x)^{2+\varepsilon_0}<y<x\) in Theorem 1.2. These are main-term
counting theorems for friable integers, not oscillatory discrepancy bounds
for unrestricted (P(X)), and “smooth” again has the wrong meaning. No
project owner imports.

### 2.3 Square-root moments and exponent pairs

Primary source: Yixiu Xiao,
[*Moment Estimates and Discrepancy for Sums of Square Roots Modulo One*,
arXiv:2606.28986v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28986), submitted
2026-06-27; [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28986v1).
For

\[
 S(h,n)=\sum_{n/2\le a\le n}e(h\sqrt a),

Theorem 1.1 assumes fixed (delta>0) and
(H\ge n^{1/2+\delta}) and proves

\[
 \sum_{h\sim H}|S(h,n)|^2\ll_{\varepsilon,\delta}Hn^{1+\varepsilon}.
\tag{182.6}
\]

Theorem 1.2 assumes fixed (0<\delta<1/6) and
(n^{1/2+\delta}\le H\le n^{2/3}), and gives the fourth moment

\[
 \sum_{h\sim H}|S(h,n)|^4\ll_{\varepsilon,\delta}Hn^{2+\varepsilon}.
\tag{182.7}
\]

Theorem 1.3 derives a discrepancy estimate using (182.6) together with
separate pointwise exponential-sum estimates.  For a fixed project
multiplier (t), the formal map is

\[
 n_{\rm Xiao}\asymp\frac{L^2}{t^2},\qquad
 h=t\sqrt X,\qquad H\asymp t\sqrt X.
\]

For general real (X), (h) is not an integer, so there is no literal source
map.  Even on the diagnostic square subfamily (X=J^2), the critical hard-M1
shell (L\asymp X^{1/6}) lies outside the upper range of Theorem 1.2 at
(t=1), since that theorem would require

\[
 \sqrt X\le (L^2)^{2/3}=L^{4/3}\asymp X^{2/9},
\]

which is false.  Theorem 1.1 is in range, but positivity isolates a single
frequency only at the non-saving scale

\[
 |S(tJ,n_{\rm Xiao})|
 \le (Hn_{\rm Xiao}^{1+\varepsilon})^{1/2}
 \ll X^{1/4}L t^{-1/2}X^\varepsilon.
\]

At (t=1) and (L\asymp X^{1/6}), this is
(X^{5/12+\varepsilon}), worse even than the raw
(L^2=X^{1/3}) capacity and far above the
(L^{3/2}=X^{1/4}) target.  The project also has a sparse fixed-(J)
frequency section rather than the complete dense (h\sim H) family.  Finally,
the source coefficient is (1), whereas (182.3) has
(C_{L,X}^{\sigma}(st^2)\mu^2(s)) with hard zero extension.  Thus the exact
failures are the integer/frequency-family and fourth-moment-range
quantifiers, the non-saving second-moment consequence, and then the
coefficient and endpoint class.

## 3. Proof or derivation

### 3.1 Audit procedure

Every candidate theorem was tested in the following order:

1. current version, theorem status, theorem number, and publication record;
2. pointwise versus averaged, almost-all, smoothed, or conditional
