## Exact parabola Fourier support

For one shell, set
\[
 \xi_n=\sqrt{n/(4N)},\quad v_n=(\xi_n,\xi_n^2),\quad
 y_0=(2R\sqrt N,0),
\]
and choose the target's fixed Schwartz envelope \(\eta\), with
\(\eta(0)=1\) and Fourier support in a sufficiently small fixed ball.
Then
\[
 F(y)=\eta((y-y_0)/N)\sum_n a_ne(v_n\cdot y),\qquad
 F(y_0)=\sum_n a_ne(R\sqrt n).                            \tag{R3}
\]
The second equality is exact, since \(v_n\cdot y_0=R\sqrt n\).
The frequencies stay in a fixed compact interior part of the parabola.
For the summand \(F_n(y)=\eta((y-y_0)/N)e(v_n\cdot y)\),
\[
 \widehat F_n(\zeta)
   =N^2 e(-y_0\cdot(\zeta-v_n))\widehat\eta(N(\zeta-v_n)).
                                                               \tag{R4}
\]
If \(\zeta=v_n+u/N\), the vertical displacement from the parabola is
\[
 \zeta_2-\zeta_1^2=(u_2-2\xi_nu_1)/N-u_1^2/N^2.
\]
A sufficiently small fixed Fourier support for \(\eta\) puts this
inside the literal \(N^{-1}\) neighborhood. Moreover
\(\xi'(x)=1/(4\sqrt{Nx})\asymp N^{-1}\) on the shell, so the
bumps in (R4) are disjoint after another fixed reduction of that
support. Large \(y_0\) changes only the unit phase in (R4); there is
no radius derivative cost.

## Canonical caps and smooth projection norms

Choose the source-compatible smooth partition \(\chi_\theta\),
uniformly bounded, with bounded overlap and tangential intervals
of length comparable to \(N^{-1/2}\). Write
\[
 F_\theta=\mathcal F^{-1}(\chi_\theta\widehat F),\quad
 {\cal I}_\theta=\{n:\operatorname{supp}\widehat F_n
                        \cap\operatorname{supp}\chi_\theta\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
By the spacing above, \(\#{\cal I}_\theta\ll\sqrt N\), and each
index belongs to \(O(1)\) such sets. All implied constants are fixed.
These statements include bumps split by a smooth cap boundary.

Put \(G=(\sum_\theta|F_\theta|^2)^{1/2}\). Plancherel, bounded
overlap, and the disjoint small bumps prove
\[
 \|G\|_2^2
 =\int\sum_\theta|\chi_\theta(\zeta)|^2|\widehat F(\zeta)|^2\,d\zeta
 \ll N^2\|\eta\|_2^2\sum_n|a_n|^2.                     \tag{R5}
\]
Thus \(\|G\|_2\ll N\|a\|_2\). The dimension-two envelope contributes
\(N\) to the norm, as claimed.

For the infinity norm, it is unnecessary to assume a cost-free
multiplier bound on a large function. Directly from (R4),
\[
 \|\mathcal F^{-1}(\chi_\theta\widehat F_n)\|_\infty
 \le \|\chi_\theta\widehat F_n\|_1
 \ll \|\widehat\eta\|_1,
\]
uniformly in \(n,\theta,N,y_0\). Therefore at every \(y\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 G(y)^2
 &\ll\sum_\theta\left(\sum_{n\in{\cal I}_\theta}|a_n|\right)^2\\
 &\le \max_\theta\#{\cal I}_\theta
             \sum_\theta\sum_{n\in{\cal I}_\theta}|a_n|^2
 \ll \sqrt N\,\|a\|_2^2 .
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{R6}
\]
This proves the target's bound for the actual smooth cap operators,
not only for an artificially disjoint sharp grouping.
Interpolating (R5)--(R6) yields
\[
 \|G\|_p\le\|G\|_2^{2/p}\|G\|_\infty^{1-2/p}
 \ll N^{1/4+3/(2p)}\|a\|_2.                             \tag{R7}
\]

## Tube constant and point evaluation

Take \(H(y)=(1+|y-y_0|^2)^{-B}\), with fixed \(B>1\).
Its total mass is a fixed finite constant and \(0<H\le1\).
For every eligible small tube \(V\subset U\), at scale \(s\),
\[
 H(V),H(U)\ll_B1,\quad |V|\asymp s^{-3},\quad |U|\asymp N^2s.
\]
The exponent \(1/p-1/4\) is nonnegative. Consequently
\[
 \kappa_{p,H}(U)
 \ll s^{3/4}(N^{-2}s^{-1})^{1/p-1/4}
 =N^{1/2-2/p}s^{1-1/p}
 \le N^{1/2-2/p}.                                      \tag{R8}
\]
The maximum includes \(s=1\). In its fixed-direction tiling, choose a
large tube near \(y_0\) and a contained unit-scale tube within bounded
distance of \(y_0\); if \(y_0\) is on a tiling boundary, move a bounded
distance inward. The bounded condition numbers at \(s=1\) make these
distances uniform. Both tubes then capture a fixed positive amount of
the decaying weight, proving the matching lower order for the maximum.
This also covers small dyadic \(N\) after a fixed change in constants.
Only the upper bound is needed for the final estimate.

All Fourier supports in (R3) lie in one fixed compact set. Choose a
fixed Schwartz kernel \(k\) with \(\widehat k=1\) on that set. The exact
identity \(F=F*k\) and weighted Holder give
\[
 |F(y_0)|\le\|F\|_{L^p(H)}
 \left(\int |k(y_0-y)|^{p'}H(y)^{-p'/p}\,dy\right)^{1/p'}.
                                                               \tag{R9}
\]
The integral is finite and independent of \(N,T,R,y_0\): after
translation its non-kernel factor has only fixed polynomial growth,
whereas \(k\) has arbitrary fixed decay. No assertion about a compact
unit ball or a missing spatial tail is required.

Combining the selected theorem, (R7)--(R9), and its \(N^{-100}\)
remainder yields
\[
 |F(y_0)|\ll N^{3/4-1/(2p)+\epsilon}\|a\|_2
       \ll A N^{1/2-1/(2p)+\epsilon}.                   \tag{R10}
\]
The last step uses the actual divisor/Schwartz coefficient bound
\(\|a\|_2\ll AN^{-1/4+\epsilon}\). The remainder is smaller throughout
\(2\le p\le4\). The exponent in (R10) increases with \(p\), so the
best of these estimates is \(AN^{1/4+\epsilon}\) at \(p=2\);
at \(p=4\) it is \(AN^{3/8+\epsilon}\). This proves (R1).
