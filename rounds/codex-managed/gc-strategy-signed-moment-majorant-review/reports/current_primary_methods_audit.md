## 3. Derivation and dated primary-source audit

### 3.1 Two potential imports, with exact arithmetic statements

Define the unnormalized kernel
\(S(a,b;c)=\sum_{x\bmod c,(x,c)=1}e((ax+b\bar x)/c)\).

**Candidate A: all moduli.** Blomer--Pascadi, [arXiv:2607.24311v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24311v1), 27 July 2026, [Theorem 1.1, (1.3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24311v1): for positive integer \(c\), interval lengths at most \(N\le c\), arbitrary complex product coefficients and \((a,c)=1\),

\[
\left|\sum_{\substack{m\in I,n\in J\\(m,n,c)=1}}
\alpha_m\beta_nS(am,n;c)\right|
\ll\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2c^{1+o(1)}
\left(\frac{N^{1/8}}{c^{3/32}}+
\frac{N^{5/16}}{c^{3/16}}+\frac{N^{2/3}}{c^{7/18}}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

At \(N\asymp\sqrt c\), this is \(\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2c^{31/32+o(1)}\). Deleting the gcd condition is certified for initial intervals \(I=J=[1,N]\), not arbitrary translates. The full unequal-length Theorem 5.5, (5.12), was checked; it has its own function \(H(M,N,c)\), and does not license assigning the critical saving to every unbalanced rectangle. The record has only v1. This source is not withdrawn.

**Candidate B: compatible factorization.** Pascadi, [arXiv:2511.08445v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08445v2), 21 June 2026, [Theorem 1.2 and Example 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08445v2): let \(c=dd'e\), \(d'\mid d\), \((d,e)=1\), and let \(f\) be the largest integer with \(f^2\mid cd\). For intervals of lengths \(\ll c^{1/2+o(1)}\), arbitrary complex product coefficients and \((a,c)=1\),

\[
\left|\sum_{\substack{m\in I,n\in J\\(m,n,c)=1}}
\alpha_m\beta_nS(am,n;c)\right|
\ll\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2c^{1+o(1)}
\left(\frac f{\min(c,d^2)}\right)^{1/6}.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(d\mid c\) and \(c/d\) is squarefree, the factor becomes \(\min(d,c/d)^{-1/6}\). In particular \(c=p^2\), or \(c=pq\) with distinct comparable primes, gives \(c^{-1/12}\). This is not an all-moduli \(c^{-1/12}\) statement. The current v2 records referee revisions and minor corrections; no later version or withdrawal appears on the record.

### 3.2 Complete project cost ledger for both candidates

Neither (3) nor (4) is an inequality for a square-root phase merely because both are oscillatory. The actual kernel in (1), with \(m=N+r,n=N\), is

\[
1_{0<m-n<R,\ 2\mid m-n}(1-(m-n)/R)
e(\sigma\sqrt X(\sqrt m-\sqrt n)).
\tag{5}
\]

Its phase is separable in these product coordinates. There is no complete sum over units modulo an identified integer \(c\). Likewise (2) has a continuous displaced Fourier kernel in a radical difference, not a modular product kernel. A Kloosterman completion or spectral transform must be derived before either candidate can be applied.

| Ledger item | What is required | What is actually established |
|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic identity | Exact complete modular kernel with every transform factor | No such identity from (1) or (2) |
| Modulus and unit | Integer \(c\), unit multiplier; candidate B's factorization additionally | No physical modulus or multiplier identified |
| Summation lengths | Actual transformed intervals in each term; unequal cases separately | Original product support does not identify transformed lengths |
| Coefficients | Product vectors, or bounded projective norm of mixed amplitude | Original rank-one norm is \(D\ll L^2X^\epsilon\); no transformed norm certificate |
| Sharp masks | At most \(J\) row intervals with all other arithmetic constraints retained | Row intervals cost logarithms; arbitrary gcd/deletion masks do not inherit that property |
| Completion/BV | Every Fourier coefficient, transform scale and sharp-trace variation | Not yet bounded; \(V_g\) remains open in the scalar alternative |
| Exceptional terms | Nonunit/gcd terms, bad factorization moduli, endpoints and tail | No bound for a transformed complement |
| Averaging | Fixed discrete modulus theorem; no hidden radius or modulus average | (1) is pointwise in real \(X\); (2) averages continuous radius only |
| Outer modulus | Sum transformed estimates with actual absolute-value placement | Cannot first replace (2) by a positive count and claim a signed gain |
| Reassembly | Return to full original \(t=1\), or full moment, with all powers | Not supplied by these theorems |

Here is the quantitative gate, so the zero certified saving is not a vague refusal to estimate. Suppose an **as-yet unproved** identity expresses \(C_{\rm even}\) as a sum of transformed bilinear terms plus an error \(E\). For term \(\nu\), denote its external scalar factor by \(k_\nu\), its mixed coefficient projective norm by \(A_\nu\), and all mask/BV costs by \(W_\nu\). Then a lawful critical-range application would give

\[
|C_{\rm even}|\ll
\sum_\nu |k_\nu|W_\nu A_\nu c_\nu^{1-\delta_\nu+o(1)}+|E|,
\tag{6}
\]

where \(\delta_\nu=1/32\) for candidate A, or is computed from (4) for B. A union of \(J\) row intervals contributes at most \(2J(1+\lceil\log_2 N\rceil)\) to \(W_\nu\), *after* the all-vector theorem is matched. Both \(A_\nu\) and \(W_\nu\) must be summed, not replaced by the number of nonempty pieces.

If \(L=X^\lambda\), all relevant \(c_\nu\asymp X^\gamma\), and the unsaved cost satisfies
\(\sum|k_\nu|W_\nu A_\nu c_\nu\ll L^3X^{a+\epsilon}\),
then (6) yields only

\[
Q_R\ll L^2X^\epsilon+L^3X^{a-\gamma\delta+\epsilon}+|E|.
\tag{7}
\]

The net correlation saving is \(X^{\gamma\delta-a}\); closing this interface requires \(\gamma\delta-a\ge\lambda\) and \(|E|\ll L^2X^\epsilon\). After square-root scalar reconstruction the saving and the deficit both halve. As a purely conditional cost check, \(c\asymp L^2\) and \(a=0\) would leave \(L^{47/16}\) under A, or \(L^{17/6}\) under the favorable B factorization, both above \(L^2\). Neither assumed modulus nor cost is asserted for the source. There is no legal positive value of \(\gamma\delta-a\) available from the present reduction.

There is also a rigorous scope restriction on a direct all-vector approach. In the common product coordinates the even-Fejer operator has norm \(R/2\) for even \(R\), and \((R+R^{-1})/2\) for odd \(R\); diagonal conjugation by the square-root phase preserves it. Therefore a reduction that keeps arbitrary vectors, uses only \(D\), and concludes an operator bound \(O(X^\epsilon)\) for every active growing \(R\) would contradict that norm. An eventual transform must use a property of the physical coefficients or a restricted transformed class. This restricts the proposed proof logic; it does not rule out (1).

The scalar alternative remains exactly

\[
|T_{\rm full}|\ll X^\epsilon
\sum_{\substack{g\ll L\\g\ {\rm odd}}}
\frac Lg V_g\log(2+L/g)B_g.
\tag{8}
\]

Here \(B_g\) is an all-vector bound for \(e(\sigma g\sqrt{Xab})\), on \(a,b\asymp L/g\). Neither candidate is a bound for that kernel. Subpower \(V_g\) and \(B_g\ll(L/g)^{1/2}X^\epsilon\) are still requirements, not consequences of (3)--(4).

times \(\sum_\gamma\|f_\gamma\|_{12}^{12}\). Theorem 2 is an amplitude-restricted wave-envelope superlevel inequality, still with three-dimensional measure. These are not fixed-axis radius moment estimates.

A valid cone application must localize this lift, derive its scale, control cap amplitudes, and pay to recover the central trace. An integral over three-dimensional space alone controls no prescribed line.
