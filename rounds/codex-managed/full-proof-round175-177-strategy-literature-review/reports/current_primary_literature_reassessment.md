### 2.2 Shen: the closest new inverse-residue result

Primary source: Qixiang Shen, *A problem of D. H. Lehmer in short
intervals. II*, [arXiv:2607.06575v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06575),
submitted 2026-07-02; [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06575v1).

Theorem 1 assumes an **odd prime** modulus \(q\),
\(1\le N\le q-1\), \(0<\delta<1/4\), and
\(N\ge q^{1/2}\exp(- (\log q)^{1/2-2\delta})\), and proves an asymptotic
for the parity-versus-multiplicative-inverse count.  It is not itself a
weighted exponential-sum theorem.

For

\[
 \Sigma(\alpha)=\sum_{r\sim R}\alpha_r
 \sum_{n\le N}e(kr\bar n/q),
\]

Theorem 2 assumes prime \(q\), \((k,q)=1\),
\(1<R<q/2\), \(R<N<q\), and an integer \(s\ge1\), and states

\[
 \Sigma\ll \|\alpha\|_\infty RN\log q
 \left(
 \frac{s!q\log q}{NR(N/R)^s}
 +\frac{s q^{1/2}\log q}{NR}
 +\frac{s!}{(N/R)^s}+\frac{s}{q^{1/2}}
 \right)^{1/(2s)}.
\]

Theorem 3, with prime \(q\), \((k,q)=1\), \(1<R<q/2\), \(1<N<q\), gives

\[
 \Sigma\ll \|\alpha\|_\infty RN\log q
 \left(\frac{s q^{1+1/s}\log q}{RN^2}+\frac{s}{N}\right)^{1/(2s)}.
\]

In the most favorable fictional specialization \(R,N\asymp q\), \(s=1\),
Theorem 3 gives only a \(q^{-1/2}\) saving.  Translated to the already
restored project raw capacity \(Lq\), this is \(L\sqrt q\) (up to
logarithms), exactly (177.K32), not the \(L\) in (177.K34).  Theorem 2 is
weaker at comparable \(R,N\) because its \((N/R)^{-s}\) term need not
save a power.

The literal application fails even before that power comparison.  The
first failed hypothesis is that the project conductor \(q=u_0/(\ell,u_0)\)
is arbitrary and can be composite, whereas Theorems 1--3 require prime
\(q\).  On the prime subfamily the next failure is coefficient class:
Shen has one separable outer sequence and an unweighted initial inverse
row; (177.K15) has a joint \((v,n_0,t)\) symbol, moving square-root phase,
two orientations, physical lifts and hard boundaries.  The source also
requires \(N<q\), while a completion of the project lifts may not replace
the literal ranges by one primitive period.

Theorem 4 assumes \(1<N<Q\), permits arbitrary \(\alpha\), and proves the
positive modulus-average second moment

\[
 \sum_{\substack{q\sim Q\\q\ {\rm odd}}}|\Delta(\alpha,N;q)|^2
 \ll_\varepsilon \|\alpha\|_2^2N^{11/12}Q^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]

At \(Q\asymp N\), division by the number of moduli and square root yields
only an RMS scale \(\|\alpha\|_2N^{11/24+\varepsilon}\) per modulus.  It
does not select the one fixed supported \((\kappa,u)\), and its positive
square cannot retain the outer alias and orientation signs in (177.K34).
Lemma 1 rewrites the parity discrepancy using absolute values inside the
inverse-mode decomposition; that placement likewise cannot provide the
required signed alias recombination.  **Conclusion: no `K17a` or `K26`
import.**

### 2.4 Guo: growing-shift logarithmic Chowla correlations versus `K26`

Primary source: Jizhou Guo, *Logarithmic Chowla Correlations Uniformly over
Fixed Polylogarithmic Shift Ranges*,
[arXiv:2608.23500v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23500), submitted
2026-08-24 and revised 2026-08-26; [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23500v3).
This is the official abstract-record title; the rendered v3 manuscript uses
the internal title *Quantitative Logarithmic Chowla Correlations over Growing
Shift Ranges*.

Theorem 1.1 gives an almost-all statement for
\(h\le\exp((\log x)^\eta)\), outside one structured exceptional set.
Theorem 1.2, for fixed \(1<p<\infty\), arbitrary 1-bounded \(b\), and a
shift interval \(L<h\le L+H\), has the maximal moment form

\[
 \frac1H\sum_{L<h\le L+H}\sup_{y\le x}
 \left|\sum_{n\le y}\frac{\lambda(n)b(n+h)}n\right|^p
 \ll_p 1+\frac{x}{H}.
\]

Theorem 1.3 removes the \(x/H\) loss for polynomial shift windows
\(H\ge x^\theta\), \(\theta>1/3\), but remains an averaged-shift moment.
Theorem 1.4 supplies first/second shift moments.  Theorem 1.6 gives an
all-shifts power-logarithmic saving only for every fixed polylogarithmic
range \(h\le(\log x)^A\).

The first failed `K26` hypothesis is the literal coefficient: Guo fixes the
first factor \(\lambda(n)/n\) and one translated 1-bounded sequence, while
`K26` is the Fejer endpoint correlation of the actual project coefficient,
its moving square-root phase, cardinal selector, orientations, floors,
stars, and endpoint law.  The quantifier is also wrong: shift average or
almost-all shift does not prove the one literal maximal endpoint.

There is no hidden power gain after normalization.  In the most favorable
fictional comparison \(x\asymp H\asymp M=L^2\), removing the factor \(1/n\)
restores a scale \(x\) to each row and the full \(H\)-shift accumulation is
\(Hx\asymp x^2=L^4\), precisely the coefficient-uniform positive `K26`
capacity, not \(L^3\).  Theorem 1.6 covers only the already-safe
polylogarithmic shift sector and saves logarithms, not the missing factor
\(L\).  **Conclusion: no `K26` import.**

2026-08-26.  Theorem 1.1 assumes degree \(n\ge3\), \(0<y\le0.01x\),
\(\alpha=a/q+\lambda\), \((a,q)=1\), and

\[
 (qx^{n-2}y)^{-1}\ll|\lambda|\ll(qy^{n-1})^{-1},
\]

and for the coefficient-one polynomial Weyl sum proves

\[
 |T(a/q+\lambda;x,y)|\ll
 q^{1-1/n}\log q+q^{1-1/n}\log(2+|\lambda|x^{n-1})

\]

The first failed hypothesis is the phase: degree-\(n\) polynomial versus
the inverse-residue or square-root product/difference phase.  The source
also has no arbitrary actual coefficient, selector, or endpoint.  There is
no project power map.

### 2.8 Direct `M1`, `GAR`, and classical radial sources

**Banerjee--Khurana.** D. Banerjee and K. Khurana, *Character analogues of
Cohen type identities and related Voronoi summation formulas*,
[arXiv:2306.12399v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12399).  Equation (5.11)
gives the exact Dirichlet-series coefficient identity

\[
 \overline\sigma_{z,\chi}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^z\chi(n/d),
 \qquad
 \tau_{\chi_4,z}(n)=\overline\sigma_{-z,\chi_4}(n).
\]

Theorems 4.3--4.4 assume an odd primitive character, an analytic test on a
finite interval, nonintegral endpoints, and \(0<\Re\nu<1/2\), and give
fixed-parameter Voronoi identities with explicit \(J_\nu,Y_\nu,K_\nu\)
kernels.  The first failed project hypothesis is uniformity: no theorem is
stated uniformly for arbitrarily large imaginary order or the hard
\(1/u\) Perron top, and there is no maximal bound.  The identity does not
supply the missing \(X^{1/12}\) or prove `GAR`.

**Kiral--Zhou.** E. M. Kiral and F. Zhou, *The Voronoi formula and double
Dirichlet series*, [arXiv:1508.01985](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.01985),
[published DOI](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2016.10.2267).  Theorem 1.3
derives Voronoi formulas from Hecke relations, moderate growth and exact
functional equations for primitive twists; Example 1.8 treats certain
noncuspidal isobaric sums, and Remark 3.2 gives a separate degree-two
modification.  The project product
\(\zeta(s+z/2)L(s-z/2,\chi_4)\) is degree two, noncuspidal, ramified at 4,
and has a zeta pole.  The first failed black-box hypothesis is therefore
the untreated polar/ramified degree-two package.  The source also gives no
mode-integrable bound uniform in the complex shift and top Perron
parameter.  It is structural support only.

**Popov radial return.** D. A. Popov, *Voronoi's formulae and the Gauss
problem*, Russian Math. Surveys 79:1 (2024),
[official Math-Net record](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm10162).  Theorem 5,
equations (5.1)--(5.2), states for \(N,x\ge3\)

\[
 P(x)=-\frac{x^{1/4}}\pi\sum_{n\le N}
 \frac{r_2(n)}{n^{3/4}}
 \cos(2\pi\sqrt{nx}+\pi/4)+\Delta_NP(x),
\]

\[
 \Delta_NP(x)\ll \sqrt{x/N}\,\overline r(x)+\overline r(N)\log N,
 \qquad \overline r(t)=t^{o(1)}.
\]

At \(N\asymp X^{1/2}\), the error is target-sized.  This exactly certifies
the radial Hardy--Voronoi return map if the project's angular coefficient
has first been completed to \(r_2(n)/4\).  The first unproved project step
is precisely that angular completion; the theorem gives no new estimate
for the radial sum.  It therefore does not prove direct `M1` or `GAR`.

**Popov local moments.** In the same paper, Theorem 10(ii), equation
(10.3), gives uniformly for \(H\le T/2\)

\[
 \int_{T-H}^{T+H}|P(x)|^2dx
 \ll H\sqrt T+T(\log T)^2.
\]

Theorems 10(iii)--(iv) begin fourth/sixth local moments only at
\(H\ge T^{1/2}\).  At \(H=T^\alpha\), \(\alpha\le1/2\), the additive
\(T\)-term forces the persistence exponent
\(\max(1/3,(1-\alpha)/2)\), equal to \(1/3\) for
\(1/3\le\alpha\le1/2\).  This is a valid assembled-remainder guardrail,
but no improvement and no literal rational-cluster estimate.

These exact identities explain the return geometry but supply neither the
two independent direct-`M1` signed estimates nor the complete lower-radial
`GAR` estimate.

### 2.9 Complete `t=1`, hard-`TOP`, and hybrid-moment sources

**Chen--Lin.** Z. Chen and Y. Lin, *The second moment of twisted modular
L-functions and Dirichlet L-functions at the central point*,
[arXiv:2608.25812v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25812), submitted
2026-08-26.  Corollary 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 assume Selberg's eigenvalue
conjecture.  The family fixes a holomorphic Hecke cusp form for
\({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), averages primitive even characters, and uses
factorized moduli

\[
 q=q_1q_2\asymp Q,\qquad Q_2\asymp Q^{\delta_1},\quad
 0<\delta_1<0.0004,\quad (q_1,q_2)=1,\quad(q,6)=1,

with smooth modulus weights.  Theorem 1.2 has a family main term and error
\(O(Q^{2-\delta_1/5})\).  The **first failed hypothesis** is conditionality:
the project does not assume Selberg.  Even conditionally, the theorem is a
character/factorized-modulus average with fixed automorphic coefficients,
not the two-independent-height literal product collar, hard endpoint or

**Kwan.** Chung-Hang Kwan, *Spectral Moment Formulae for GL(3) x GL(2)

[arXiv:2310.09419v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09419), revised

**Liyang Yang.** Liyang Yang, *Spectral Reciprocity: A Fourier--Analytic

Yang's later *Fourier Spectral Reciprocity and Canonical Hecke L-Functions*,
[arXiv:2608.23886v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23886), submitted

**Tang and Ji.** Z. S. Tang,

## 3. Proof or derivation

### 3.1 Audit method

Each candidate was tested in this order:

1. current theorem status, exact version and theorem number;
2. pointwise/averaged/almost-all/smoothed quantifier;
3. modulus, primality, coprimality, factorization and range conditions;
4. literal coefficient class, including whether it is joint or separable;
5. phase, selector, centre, physical lifts and endpoint support;
6. placement of absolute values and positive spectral/energy norms;
7. restoration of normalization, modulus, shifts, blocks and incomplete
   lifts to project scale; and
8. downstream owner scope.

The first failed item stops a black-box import.  A favorable fictional map
was nevertheless priced when it reveals how far the numerical theorem is
from the target.  Such a price is diagnostic only and is never promoted as
an application.

### 3.2 `K17a` derivation

At exact conductor \(q\), the project has already paid every physical lift
and has positive capacity \(Lq\log(2q)\).  The required aliaswise statement
has size \(L\) after the factor \(u_0/q\), so the saving must be a full
factor \(q\).  A theorem producing one square root yields

\[
 Lq\cdot q^{-1/2}=L\sqrt q,
\]

which is precisely the parked (177.K32) boundary.  Shen Theorem 3 reaches
only this boundary in the fictitious prime/separable comparable-length
case.  Its actual prime and coefficient hypotheses fail, and its Theorem 4
averages positive squares over moduli.  The Blomer--Pascadi/Pascadi/MQW/
Wright corpus either fixes the modulus, separates coefficients, places
absolute values inside outer sums, or restores to a non-saving power.
Therefore no theorem proves (177.K34) or (177.K35).

### 3.3 `K26` derivation

Round 175 makes the full stopped chain the exact endpoint coboundary
\(Q_M^*-Q_{R_0}^*\), with the lower endpoint and restoration seams already
target-safe.  Hence only

\[
 Q_M^*\ll L^3X^\varepsilon
\]

remains, against positive capacity \(L^4X^\varepsilon\).  Fixed-shift
determinant errors must be summed over at least one or two shift variables,
and positive modulus/shift moments erase the literal outer sign.  Guo's
logarithmic normalization, when removed in the best fictional full-shift
map \(x=H=L^2\), also returns \(Hx=L^4\), not \(L^3\).  Exact-energy
theorems do not encode the actual approximate phase and joint coefficient.
Thus the missing factor \(L\) remains.

### 3.4 Hard `TOP`, `BAL`, and `UNBAL` derivation

The hybrid-moment sources have one spectral height or a family average;
none accepts the two independent heights, cardinal selector and moving
product collar.  Their constant/residual/continuous terms cannot be

\(t=1\), and other few-point channels remain independent.

For critical `BAL`, exact additive-energy and positive decoupling estimates
begin after coefficient erasure and so stop at the audited \(L^4\)
capacity.  Their algebraic surfaces/curves also do not encode the two far

\[
 \mu(a)=\begin{cases}
 a-1/4,&1/4<a\le1/3,\\
 (1-2a)/4,&1/3\le a<1/2.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first unproved step is source-specific; it is not a generic request for
"more cancellation."

1. **`K17a`:** prove a theorem for arbitrary (possibly composite) reduced
   conductor \(q\) that accepts the joint \((v,n_0,t)\) actual symbol,
   both orientations and all physical lifts, and gains the full \(q\) in
   the signed alias/modulus packet.  Shen fails first at prime modulus and
   then at coefficient/support class.

   inequality giving the one factor \(L\) which disappears under positive
   closure.  Guo and the determinant/energy sources fail first at the
   coefficient/test-function map.
3. **hard `TOP`:** construct a legal two-independent-height/cardinal test
   section and retain every main, residual and continuous piece.  Existing
   reciprocity sources fail at that section before a power estimate.
4. **critical `BAL`:** prove a nonlocal actual-symbol estimate for the
   oscillatory zero-subtracted double-far remainder.  Exact unsigned energy
   and local commutator routes do not reach it.
5. **remaining-label `BAL`:** state and prove one uniform owner for all
   uncovered labels and the exact-square boundary.  No source even has the
   required quantifier.
6. **`UNBAL`:** keep the literal matrix vector and signs across varying
   moduli.  The first legal scalar source map is numerically non-saving.
7. **direct `M1`:** independently recover \(X^{1/12}\) on both critical
   parents.  Fixed-parameter Voronoi identities are not estimates uniform
   in the hard Perron mode.
8. **`GAR`:** prove the entire lower-radial signed owner, not only the radial
   identity or selected scalar layers.
9. **endpoint/bridges:** obtain all upstream parents first and then verify
   real-\(X\), floors, stars, entry/exit and route scope without double use.
10. **graded lane:** prove an owner-preserving determinant/local-moment
    theorem below the complete \(Y^{35/48}\) capacity; target strength is
    \(Y^{1/2}\).
11. **Li--Yang:** outside the narrow repaired final application, the first
    doubtful step remains the unrepaired general range/decoupling chain;
    it may not be used to certify a new project lemma.
