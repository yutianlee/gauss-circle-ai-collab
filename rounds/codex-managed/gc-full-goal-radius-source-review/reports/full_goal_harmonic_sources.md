## 3. Proof, complete costs, and frontier comparison

### 3.1 A literal cone lift and its trace price

Define the two-dimensional torus wave

\[
 F_N(x,R)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}}c_m
                         e(x\cdot m+R|m|),\qquad
 c_m=|m|^{-3/2}\Psi(|m|/\sqrt T)v(|m|^2/N).        \tag{214.H6}
\]

Then \(F_N(0,R)=S_N(R)\) exactly. There are \(O(N)\) lattice frequencies,
each of size \(O(N^{-3/4})\). Multiplicity has not been erased: all
representations of each integer norm contribute separately and recombine
to the exact \(r_2(n)\) at \(x=0\).

For every fixed \(p\ge1\), a smooth torus reproducing kernel for the
frequency disk \(|m|\ll\sqrt N\), followed by Holder, gives

\[
 |F_N(0,R)|^p\ll_p N\int_{[0,1]^2}|F_N(x,R)|^pdx.               \tag{214.H7}
\]

Indeed its \(L^1\) norm is bounded and its supremum is \(O(N)\).
The power \(N\) is a spatial trace cost; a spacetime norm cannot be
identified with the value on the fixed spatial line \(x=0\).

Partition the whole radius interval into \(O(T/\sqrt N)\) windows of
length at most \(\sqrt N\). For a window centered at \(R_0\), put
\(y=\sqrt N x\), \(u=\sqrt N(R-R_0)\). Multiply the rescaled wave by a
fixed Schwartz cutoff at scale \(S\asymp N\), whose Fourier transform is
supported in a sufficiently small ball and whose modulus is bounded below
on the required central cube. Such a cutoff can be made by rescaling the
inverse Fourier transform of a nonnegative smooth bump. Denote the result
by \(f(y,u)\). Its frequencies are

\[
 (m/\sqrt N,|m|/\sqrt N),                           \tag{214.H8}
\]

thickened by \(O(N^{-1})\). A fixed radial rescaling and a bounded
partition cover the source's truncated cone. Modulation of \(c_m\) by
\(e(R_0|m|)\) changes no norm. All constants are independent of \(R_0,T,N\).

Take the lawful parameters \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1/2\). A small cap has both
tangential dimensions \(N^{-1/2}\). Its spatial projection has diameter
\(O(N^{-1/2})\), so it meets only \(O(1)\) frequencies \(m/\sqrt N\).
This remains true on rational rays and for repeated integer norms.
Use a bounded-overlap smooth cap partition; each cap cutoff has uniformly
bounded convolution \(L^1\) norm after its affine scaling. Consequently

\[
 \sum_\gamma\|f_\gamma\|_p^p
          \ll_p N\,N^{-3p/4}\,N^3=N^{4-3p/4}.                 \tag{214.H9}
\]

The physical spatial cutoff occupies a square of side \(\asymp\sqrt N\).
It contains \(\asymp N\) full torus cells on which the cutoff is bounded
below. The rescaling Jacobian is \(N^{3/2}\). Combining this periodic
cell count with (H7) gives, for the selected radius window \(J\),

\[
 \int_J|S_N(R)|^p dR\ll_p N^{-3/2}\|f\|_p^p.                  \tag{214.H10}
\]

Thus no free spatial averaging is used. In (H4), \(s=1\), and the
three source powers become \(N^{p/2-1},N^{p-3},N^{p/2-1}\).
Equations (H9)--(H10) yield

\[
 \int_J|S_N|^p\ll N^\epsilon
           \{N^{3/2-p/4}+N^{p/4-1/2}\}.                       \tag{214.H11}
\]

Sum every window, use \(|H_N|\le|S_N|\), and absorb source epsilon
losses into \(T^\epsilon\). This proves the candidate consequence (H1).
Bounded \(N,T\), noninteger window centers and the two partial end windows
are included by enlarging fixed constants. This proof needs no numerical
experiment and no claim about the distribution of lattice angles.

At \(p=10\), the ratio of the expensive term in (H1) to (H3) is

\[
 T^{-18/65}N^2.                                                \tag{214.H12}
\]

Even at the lower band boundary it is \(T^{242/325}\). It only increases
with \(N\). Thus this valid source application supplies no tenth-gate gain.
At \(p=12\), the corresponding price is \(TN^2\). Using a single
radius window of length \(T\) would give a still worse \(T^{p/2-1}\)
term; localization has already removed that avoidable loss.

There is a sharp local control for the expensive (H11) term in the
enlarged coefficient class. Choose \(c_m=N^{-3/4}e(-R_*|m|)\) on a fixed
interior lattice annulus. At \((0,R_*)\) the wave is \(\asymp N^{1/4}\),
and remains so on a radius interval of length \(cN^{-1/2}\). Its local
\(p\)-moment is at least \(c_pN^{p/4-1/2}\). These are deliberately
aligned coefficients, not the fixed physical coefficients. They show why
the coefficient-uniform theorem plus a trace inequality cannot delete
that local term. They do not refute the actual arithmetic target.

### 3.2 Why the current Dirichlet-polynomial theorem does not transfer

For (H2), the dimensionless weight
\(r_2(n)(n/N)^{-3/4}\Psi(\sqrt{n/T})v(n/N)\) is \(O_\eta(T^\eta)\).
Dividing by a suitable constant times \(T^\eta\) meets the coefficient
hypothesis in (H5). The corresponding unnormalized level would be
\(V\asymp N^{3/4}T^{-\eta}\) times the desired physical block level.
That normalization is not the obstruction.

An exact time reparameterization would require, up to a common phase and
fixed coefficient phases, \(2\pi R\sqrt n=t(R)\log n\) for all active
\(n\). Differentiating in \(R\), or just comparing three frequencies,
would force the square-root sequence to be affine in \(\log n\).
The derivative ratio is \(\sqrt n/2\), which is not constant. Consequently
(H5) is not a theorem for this family. Its multiplicative ratio algebra
cannot be replaced by a different frequency curve without a new theorem.

The elementary approximation does not repair the full block. Match the
first derivative at \(n=N\) using \((\sqrt N/2)\log(n/N)\). For
\(n=N+j\), the difference from \(\sqrt{N+j}-\sqrt N\) is
\(j^2/(8N^{3/2})+O(j^3/N^{5/2})\). At \(R\asymp T\), the phase
error is order \(TJ^2/N^{3/2}\) on a subinterval of length \(J\).
Keeping it bounded requires \(J\ll N^{3/4}T^{-1/2}\). For most of the
critical band, in particular \(N<T^{2/3}\), this does not even give an
unbounded integer interval. On the rest it introduces power-many pieces.
Moreover zero-extending a short interval in (H5) leaves ambient length
\(N\), not \(J\); shifting the integer index does not turn
\(\log(N+j)\) into \(\log j\). Absorbing a central radius into the
coefficients only replaces \(T\) by a local radius-window length and adds
the cost of covering all windows. None of those costs is supplied by
a valid complete transfer here. The import stops at its first exact
frequency mismatch; no numerical bound from (H5) is credited.

### 3.3 The whole moment route and the radius route have different limits

The accepted kernel gives, for the complete fixed series,

\[
 D_T(V)\ll T^\epsilon(V^{-2}+TV^{-136/13}),\quad
 \|B_T\|_\infty\ll T^{\beta+\epsilon},\quad
 M_p(B_T)\ll T^{1+\epsilon}+T^{(p-2)\beta+\epsilon}.           \tag{214.H13}
\]

No unweighted moment theorem is substituted for its actual coefficients.
The \(V^{-2}\) term includes the entire Gram diagonal and the one-window
covering term. The aligned radial coefficient control in the accepted
kernel prevents removing it by the generic coefficient norm alone.

If a complete radius moment is \(M_p\ll T^{E(p)+\epsilon}\), sharp
reconstruction multiplies by \(T^{p/2}\), the Jacobian by \(T\), and
adds the target-sized smoothing contribution \(T^{2+p/2+\epsilon}\).
Monotonicity of the inclusive lattice count gives persistence on a
one-sided squared-radius interval of length comparable to \(|P(X)|\).
Thus the resulting pointwise exponent is

\[
 \Theta(p)=\frac{p/4+(1+\max(1,E(p)))/2}{p+1}.                  \tag{214.H14}
\]

For \(E_0(p)=\max(1,(p-2)\beta)\), this approaches \(\theta\), not
\(1/4\). Removing only the \(V^{-2}\) term while retaining the one fixed
tail exponent \(136/13\) would likewise fail to establish the hierarchy
as \(p\to\infty\). For the full goal a sufficient complete family is
\(E(p)=1+o(p)\) along unbounded fixed \(p\), with constants allowed to
depend on \(p\). In particular \(M_p\ll T^{1+\epsilon}\) for every
fixed \(p\) suffices. No \(p\) depending on \(T\) is required.

A concrete existing complete test is

\[
 \int_{|B_T(R)|>T^{13/125}}w(R/T)|B_T(R)|^{12}dR
                 \ll T^{29/25+\epsilon}.                      \tag{214.H15}
\]

Here the fixed nonnegative \(w\) covers the full reconstruction interval.
The accepted distribution already pays the complementary low values:
integrating its two terms up to \(T^{13/125}\) gives
\(T^{26/25}\) and \(T^{29/25}\), respectively. The complete existing
twelfth moment is \(T^{10\beta+\epsilon}\), so the missing saving is
\(10\beta-29/25\). Restoring amplitude and Jacobian gives sharp moment
\(Y^{102/25+\epsilon}\); persistence gives \(102/325\). This remains
only a milestone. (H3) is another sufficient complete test at that
milestone, not a theorem equivalent to all moments or to (H15).

For comparison, the inherited radius certificate keeps the actual
four-factor coefficient
\(r_2(n)r_2(n+h)r_2(n+k)r_2(n+h+k)\), complete shifts and zero extensions.
Its energy expansion has eight factors. With
\(\Lambda_*=TLK/N^{3/2}\), full product-shell fractions, the
\(\sqrt q\) bandwidth recovery, tails, and already paid complementary
sectors give the sufficient upper-certificate price

\[
 \sqrt{N\Lambda_*}+N/\Lambda_*.                                \tag{214.H16}
\]

Writing \(\Lambda_*=N^{1/3}z\) makes this
\(N^{2/3}(\sqrt z+z^{-1})\), bounded below by a positive constant times
\(N^{2/3}\) **as a cost of this positive certificate**. Thus (H16)
cannot certify \(T^{4b}\) above \(N=T^{6b}\), however its free lengths
are chosen. The complementary high-scale cap begins at
\(N=T^{1-2b}\), forcing \(b\ge1/8\) if the complete intervening band
is to be covered by this certificate. At \(b=1/8\), the usual lengths
\(L\asymp N^{1/2}T^{-1/4}\), \(K\asymp NT^{-1/2}\) give
\(\Lambda_*\asymp T^{1/4}\). Both terms in (H16) and the two
differencing diagonals are then at most \(T^{1/2}\) on
\(T^{1/2}<N<T^{3/4}\); lower and higher scales use their complementary
caps. Rounded lengths near the lower equality endpoint are handled by
the trivial cap. This is only a conditional extension requiring its
arithmetic input on the enlarged band. It gives exponent
\(1/4+(1/8)/2=5/16\), and is not quarter.

The displayed argument does not analyze a changed radius theorem with
different decay, a signed summation between shells, or a different
pointwise bridge. Such inputs can escape this specific certificate.
Nor does it assert that actual \(Q_j\) attains its proposed upper bound.

### 3.5 One concrete source-inspired direction: actual wave mass near the spatial line

Use the exact localized waves \(f_j(y,u)\) constructed in Section 3.1,
one for every radius window of length \(\sqrt N\), with their actual
modulated lattice coefficients \(c_m e(R_j|m|)\). Choose a fixed
nonnegative rapidly decreasing spatial weight
\(W(y)=C_A(1+|y|^2)^{-A}\), with \(A\) large enough. The spatial
Fourier support of each \(f_j\) is in a fixed disk, so a fixed smooth
reproducing kernel and Holder give

\[
 |f_j(0,u)|^p\ll_p\int_{\mathbb R^2}W(y)|f_j(y,u)|^pdy.
 \tag{214.H17}
\]

The cutoff at \(y=0\) is bounded below on its assigned central radius
window. Consequently the following **complete weighted-tube gate** is
sufficient for (H3):

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sum_j\int_{\mathbb R^3}W(y)|f_j(y,u)|^{10}\,dy\,du
       \ll T^{83/65+\epsilon},\qquad
 T^{166/325}<N<T^{242/325}.\quad}                  \tag{214.H18}
\]

The factor \(N^{-1/2}\) converting \(du\) to \(dR\) is exactly the
factor in (H3). The sum includes every window, both partial endpoints,
every lattice direction and every integer norm. The real-part target
follows from this stronger complex estimate. The localization cutoffs,
including their tails, are part of \(f_j\); no window may choose its
coefficient phases independently.

This identifies a specific mechanism worth testing: preserve the
amplitude-dependent cone wave envelopes and their actual modulated
lattice coefficients **before** replacing mass near the spatial line by
the full torus norm. Equivalently, (H18) asks for a bound on

\[
 10\sum_j\int_0^\infty a^9
       \int_{\{|f_j(y,u)|>a\}}W(y)\,dy\,du\,da.
 \tag{214.H19}
\]

The first unproved inequality is (H18), or an actual-coefficient envelope
estimate that implies (H19) at its displayed size after summing every
scale and all narrow/aligned envelopes. The source's unweighted
superlevel-set geometry is an analytic model for that task, not an
imported weighted-tube theorem. We do not claim that replacing Lebesgue
measure by \(W(y)\,dy\,du\) is free. Bounding \(W\) by its supremum and
then using the generic full-cap/torus procedure returns the already
insufficient costs above.

The corresponding full-goal family would bound the left side with power
\(p\) by \(T^{1+o(p)+\epsilon}\sqrt N\), uniformly on every effective
dyadic scale, for unbounded fixed \(p\). Then (H17), complete dyadic
recombination and the paid Schwartz tail give \(E(p)=1+o(p)\) in (H14).
This family, or a stronger complete alternative, is still required;
(H18) is a concrete milestone test, not its substitute. It is deliberately
stated as an open sufficient estimate, with no claim that these source
papers already provide the missing actual envelope information.
