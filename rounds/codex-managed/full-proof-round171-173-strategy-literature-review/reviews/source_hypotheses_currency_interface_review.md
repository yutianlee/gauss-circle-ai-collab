### 2.2 Pointwise circle records and dependency status

**Li--Yang.** The official record for Xiaochun Li and Xuerui Yang, *An improvement on Gauss's circle problem and Dirichlet's divisor problem*, is [arXiv:2308.14859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14859): v1 was submitted 2023-08-28 and v2 on 2023-09-14, with no later indexed arXiv version at the review cutoff. The current author publication page continues to label the work “Preprint.” Theorem 1.2 uses the area variable

\[
 R(X)=\sum_{m^2+n^2\le X}1-\pi X
\]

and asserts, pointwise for every (\varepsilon>0),

\[
 R(X)=O_\varepsilon(X^{\theta_*+\varepsilon}).
\]

Thus no radius-to-area conversion is needed for this record. The Round-174 report correctly limits the project import to the repaired narrow Theorem-1.2 chain recorded in `sources/li_yang_2023.md`; it does not silently import the unrepaired general Proposition 3.1/Theorem 4.2 range chain.

**Huxley.** The official journal abstract for M. N. Huxley, *Exponential sums and lattice points III*, gives (K=131/208) in the maximum radius-of-curvature variable (R). Under the circle convention (X=R^2),

\[
 R^{131/208}=X^{131/416}.
\]

The Round-174 normalization is therefore correct. A citation of (131/208) directly as an area-variable exponent would be a normalization error.

**Bourgain--Watt.** [arXiv:1709.04340v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04340) was withdrawn on 2023-07-16. The notice identifies problems in Propositions 2, 3, and (1'), and states that Theorems 1--3 lose theorem status. Those propositions and theorems cannot be theorem dependencies. The withdrawal does not, however, erase algebraic identities that have been checked and rederived independently. In particular, the repaired Li--Yang source card retains directly audited Section-7 two-sign/two-range sawtooth algebra and separately rederived symbolic spacing material; it does not rely on the withdrawn Bourgain--Watt theorem statements as black boxes.

### 2.3 Fixed-modulus Kloosterman records

### 2.4 Restored `UNBAL` powers and the arbitrary-center clause

For the favorable unbalanced orientation with ((N_0,n)=1), set ((c,M,N)=(n,1,n)), (a=N_0), and use the full (h)-row with zero padding at (h=n). The Round-174 arithmetic is correct:

- Blomer--Pascadi Theorem 5.5 has
  \[
  \mathcal H(1,n,n):\quad n^{-1/32},\ 1,\ n^{2/15},\ n^{1/9},\ 1,
  \]
  and hence gives (n^{17/15+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2).
- Theorem 1.1 at (N=n) gives (n^{23/18+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2).
- Theorem 5.7 has bracket (n^{-1/4}+1+n^{-1/4}\asymp1), hence gives (n^{1+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2), with zero power saving.
- Splitting into (n^{1/2}) translated square-root blocks and applying Theorem 1.1 by triangle/Cauchy gives (n^{39/32+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2). This is not a theorem for the signed recombined row.
- Pascadi's hypothetical analogous block recombination has size (n^{5/4-1/700+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2); it remains hypothetical because the theorem is for initial ranges, not arbitrary translated blocks.
- In the Milićević--Qin--Wu theorem, setting (M=1,N=q) and restoring the omitted (q^{1/2}) from normalized (\mathrm{Kl}_2) makes the leading size (q^{7/6+o(1)}\|\beta\|_2).

The claimed universal first failure at ((N_0,n)=1) is nevertheless false. Kloosterman symmetry gives

\[
 S_{\rm proj}(N_0,h;n)=S_{\rm src}(h,N_0;n).
\]

Apply Blomer--Pascadi Theorem 1.1 with (c=N=n), (a=1), and (I=J=\{1,\ldots,n\}). Put

\[
 \alpha_h=
 \begin{cases}
 \widehat\gamma_{g,n}(h),&1\le h<n,\\
 0,&h=n,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \beta_m=\mathbf 1_{m=r(N_0)},
\]

where (r(N_0)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}) is the representative of (N_0\bmod n), with (r(0)=n). The multiplier (a=1) is a unit, and the initial-interval clause removes the joint gcd condition. This is a legal application for every (N_0), including ((N_0,n)>1). It yields the same (n^{23/18+o(1)}\|\widehat\gamma_{g,n}\|_2) bound.

Therefore coprimality is the first failure only for the sharper **orientation** (M=1,a=N_0) used with Theorems 5.5/5.7. It is not the first obstruction to every fixed-modulus application. The robust obstruction is quantitative and structural: even the all-center Theorem-1.1 map gives no useful saving, stays at one fixed modulus under an absolute value, and does not preserve the outer varying-modulus signs.

## 3. Proof or derivation

The review used the following source-to-project checks.

1. **Version and status.** The official abstract/version history was compared with each cited version, and publication status was kept separate from arXiv and project-import status. This confirms Li--Yang v2/preprint, Bourgain--Watt v2/withdrawn, Blomer--Pascadi v1, Pascadi v2, Milićević--Qin--Wu v1, and the stated current August versions.
2. **Normalization.** Radius powers were converted by (X=R^2); normalized (\mathrm{Kl}_2) bounds were multiplied by (q^{1/2}); block counts and coefficient norms were restored before comparison with `UNBAL`.
3. **Literal hypotheses.** The interval type, length range, gcd/unit condition, coefficient class, smoothness, fixed versus varying modulus/shift, and every placement of an absolute value were copied before attempting a parameter map.
4. **All-center falsification.** The symmetry (S(u,v;c)=S(v,u;c)), the initial-interval gcd-removal clause of Blomer--Pascadi Theorem 1.1, a delta sequence at the residue of (N_0), and zero padding at (h=n) give a direct counterexample to the report's claim that nonunit (N_0) makes the theorem unavailable. Substituting (N=c=n) then reproduces the third term (n^{2/3}n^{-7/18}=n^{5/18}) inside the bracket and hence (n^{1+5/18}=n^{23/18}), since the delta sequence has (\ell^2)-norm one.
5. **Determinant seam.** Direct expansion verifies (d'm'-dm=r), but no algebraic equality proves automorphy, the autocorrelation condition, smooth admissibility, or affordable derivative losses. The report appropriately refuses that inference.
6. **Full-spectrum and sign test.** Exceptional-only positive sums and modulus averages with inner absolute values cannot be substituted for a signed varying-modulus arithmetic side. The missing spectral pieces and outer signs are theorem data, not removable bookkeeping.
7. **Cutoff logic.** The date certifies only the queried official index and directly inspected records. The report's explicit caveat is therefore essential to, and consistent with, the no-match conclusion.

These derivations preserve the report's main negative result. The newly found all-center map makes one local obstruction statement false, but its restored power is quantitatively worse than the needed row estimate and it still destroys the cross-modulus sign; it closes no owner.
