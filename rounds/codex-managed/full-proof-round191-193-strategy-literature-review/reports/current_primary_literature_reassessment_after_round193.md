## 3. Proof or derivation

### 3.1 Audit method

For every plausible source, the audit was performed in the following
order.

1. Check the primary record, current version and withdrawal/erratum status.
2. Transcribe the theorem's quantifiers, coefficient class, support,
   modulus class, smoothness/endpoints, and exact placement of absolute
   values or norms.
3. Demand a literal map from all project variables and the physical mask to
   the source variables.  No selector, orientation, affine site, lift, or
   outer aggregation may be silently discarded.
4. Substitute the project ranges into the theorem, then restore every
   omitted row, height, modulus, cofactor, orientation, and endpoint collar.
5. Compare the restored result with the owner target.  A formal saving in a
   scalar subrow is not counted if positive recombination consumes it.

This procedure explains the no-match result without making an impossibility
claim.  MRS and Shen fail at Step 3.  The fixed-modulus bilinear theorems
also fail at Step 3 and, even under a fictitious favorable embedding, fail
Step 5.  Xiao, TTY, energy, Popov, statistical, and spectral-family inputs
put an absolute value or positive norm at a different location, so Step 4
restores the known deficit.  Li--Yang proves a different terminal theorem
and cannot be substituted into an internal owner.

### 3.2 Why the Round-193 split changes the source question

Round 193 did not create a predicate on Fourier modes.  It deleted
physical atoms and transported that deletion through the exact Rounds
187--192 linear source expansion.  Therefore a theorem for an already
formed scalar Kloosterman row cannot be applied first and then multiplied
by \(P_i\).  Identity (194.10) shows the extra affine-site collar that any
valid transform must retain.  The source must either accept that joint
coefficient directly or prove a preliminary exact transform preserving its
signs.  None of the audited papers states such a theorem.

The partition (194.9) is ordered by first failure.  The lower-far mask
\(P_1\) includes every atom whose first close inequality fails, regardless
of the upper inequality.  Only after the lower inequality holds does
\(P_2\) record upper failure.  Consequently separate estimates on a
symmetric far/far subset, an orbit-closed image, a coprime submask, or
\(r\equiv2\pmod4\) do not imply either full complement estimate.

### 3.3 Restored power at the first seam

The Rounds 187--192 positive capacity for the rho-large residual is
target-sized except for the exact factor (194.4): at a fixed spectral
packet the available height/row ledger is proportional to \(Y\kappa u\),
whereas the target permits \(H_B\mathfrak m\kappa u\).  Dividing gives
\(Y/(H_B\mathfrak m)\).  The strict inequality in (194.5) says the factor
can genuinely exceed one.  Any theorem applied after a rowwise absolute
value, \(\ell^2\) norm, or positive modulus average is therefore too late;
it has already discarded the signs required to remove that quotient.

The Round-193 close count avoids this issue by determinant localization:
its two inequalities force a square-root-width physical family, and
(194.11) pays it absolutely.  A one-sided failure has no corresponding
width restriction, so the same count cannot be widened.  At
\(D=L^{1/2+\delta}\), the explicit \(LD^2\) contribution proves that this
particular absolute proof already exceeds the target by
\(L^{2\delta}\).  No audited source supplies the missing signed
recombination on the physical complement.
