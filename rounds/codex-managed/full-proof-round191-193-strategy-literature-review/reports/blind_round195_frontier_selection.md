# 3. Proof or derivation

The recommendation follows from four exact deductions and a complete comparison.

First, after removal of the double-close mask

\[
P_{\rm cl}=\mathbf1_{|d-gm|\le D}\mathbf1_{|d'-gm'|\le D},
\]

Boolean partitioning gives

\[
1-P_{\rm cl}
=\mathbf1_{|d-gm|>D}
+\mathbf1_{|d-gm|\le D}\mathbf1_{|d'-gm'|>D}
=P_1+P_2,
\]

and the two terms are disjoint.  Hence a theorem for the complete \(P_2\) mask is theorem-sized at the first-failure interface; it is not an arbitrarily thin new projector.

Second, the absolute close-window ledger

\[
LD'\log(2L)+L(D')^2
\]

is target-safe at the given boundary \(D'=D\asymp L^{1/2}\), but increasing \(D'\) past that boundary makes \(L(D')^2\) exceed the \(L^2\) scale.  Widening the close sector therefore cannot constitute the missing mechanism.

Third, the current positive control is too large by \(Y/(Qm)\).  Because \(Qm<Y\), (P2-VD) asks for a genuine cancellation factor strictly smaller than one, \(Qm/Y\).  This also proves that an unsigned or coefficient-uniform version cannot be the right theorem: phase-aligned coefficients make the real part equal the positive mass.

Fourth, \(P_2\) offers an asymmetric coordinate pair unavailable in \(P_1\): \(\Delta_-\) is confined to the critical close scale while \(\Delta_+\) is outside it, and both remain tied to the determinant variable \(h\).  This is enough to formulate a complete new vector-dispersion seam.  It is not enough to prove it, which is why the objective remains candidate strategy.

# 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first unproved step is the coefficient-aware Gram estimate needed by (P2-VD).  The packet specifies that the coefficients are actual endpoint products and that orientation pairing produces differences of unrelated products, but it does not state an identity, orthogonality relation, variation bound, or energy estimate for those products.  Consequently there is presently no justification for

\[
\left\|
\sum_{\substack{\omega\in\Omega_{P_2}\\
(g,\Delta_-)=\text{fixed}}}
\mathbf C(\omega)e(\Phi(\omega))
\right\|^2
\ll_{\varepsilon,C_0}
X^\varepsilon\left(\frac{Qm}{Y}\right)^2
\mathcal B_{+,P_2}^2
\]

after summing the determinant/far-coordinate fibres.  In particular, the diagonal may already be too large, or many distinct physical incidences may share the same effective \((\Delta_+,h)\) frequency once endpoints and carries are included.  Counting physical incidences alone cannot settle this coefficient Gram problem.

Round 195 must stop and return a no-go result, without carving out another favourable slice, if any one of the following occurs:

1. the exact \(TT^*\) diagonal exceeds the target by a power or by the full factor \(Y/(Qm)\);
2. an admissible collision family with identical effective phase data has coefficient Gram mass too large for the required saving;
3. controlling endpoint, carry, birth/death, zero-extension, or affine-site commutator vectors costs back \(Y/(Qm)\);
4. the proposed inequality is provable only after replacing actual endpoint products by arbitrary coefficients, their moduli, or separate orientation sums;
5. either \(T=0\) or the strict \(T\ge1\) branch has to be excluded;
6. the proof requires \(D'>\sqrt L\), another Farey subcover, or an additional proper submask of \(P_2\); or
7. the argument also “proves” the phase-aligned adversarial or unsigned analogue from Section 5.
