## 3. Independent derivation

For a legal factor block with divisor-size actual vectors, Cauchy
in the \(M\) variable leaves an unweighted interval in that variable
and differences \(u=R(\sqrt n-\sqrt{n'})\). On a dyadic \(K\) range,
for nonzero difference \(r=|n-n'|\), \(|u|\asymp Tr/\sqrt K\).
Since \(N\le T^{3/2}\), \(|u|/\sqrt M\gg T/\sqrt N\gg T^{1/4}\)
for sufficiently large T. The accepted large-parameter exponent-pair
regime is therefore legal. Its kernel price is, with \(\kappa=13/84\),
\[
 |u|^\kappa M^{1/2+\kappa/2}+\sqrt M/|u|.
\]
The exact diagonal gives \(M^2K\) in the squared bilinear bound.
Summing the first off-diagonal price over r with divisor-size
coefficient norms gives
\[
 |S_{M,K}|^2\ll T^\epsilon\{
 M^2K+T^\kappa M^{3/2+\kappa/2}K^{2+\kappa/2}\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The inverse-u term is absorbed by the displayed terms in this range;
its r sum costs at most a logarithm. Taking square roots and dividing
by \(N^{3/4}\) gives (1). Fixed smooth product weights can be handled
by partial summation once their variation is proved. Literal product
intersection intervals retain their endpoints; an arbitrary mixed
amplitude is not automatically such a weight.

The first affine term in C decreases with k and the second increases.
They meet at
\[
 k_*=(71\nu-26)/252.
\]
For \(\nu<26/71\), the intersection lies below zero, so
\(\inf C=13/168+13\nu/336\), attained at k=0.
For \(\nu\ge26/71\), \(0\le k_*\le\nu\), and
\[
 \inf C=(55\nu+26)/504.
\tag{4}
\]
The trivial exponent \(\nu/4\) is smaller up to \(26/71\).
The increasing branch (4) meets the decreasing classical branch at
\[
 55\nu+26=252-252\nu,\quad
 \nu=226/307,\quad H(\nu)=81/614.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus H consists of the trivial increasing branch until \(26/71\),
the increasing branch (4) until \(226/307\), and then
\((1-\nu)/2\). Equation (5) is its exact global maximum.

At the familiar critical scale \(\nu=2/3\),
\[
 k_*=16/189,\qquad \inf C=47/378.
\tag{6}
\]
This is below beta, since \(47/378<1/8<\beta\), but (5) is above
beta, since \(27/208<81/614\). More precisely,
\[
 (504\beta-26)/55<\nu<1-2\beta
\tag{7}
\]
is a nonempty open interval on which every member of the optimistic
menu has exponent greater than beta. These endpoints lie on the
relevant increasing and decreasing branches; the trivial branch is
also larger there. Consequently
\[
 \sup_{0\le\nu\le3/2}\min\{\beta,H(\nu)\}=\beta.
\tag{8}
\]
The free split grant is stronger than the actual finite-U,V
decomposition. Its failure is therefore already enough to reject
crediting this particular optimized menu as a complete improvement.
It says nothing against a new signed arithmetic inequality.

For reconstruction, the singleton n=1 is O(1). At a fixed large
Schwartz index J, the tail beyond \(Z=T^{3/2}\) has the uniform
absolute price
\[
 \sum_{n>Z}r_2(n)n^{-3/4}|\Psi(\sqrt{n/T})|
 \ll_{\epsilon,J} T^{J/2}Z^{1/4-J/2+\epsilon}
 \ll T^{3/8-J/4+\epsilon}.
\tag{9}
\]
For any prescribed A, fix J sufficiently large to make (9) \(O(T^{-A})\).
The fixed seminorm constant is allowed; no R average or positivity of
Psi is used. All remaining scales must still be estimated. A complete
real-wave exponent gamma would give physical squared-radius exponent
\(1/4+\gamma/2\), with the already paid smoothing error. The optimistic
menu's \(81/614\) would correspond to \(97/307\), which is worse than
theta_LY; this is a diagnostic conversion, not a newly proved bound.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

Equation (1) alone does not prove that the full Vaughan decomposition
admits arbitrary factor lengths. Even that favorable grant does not
improve beta by (8). Actual Mobius/character cancellation might give
a stronger inequality than (1); it has not been obtained here.
Taking a lower bound on H as a lower bound on a circle wave would
reverse the direction of an upper estimate and is invalid.
