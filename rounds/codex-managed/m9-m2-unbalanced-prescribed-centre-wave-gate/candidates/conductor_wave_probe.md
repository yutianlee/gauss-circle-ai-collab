# Conductor candidate: reciprocal selector and coherent-run probe

Campaign: `m9-m2-unbalanced-prescribed-centre-wave-gate`

Status: diagnostic only; no proof-state effect.

## Candidate selector form

The exact wave may be viewed as

\[
 \sum_{r\asymp X/D\atop r\ {m odd}}
 \chi_4(r)W\!\left({X\over rD}\right)
 \sum_{d\asymp D}
 \mathcal Q_L\!\left({r(X-rd)\over4X}\right).
\]

At fixed \(r\), at most \(O(1)\) denominators survive rapid decay. The
raw \(D/L\) capacity is therefore carried by a sparse reciprocal selector,
not by a full rectangle.

## Candidate coherent run

Let \(r_d\) be the nearest admissible odd integer to \(X/d\). On a short
\(d\)-interval, the first difference of \(X/d\) can remain close to an
integer multiple of four for length up to \(D^3/X\), apparently freezing
\(\chi_4(r_d)\). Test this as a hostile control, not as an assumed lower
bound.

The audit must answer:

1. how many \(d\)'s also satisfy \(|X-r_dd|\ll D/L\);
2. whether the literal complex kernel has a common real direction there;
3. whether the fixed profile contains the run with nonnegligible weight;
4. whether all other active pairs can cancel the selected run; and
5. whether the construction is compatible with the residual exponent
   region as \(X\to\infty\).

If any complement is uncontrolled, record a no-go for the proposed lower
bound rather than a counterexample to the wave estimate.

## Candidate positive route

Resolve \(\chi_4(r)\) additively and treat the sparse selector without
first applying a full Poisson inversion. A useful result must beat
\(D/L\) after every selector, profile, and Cauchy cost. A one-variable
second-derivative estimate followed by the \(k\)-triangle is already known
to be insufficient; any successful inequality must be genuinely joint or
use an inverse theorem for coherent selector runs.
