## 3. Pair-count and coherent-run diagnostics

Writing \(s=rd\), the active scales are

\[
 r\asymp R={X\over D},\qquad d\asymp D,
\]

and the allowed \(d\)-width at fixed \(r\) is

\[
 {\Delta\over R}={D^2\over XL}={1\over K}.
\]

Thus there are only \(O(1)\) candidate \(d\)'s per \(r\), and about
\(R/K=\Delta\) active pairs globally. The selector is arithmetically
sparse and discontinuous.

A tempting control chooses a short interval of \(d\)'s on which
\(r_d\approx X/d\) advances by a fixed multiple of four, so
\(\chi_4(r_d)\) is constant. The derivative changes on scale

\[

\]

This is only a candidate coherent run. To become a countermodel it must
also keep the product residual inside \(D/L\), show the real part of the
literal wavelet has one sign, lie under the fixed profile, and control the
entire complementary \(d\)-range. Failure of the last condition is a
rigorous obstruction to that lower-bound proof, not evidence for (118.B8).

## 6. Promotion threshold

Promote only one of:

1. the full target (118.B8), with endpoints and owner scope stated;
2. a nonempty strict exponent region where (118.B8) is proved;
3. a quantified actual-sign saving with the exact remaining capacity;
4. a rigorous literal countermodel that controls the complement; or
5. a scoped no-go that eliminates a named method and leaves an explicit
   smaller signed survivor.

No numerical experiment is planned. Exact finite controls may diagnose or
falsify an identity but cannot certify an asymptotic theorem.
