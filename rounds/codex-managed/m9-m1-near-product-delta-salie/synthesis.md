# Round 70 synthesis: the delta method exposes a short numerator, not a free Weil saving

The exact signed \(s\)-Poisson transform localizes the circle variable to

\[
 \|\alpha\|\asymp T^{-1}.
\]

At the natural Farey order \(R=Q=J/T<T\), this support lies entirely in
the \(0/1\) cell.  The finite delta decomposition therefore collapses
identically to the existing two-dimensional Fourier self-return.

At conductor order \(J\), nonzero cells do appear, but only with

\[
 T\ll c\ll J,\qquad
 \min(a,c-a)\ll c/T,\qquad
 A_c\asymp c/T\leq Q.
\]

The exact residue algebra gives ordinary Kloosterman sums for odd
moduli only after counterfactual completion; even moduli give
conductor-\(4\) twisted analogues.  The double zero mode vanishes, while
single-zero axial modes and their gcd factors survive.  Because the
actual numerator has length below \(\sqrt c\) (since
\(c\leq J<T^2\) at the benchmark), completion plus Weil is
non-improving, and standard Kuznetsov does not apply to the resulting
moving incomplete-numerator family.

Thus Round 70 promotes an exact reduction/no-go but no estimate.  The
smallest survivor is a joint short-numerator, modulus, and dual-frequency
large-sieve inequality with the actual weights.  It must supply the same
missing factor

\[
 \frac{T}{J^{1/2}}=\frac{J^{1/2}}Q=X^{1/20}.
\]

The signed fixed-centre wavelet, global angular-radial estimate, full
cone, \(M9\)-\(M1\), \(M9\), and the final exponent remain open.
