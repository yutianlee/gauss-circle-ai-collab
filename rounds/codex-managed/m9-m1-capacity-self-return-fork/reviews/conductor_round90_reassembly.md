# Round 90 conductor reassembly and ownership review

## Linear coefficient

Summing the ordered physical pairs gives

\[
 \sum_{x\ne y}^{*}e_M\!\left(dx+n(x-y)+K(\bar x-\bar y)\right)
 =S(n+d,K;M)\overline{S(n,K;M)}-c_M(d).
\]

Thus the deep physical row is exactly the accepted centered
dual-difference coefficient, with the actual stationary multiplier and
support retained.

## Gram expansion

The Fejer expansion of the deep coefficient is the four-row symbol
used in Rounds 87--89.  The literal shift \(u=0\) is the whole global
diagonal

\[
 D\sum_{b,d}|\mathcal A_{b,d}|^2.
\]

It includes same-group, cross-group, and all Ramanujan cross/square
incidences.  It is owned once.  When a previously accepted proof uses a
full positive same-group square to majorize its signed \(u\ne0\)
subset, that diagonal is an inequality aid and not a second exact
owner.

On \(u\ne0\), the exact edge set is partitioned in the order R87,
R88, R89, hard residual, each later package intersected with all prior
complements.  Empty masks are zero.  This gives a one-count set
identity before any absolute value.

## Descent and inversion

For every full prime-power factor, complete Fourier inversion returns
the masked weight.  If depth \(j\) restricts the frequency to
\(p^j\mid u\), the factor \(p^{2j}\) in the completed trace cancels
the change from \(q^2\) to \((q/p^j)^2\).  Grouping all integer
representatives before inversion keeps every nonzero modulus multiple
and its exact Fejer weight.  Transposition supplies the opposite shift
and conjugate orientation.

Therefore the complete transforms return exactly to the deep Gram
operator.  They do not invert the Fejer/Cauchy inequality and do not
make the linear operator algebraically equal to its square.  The
reassembly, descent, sign/class, stationary-symbol, and one-count gates
pass at the Gram level.
