# Round 167 statement-only packet

Let (J=\sqrt X), (1\ll L\ll H\le J^{1/2}),
(R_0=\lceil L\rceil), and (0<\gamma<1) be fixed. Let
(c_N^{\rm rem}) be a real coefficient supported on a fixed-relative
squarefree shell (N\asymp L^2), zero elsewhere, with

\[
 \sum_N|c_N^{\rm rem}|^2\ll_\varepsilon L^2X^\varepsilon.
\]

It is the literal bounded normalized odd-divisor sum

\[
 c_N^{\rm rem}
 =\sum_{\substack{d\mid N\\2\nmid d}}
 \chi_4(d)\lambda_N^{\rm rem}(d),
\]

where (d,m=N/d\asymp L), and
(\lambda_N^{\rm rem}(d)) retains a squarefree row, a
selector depending jointly on (N,d), both parity branches, fixed smooth
profiles, finitely many hard point values, endpoints, and zero extension.

After expanding both endpoints, the frozen target is

\[
 \Re\mathfrak C_{R_0,2,{\rm opp},g<\gamma L}^{\rm rem}
 \ll_{\gamma,\varepsilon}L^2X^\varepsilon,
\tag{167.B1}
\]

where the aggregate keeps exactly even (1\le r<R_0), opposing tangent
displacements, divisor gcd (g=(d,d')<\gamma L), the Fejer factor
(1-r/R_0), and

\[
 N=dm,\qquad N+r=d'm',\qquad
 d'm'-dm=r,
\tag{167.B2}
\]

with phase (e(J(\sqrt{d'm'}-\sqrt{dm}))). The real part is outside the
complete shift and incidence aggregate. The exact opened-incidence positive
capacity is (L^3X^\varepsilon), so one factor (L) of genuine signed
cancellation is required.

Starting only from this packet, independently derive a multiplicity-
preserving determinant or orbit parameterization of (167.B2). Determine
whether a single variable-determinant oscillatory transform can preserve the
actual selector, (\chi_4(d')\chi_4(d)), parity/gcd restriction, Fejer
weight, phase, endpoints, and outer real part at the (L^2X^\varepsilon)
scale. If it cannot, prove the first exact interface or restored-power
failure and scope it to the attempted transform.

Do not use fixed-shift triangle inequalities, positive norms, or arbitrary-
array analogues as a proof of (167.B1). A phase-aligned arbitrary array is
only a filter-erased control unless it is lifted to the literal opened
incidences. Record every multiplicity, modulus, main term, error, boundary,
and power.
