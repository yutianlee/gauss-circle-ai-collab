# Round 165 conductor seed

The complete residual target is reduced to the single one-sided aggregate
correlation

\[
 \Re\mathfrak C_{R,J,L}^{\rm rem}
 \leq C_\varepsilon L^2X^\varepsilon,\qquad R=\lceil L\rceil.
\]

Its literal opening is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak C_{R,J,L}^{\rm rem}
 ={}&\sum_{1\leq r<R}\left(1-\frac rR\right)
 \sum_{\substack{d,m,d',m'\\d'm'-dm=r}}
 \mathcal W_{L,X}(d,m;d',m')\\
 &\times\chi_4(d')\chi_4(d)
 e\!\left(J(\sqrt{d'm'}-\sqrt{dm})\right),
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\mathcal W_{L,X}\) contains both supported squarefree rows,
residual selectors, normalizations, literal amplitudes, parity branches,
floors, stars, endpoints, and zero extensions.  It is zero off that domain.

The first conductor test is the integral tangent equation.  With
\(d'=d+a\), \(m'=m+b\),

\[
 db+ma+ab=r.
\]

A promising decomposition must keep the complete \(r\)-weighted real
aggregate and exhibit either:

1. a long \(\chi_4\)-sum in an exact arithmetic progression;
2. nonstationary square-root phase in an integral parameter, with
   near-integral derivatives isolated;
3. an exact sign-reversing pairing whose profile difference is
   target-safe; or
4. a spectral/delta-type shifted-convolution norm whose printed or derived
   powers remain \(O(L^2X^\varepsilon)\) after every literal coefficient
   and opening scale is restored.

Raw counts and positive norms have \(L^{2-o(1)}\) capacity.  The candidate
must fail on phase-aligned arbitrary arrays.  The first useful no-go would
identify, with exact powers, why all standard additive-shift
parameterizations lose the aggregate sign and what genuinely stronger
actual-direction theorem remains.
