## 1. Result

**Revise, do not promote the complete transform.**  The strict-interior
three-stationary normalization in (106.13) is correct, including its
Jacobian and final Gaussian unit, but the displayed dual theorem
(106.14)--(106.16) is not established for the literal actual row.  Two
corrections are mandatory even before an estimate is attempted.

First, the complete metric expansion has two stationary signs inside each
orientation.  Put

\[
 c_\tau=\nu-\tau {g\over2},\qquad
 n=2|c_\tau|,\qquad \sigma=\operatorname {sgn}(c_\tau),
 \tag{106.H1}
\]

where \(\tau=1\) denotes the displayed orientation and \(\tau=-1\) its
conjugate.  Then \(n\) is a positive odd integer.  If the positive
\(k\)-dual magnitude is \(\ell\), the actual \(k\)-Poisson label is
\(-\sigma\ell\).  After \(q=du\) and the \(u\)-dual label \(h\), write

\[
 d-2h=-\sigma r,\qquad r>0\text{ odd}.
 \tag{106.H2}
\]

The stationary value is not always the negative square in (106.14); it is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sigma\left(J\sqrt{dn\ell/r}
       -{1\over2}\sqrt{ar/d}\right)^2 .\quad}
 \tag{106.H3}
\]

For \(\sigma=-1\), this is exactly (106.10).  For \(\sigma=1\), it is
the positive square.  Merely saying "both orientations" does not repair
the display: for one fixed orientation, \(\nu\) itself crosses the sign
of \(c_\tau\).  The linear quarter phase is

\[
 e\!\left(\sigma {ar\over4d}\right)
 =\sigma i\,\chi _4(a/d)\chi _4(r)
 =-i\,\chi _4(a/d)\chi _4(-\sigma r).
 \tag{106.H4}
\]

Second, (106.13) is a strict smooth-interior principal symbol, not a
uniform complete actual amplitude.  At the physical collars, reciprocal
entry or exit, a maximal endpoint, a lift endpoint, or the collapsing
cone edge, the correct coefficient is a complete one- or multi-sided
Fresnel functional with the exact zero extension.  It cannot be replaced
by the point value \(A^\circ_{ga,gb_*}(g\ell/n)\) and the difference put
into (106.15) without a new aggregate transition theorem.

There is a still sharper owner obstruction.  After Möbius inversion,
primitivity is smooth enough for the scalar calculation, but the residual
square-ray complement is a sparse arithmetic mask in \(u\), not a
bounded-variation stationary amplitude.  For \(a=s_0^2\), its jumps occur
at

\[
 u={t^2-s_0^2\over2d},
 \tag{106.H5}
\]

and a polynomial \(q\)-shell can contain \(\asymp D/\sqrt A\) such
locations.  The accepted square-ray theorem is an aggregate signed
owner; it is not a per-\(a\) maximal theorem allowing this mask to be
deleted before the \(u\)-process.  Its exact discrete Fourier spectrum,
or the original owner-weighted \(u\)-sum, must therefore remain in the
transformed actual vector.  The same one-count issue applies to any owner
extracted in a norm different from the frozen per-base maximal norm.

Consequently the certified outcome is a **route no-go**, not an actual
lower bound: scalar stationary phase, full Hessian rank, and the terminal

Pell, near-square, square, fourth-power, exact-centre, or cone-edge family

## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses

\[
 b=a+2q,\qquad a\asymp b\asymp A,\qquad q\asymp D,

 \tag{106.H6}

Fix an arbitrary half-open maximal test interval

The exact reciprocal interval and carrier parameters are

\[
 \delta_q=\sqrt b-\sqrt a,\qquad
 \Lambda_q={X\delta_q^2\over2},

 I_{a,q}=\left({J\delta_q\over2\sqrt a},

 \tag{106.H7}

Retain both orientations, every \(\nu\in\mathbb Z\), the density

\[
 \mathbf1_{(a,q)=1}=\sum_{d\mid a,\ d\mid q}\mu(d),

Every \(d\mid a\) is odd, so \((-1)^{du}=(-1)^u\).

\[
 k_*^2={n\Lambda_{du}\over2\ell},\qquad

\[
 b_*\in a+2I,\qquad {b_*\over4}<{\ell\over n}<a,
 \tag{106.H10}

\[
 g\int A^\circ_{ga,gb}(gu)
 e\!\left(g[k u-J\delta_q\sqrt u]\right)du,

\[

  \over dJ^{1/2}n^{3/4}\ell^{1/4}}

## 3. Proof or derivation

For one orientation and one metric mode, the complete phase before the
physical integral is compressed is

\[
 {q\over2}+\sigma {n\Lambda_q\over2k}
 +gk\left(y-{J\delta_q\over2k}\right)^2.
 \tag{106.H13}
\]

The physical saddle has positive curvature.  Since \(du=2y\,dy\), its
leading factor is

\[
 e(1/8)\sqrt{2g/k_*}\,y_*
 A^\circ_{ga,gb_*}(gy_*^2).
 \tag{106.H14}
\]

In the \(k\)-Poisson convention \(f(k)-mk\), stationarity forces
\(m=-\sigma\ell\), \(\ell>0\), and

\[
 |f''(k_*)|^{-1/2}
 =2^{-3/4}(n\Lambda_{du})^{1/4}\ell^{-3/4}.
 \tag{106.H15}
\]

Its Gaussian unit is \(e(\sigma/8)\), and the reduced \(u\)-phase is

\[
 {du\over2}+\sigma J(\sqrt{a+2du}-\sqrt a)\sqrt{n\ell}.
 \tag{106.H16}
\]

After subtracting the \(u\)-dual integer \(h\), stationarity is possible
only when \(d-2h=-\sigma r\), \(r>0\) odd.  The second derivative has
sign \(-\sigma\), Gaussian unit \(e(-\sigma/8)\), and inverse square
root

\[
 {b_*^{3/4}\over dJ^{1/2}(n\ell)^{1/4}}.
 \tag{106.H17}
\]

The units multiply to

\[
 e(1/8)e(\sigma/8)e(-\sigma/8)=e(1/8).
 \tag{106.H18}
\]

There is no missing factor two or progression-density factor.  Indeed,

\[
 \sqrt{2g/k_*}\sqrt{\ell/n}\,
 2^{-3/4}(n\Lambda)^{1/4}\ell^{-3/4}
 =\sqrt{g/n},
 \tag{106.H19}
\]

and multiplication by (106.H17) gives (106.H12).  Reindexing the
integer \(h\) by the odd integer \(-\sigma r=d-2h\) is one-to-one; a
factor \(1/2\) would arise only if that odd sum were subsequently replaced
by a continuum density.

Evaluating (106.H16) at the second saddle gives

\[
 \sigma\left\{ {dXn\ell\over r}+{ar\over4d}
                 -J\sqrt{an\ell}\right\},
 \tag{106.H20}
\]

which is (106.H3).  Equation (106.H4) follows because \(a/d\) and
\(r\) are odd.  This proves every scalar stationary constant and also
shows that the \(\sigma=1\) branch cannot be hidden in the displayed
negative-square formula.

The half-density calculation (106.H19) is also the capacity warning.  The
two scalar B-process Jacobians and the physical Gaussian exactly account
for one another; there is no spare factor of \(D^{-1/2}\) or
\(D^{-1}\).  With

\[
 M_0=X^\varepsilon L^2/A,
 \tag{106.H21}
\]

rowwise absolute control has maximal capacity \(DM_0\), generic
square-root cancellation has capacity \(\sqrt D\,M_0\), and the frozen
maximal theorem requires \(M_0\).  Thus the desired Gram needs the full
factor \(D\) over rowwise capacity, equivalently an additional
\(D^{1/2}\) beyond generic square-root cancellation.  The stationary
map itself supplies neither.

The actual-symbol transfer fails to follow from the scalar calculation.
The exact centred integral was deliberately retained in Rounds 77, 103,
and 104 because it controls complete and incomplete Fresnel transitions
uniformly.  At a transition, scaling by the stationary width produces a
fixed incomplete-Fresnel functional of the collar.  Unless a separate
large-parameter comparison is proved, that functional is of the same
order as the leading Gaussian and is not determined by the single saddle
value in (106.H12).  If both physical collars overlap, the coefficient
depends jointly on both scaled distances.  If the reciprocal or maximal
saddle also reaches an endpoint, the correct object is a coupled
multi-sided transition, not a product of three independent full
Gaussians.

The owner mask gives an independent obstruction to the \(u\)-B process.
Möbius inversion (106.H8) removes the primitive mask lawfully.  The exact
metric-centre deletion changes no value because the punctured metric
factor is zero there.  Blockwise safe-ratio and diagonal owners can be
kept disjoint.  In contrast, the square-ray complement has polynomially
many isolated jumps on legal fixed-\(a\) shells, as (106.H5) shows.  A
stationary-phase theorem for a smooth amplitude cannot be applied to it.
Replacing it by its value at \(b_*\), or silently omitting it because its
global owner was previously bounded, changes both the norm and the
one-count ledger.  The exact transformed object must contain the discrete
Fourier coefficient of this sparse mask convolved with the smooth scalar
kernel.  That fixed vector has the original capacity and no accepted
estimate.

The maximal cutoff has only two original jumps, but after the
\(u\)-process it creates a principal condition \(b_*\in a+2I\), two
one-sided Gaussian transitions, equality terms, and nonstationary
endpoint tails.  Uniformity in all \(I\subset[D,2D)\) is therefore a
maximal stationary theorem, not a consequence of a whole-shell formula.
The moving reciprocal interval similarly creates integer entry and exit
terms before the \(u\)-process.  Finite lift endpoints and height/profile
edges move with \(b_*\), and their zero-extension samples must be counted
once.  None of these modules has been summed to (106.15) in the assigned
inputs.

At the scalar smooth-interior level the canonical relation is invertible:
applying the adjoint B-processes recovers the original carrier and its
half-density.  This proves an equal-capacity obstruction to claiming gain
from full Hessian rank.  It does **not** prove owner-preserving self-return.
The leading physical Gaussian has discarded collar data, while a sharp
interval and a sparse owner return through their full Fourier kernels,
not as the same pointwise owners.  Exact inversion is available only after
all stationary, transition, nonstationary, endpoint, and owner-Fourier
pieces are restored.  At that point it is an identity, not an estimate.

Finally, expand (106.H3).  For fixed outer labels the tempting dual
denominator sum has the schematic form

\[
 \sum_{r\asymp dnJ\atop r\ {\rm odd}}
 \chi_4(r)\,\mathcal W_{a,d,g,\nu,\ell,I}(r)
 e\!\left(\sigma {dn\ell X\over r}\right).
 \tag{106.H22}
\]

The literal map to `M9-M1-terminal-frequency-divisor-bound` would be

\[
 d_{M1}=r,\qquad h_{M1}=dn\ell,qquad
 D_{M1}\asymp dnJ,qquad L_{M1}\asymp dn\ell.
 \tag{106.H23}
\]

The theorem requires \(D_{M1}\le J\), so (106.H23) fails except at the
isolated boundary possibility \(d=n=1\).  More decisively,

\[
 b_*={4d^2Xn\ell\over r^2}
 \tag{106.H24}
\]

occurs inside \(\mathcal W\).  Thus the physical profiles, maximal
condition \(b_*\in a+2I\), lift support, collars, stars, and owner mask
depend jointly on \((\ell,r)\).  They do not factor as a common bounded
denominator weight times a frequency weight with
\(\|u\|_\infty+\operatorname {Var}u\ll L_{M1}^{-1}\).  Even when
\(d=n=1\), the sharp maximal and owner interfaces remain, while the
Round-60 transfer applies only after a fixed smooth compact critical
radial separation.  The terminal M1 theorem therefore has no literal
target-producing application to the complete row.  Refactoring the phase
does not repair this: the choice \(X'=(dn)^2X\), which would put
\(r\asymp\sqrt{X'}\), forces the frequency \(\ell/(dn)\), generally
nonintegral; the always legal choice \(X'=dn\ell X\), frequency one,
returns the theorem's \(1+D_{M1}\) capacity and supplies no required
power.  Termwise trivial uses are therefore irrelevant to (106.16).
