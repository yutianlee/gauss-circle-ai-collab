## 1. Exact distinction

The hierarchical partition is

\[
 1=\psi(\beta)+(1-\psi(\beta))\psi(\alpha)
 +(1-\psi(\beta))(1-\psi(\alpha)).                 \tag{33.C1}
\]

The two internal beta shares sum only to \(\psi(\beta)\). Therefore their
endpoint right-line images sum to

\[
 D_\xi^{\beta}=D_\xi[\psi(\beta)],                 \tag{33.C2}
\]

not to the unmasked \(D_\xi[1]\). The complementary alpha-only and outer
shares are required for

\[
 D_\xi[\psi(\beta)]+D_\xi[1-\psi(\beta)]=D_\xi[1].\tag{33.C3}
\]

By contrast, the moving arithmetic pole is \(A=0\), hence \(\beta=0\)
and \(\psi(0)=1\). The beta branch alone crosses the full arithmetic
residue. Full arithmetic ownership does not imply full endpoint ownership.

## 2. Consequence

The accepted physical upper/lower endpoint theorems estimate \(D_\xi[1]\)
after the symmetric profile limits. They do not automatically estimate
\(D_\xi^{\beta}\), because the beta height mask changes the physical
profile and may destroy the fixed-scale sampled-BV argument. Thus one of
the following must be proved before the endpoint modules can be routed:

1. endpoint collapse is performed on the global unmasked operator before
   the hierarchical transition partition is inserted; or
2. all three hierarchical branches are recombined at the endpoint stratum
   by (33.C3), including every connector and axial corner; or
3. an independent bound for the masked prefix \(D_\xi^{\beta}\) is proved.

The beta-branch identity alone proves the formal ownership equality
\({\cal H}[G-E_1-R_1]=0\) only if \({\cal H}\) retains
\(D_\xi^{\beta}\) as a boundary output. It does not license deleting that
output by invoking the unmasked endpoint theorem.
