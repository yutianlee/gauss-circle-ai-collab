## 3. Derivation, signed estimate and complete costs

### 3.1 The exact signs before the joint estimate

Set \(b_{h,k}=\chi_4(h)A_{h,k}\), zero outside the literal finite support. The full autocorrelation identity is

\[
 |T|^2=
 \sum_{u,v\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{h,k\in\mathbb Z}
 b_{h+u,k+v}\overline{b_{h,k}}.
 \tag{12}
\]

If \(u\) is odd, every summand vanishes. If \(u=2r\), then exactly

\[
 \chi_4(h+2r)\chi_4(h)=(-1)^r\mathbf1_{h\text{ odd}},
 \tag{13}
\]

and the summand in (12) is

\[
 (-1)^r a(h+2r,k+v)\overline{a(h,k)}
 e\left(\sqrt X\left(\sqrt{(h+2r)(k+v)}-\sqrt{hk}\right)\right).
 \tag{14}
\]

All positive and negative increments, axes, diagonals and support intersections are present. The amplitudes are exactly the product of the two slanted symbols; their moving support is not rectangularized with equality.

A useful intermediate signed quantity, without granting a bound for it, is

\[
 \begin{split}
 \sum_k\left|\sum_h\chi_4(h)A_{h,k}\right|^2
 ={}&\sum_{h\text{ odd},k}|a(h,k)|^2\\
 &+2\Re\sum_{r\ge1}(-1)^r
       \sum_{h\text{ odd},k}a(h+2r,k)\overline{a(h,k)}
       e\left(\sqrt{Xk}(\sqrt{h+2r}-\sqrt h)\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]

Thus the actual character offers a signed sum in the increment, not a small positive collision count. Both quarter shifts and their cross terms are already included because

\[
 \chi_4(h)=\frac{e(h/4)-e(3h/4)}{2i}.
 \tag{16}
\]

The alternating sign in (15) does not justify a derivative bound by distance from zero. In a continuous \(r\) variable the phase including \((-1)^r\) has derivative

\[
 \frac{\sqrt{Xk}}{\sqrt{h+2r}}+\frac12\asymp R.
 \tag{17}
\]

It crosses integer aliases. Its stationary condition against an integer \(m\) would be

\[
 h+2r=\frac{Xk}{(m-1/2)^2}
       =\frac{4Xk}{(2m-1)^2}.
 \tag{18}
\]

This elementary derivative diagnostic explains precisely why the half shift does not remove the long set of odd labels of size \(R\). It is not used as a new Poisson or stationary-phase theorem, and no contribution at these aliases is deleted.

### 3.2 The first positive loss and the mixed four-corner phase

Let \(u_h=\chi_4(h)\) on the odd rows and let \(v_k=1\) on the chosen enclosing column interval. Then \(T=u^*Av\). The tested closure is

\[
 |u^*Av|\le\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\|A\|_{\rm op}
 \le (n_hn_k)^{1/2}\bigl(\operatorname{tr}((AA^*)^2)\bigr)^{1/4}.
 \tag{19}
\]

The first inequality is the exact first loss of the actual fixed-vector signs. It replaces their interaction with an all-vector operator bound. The second inequality replaces the largest singular value by the fourth Schatten norm. Neither is claimed to be sharp for the prescribed vectors.

Expanding the still oscillatory joint quantity gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 E_4=\sum_{h_1,h_2\text{ odd}}\sum_{k_1,k_2}
 &a(h_1,k_1)\overline{a(h_2,k_1)}
   \overline{a(h_1,k_2)}a(h_2,k_2)\\
 &\times e\left(\sqrt X(\sqrt{h_1}-\sqrt{h_2})
                         (\sqrt{k_1}-\sqrt{k_2})\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{20}
\]

This is a genuine two-coordinate rectangular difference of \(\sqrt{Xhk}\). The four-corner character product is identically one on these rows. In matrix terms multiplying every row of \(A\) by \(\chi_4(h)\) is unitary and leaves \(E_4\) unchanged. Consequently (20) cannot recover the discarded sign in (13). We now estimate (20) completely, rather than assume that its off-diagonal part is small.

### 3.3 Full upper bound for the rectangle output

Write

\[
 G(h,h')=\sum_k a(h,k)\overline{a(h',k)}
                   e\left(\sqrt X(\sqrt h-\sqrt{h'})\sqrt k\right).
 \tag{21}
\]

Then \(E_4=\sum_{h,h'}|G(h,h')|^2\). The equal-row contribution is \(O(LK^2)\). For \(r=|h-h'|\ge1\), on every nonempty overlap the amplitude in (21) has supremum plus total variation \(O(1)\), and

\[
 |f''(k)|\asymp\frac{\sqrt X\,r}{\sqrt L K^{3/2}}
                =\frac{Rr}{K^2}.
 \tag{22}
\]

Apply the elementary second-derivative estimate on each interval and Abel summation, using the full discrete estimate that includes all stationary integer aliases. It gives

\[
 |G(h,h')|\ll
       \min\left(K,\sqrt{Rr}+\frac K{\sqrt{Rr}}\right).
 \tag{23}
\]

This is the same elementary estimate already licensed in the permitted dual-transform report, with a now explicitly derived difference phase and BV amplitude. It is not a new source import or a claim that (15) has been bounded with its signs.

There are \(O(L)\) pairs at each admissible \(r\le CL\). Summing the squares in (23), including the cross term, yields

\[
 E_4\ll LK^2+RL^3+
           \frac{LK^2}{R}\log(2L)+L^2K.
 \tag{24}
\]

The final term is absorbed in \(LK^2\) since \(K>L\). Odd-row parity restricts \(r\) to even integers but improves only the constant in this upper bound. No gcd assumption is made about \(h,h'\), \(k,k'\), or \(X\). In particular, primitive rows have not replaced all rows. If \(h=gu,k=gv\) is written merely for checking character bookkeeping, its coefficient is exactly \(\chi_4(g)\chi_4(u)\); even \(g\) gives zero and every odd \(g\) remains. There is no character in the original \(k\) coordinate that could legally be inserted as an extra cancellation.

The estimate covers the whole integer column interval, not one period or only small Fourier labels. For \(Rr/K^2>1\), the trivial cap in (23) remains available; the uncapped larger expression is still a valid upper bound. This explicitly includes the regime in which many aliases are present. At \(L\asymp1\), there are only \(O(1)\) rows; the same bound, or direct summation, applies. Empty intersections contribute zero.

Substitution into (19) and use of the coefficient triangle gives

\[
 |T|\ll X^\varepsilon M^{3/4}
    \min\{M^{1/4},(K+DL+(L/D)\log(2L))^{1/4}\},
 \tag{25}

because

\[
 \frac{LK^2}{M}=K,\qquad
 \frac{RL^3}{M}=DL,\qquad

 \tag{26}
\]

This proves the full cost (5), with the logarithm absorbed only in \(X^\varepsilon\). The term \(L/D\le X^{-1/4}\) is harmless but has not been omitted before restoration.

### 3.4 A proved lower buffer for actual coefficients

Choose interior points \(x_0,u_0>0\) where \(\eta(x_0)\ne0\) and \(W(u_0)\ne0\), and set \(y_0=x_0/(4u_0^2)\). Continuity supplies fixed nonempty intervals \(I,J\) about \(x_0,y_0\) on which

\[

 \quad(x\in I,y\in J).
 \tag{27}
\]

The rectangle is inside the actual slanted support. Since \(L/H_D\to0\) and \(\Phi(0)=1\), on this fixed rectangle \(\Phi(h/(H_D+1))\to1\) uniformly. The normalized power \((xy)^{-3/4}\) is bounded above and below. Thus for sufficiently large \(X\),

\[
 |a(h,k)|\ge c>0
 \quad\text{for }h/L\in I,\ k/K\in J, h\text{ odd}.
 \tag{28}
\]

There are at least \(cL\) such odd rows and \(cK\) integer columns. This proves, rather than assumes,

\[
 S_2:=\sum_{h\text{ odd},k}|a(h,k)|^2\asymp M,
 \qquad \sum|a(h,k)|\asymp M.
 \tag{29}
\]

For the positive eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) of \(AA^*\), their number is at most \(n_h\ll L\). Cauchy gives

\[
 E_4=\sum_i\lambda_i^2
       \ge\frac{(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2}{n_h}
       \gg\frac{M^2}{L}=LK^2.
 \tag{30}
\]

Together with \(n_hn_k\asymp M\), this proves (6). The actual coefficient triangle is also at least a constant times \(M\), so taking its minimum with the rectangle certificate does not remove the floor \(L^{3/4}K\le M\).
