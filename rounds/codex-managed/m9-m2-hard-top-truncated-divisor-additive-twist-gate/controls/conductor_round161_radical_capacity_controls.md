# Conductor Round-161 analytic controls

These are candidate controls for post-unmask review. They are not accepted
proof-state claims.

## 1. A target-safe long-channel sector

For \(Z\ge2\), let

\[
 \mathcal T_{\le Z}=
 \sum_{\substack{D\le Z\\D>1\ {\rm squarefree}}}
 \sum_tB_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D).
\]

The accepted fixed-channel estimate gives, without using the phase,

\[
 |\mathcal T_{\le Z}|
 \ll_\varepsilon L^\varepsilon
 \sum_{D\le Z}\left(1+\frac L{\sqrt D}\right)
 \ll_\varepsilon (Z+L\sqrt Z)L^\varepsilon.
\tag{161.K1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal T_{\le L}|
 \ll_\varepsilon L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon.}
\tag{161.K2}
\]

More generally, \(D\le L^{1-\delta}\) has the strict margin
\(L^{3/2-\delta/2+\varepsilon}\). Since active
\(Dt^2\asymp L^2\), the complement \(D>L\) has
\(t\ll\sqrt L\), up to the fixed literal support constants. Thus the
long radical channels are removable, while the short-channel and
\(t=1\) region remains.

## 2. Exact modulo-one collisions are algebraically sparse

The square roots of distinct positive squarefree integers are linearly
independent over \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed, in the multiquadratic field
generated by the finitely many relevant primes, the squarefree prime
monomials form part of the standard basis.

Fix \(J>0\). If two distinct unordered pairs satisfy

\[
 J(\sqrt{D_1}-\sqrt{D_2})\in\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 J(\sqrt{D_3}-\sqrt{D_4})\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{161.K3}
\]

then division of the two nonzero integer equations gives a rational
linear relation among the displayed square roots. Linear independence
forces the two unordered pairs to be identical. Hence among all
frequencies \(J\sqrt D\bmod1\) there is at most one exact unequal
collision pair. Likewise at most one \(D\) has
\(J\sqrt D\in\mathbb Z\), and a distinct exact collision pair cannot
coexist with such a phase-zero channel.

Every channel involved in these exact relations is individually
target-safe by the accepted fixed-\(D\) bound. This says nothing about
near collisions at the resolution required by a length-\(t\) sum.

## 3. The mandatory one-column control

On the \(t=1\) face one has \(g=u=v=1\) in the exact incidence
coordinates, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_D(1)=L^{3/2}D^{-3/4}
 \sum_{\substack{d_1d_2=D\\d_2\le d_1\le4d_2\\d_1\ {\rm odd}}}
 &\chi_4(d_1)\eta_L(d_1)
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{d_1}{H+1}\right)\\
 &\times W\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_Xd_1}{4d_2}}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.K4}
\]

with literal support and zero extension. This is a coefficient-sensitive
square-root additive twist over \(D\asymp L^2\), not a long \(t\)-sum.

For a set of \(Q\) such channels, the common-test evaluation operator on
the single column is

\[
 A:\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathbb C^Q,\qquad
 (Aa)_D=a\,e(J\sqrt D),
\]

and has exact norm \(\|A\|=\sqrt Q\), independent of all frequency
spacing. Therefore an outer positive \(D\)-norm gives only

\[
 \left|\sum_D B_D(1)e(J\sqrt D)\right|
 \le\sqrt Q\left(\sum_D|B_D(1)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{161.K5}
\]

At \(Q\asymp L^2\), the accepted global coefficient energy permits
\(L^{2+o(1)}\), not the \(L^{3/2+o(1)}\) target. This is an exact
obstruction to the positive common-test orientation, not a lower bound
for the literal left side. Closing (161.K4) requires signed cancellation
across \(D\) using its actual truncated factorization coefficient.

## 4. Optimistic common-test capacity on a dyadic block

For \(D\asymp Q>L\), active channel length is

\[
 T\asymp1+\frac L{\sqrt Q}\ll\sqrt L.
\tag{161.K6}
\]

Even for optimally spaced frequencies, a \(Q\)-by-\(T\) Fourier
evaluation matrix has squared operator scale at least \(Q\): its
Hilbert--Schmidt norm squared is \(QT\) and its rank is at most \(T\).
The ideal large-sieve scale is therefore \(Q+T\asymp Q\), not a
cross-channel contraction.

If the physical coefficient admitted a rank-one flat common-test model,
the optimistic large-sieve/Cauchy capacity would be

\[
 Q\sqrt T=L^{1/2}Q^{3/4}.
\tag{161.K7}
\]

This reaches \(L^{3/2}\) only for \(Q\le L^{4/3}\), and it gives no
gain at the \(t=1\), \(Q\asymp L^2\) face. The literal matrix is not
rank one. For a decomposition

\[
 B_D(t)=\sum_r x_r(D)c_r(t),
\]

the same route costs the projective Hilbert norm
\(\sum_r\|x_r\|_2\|c_r\|_2\), minimized as the nuclear norm of the
dyadic coefficient matrix. No accepted estimate supplies the nuclear
norm required for the target.

Equations (161.K5)--(161.K7) do not exclude a direct signed theorem in
\(D\), a nonlinear coefficient-sensitive argument, or a new bilinear
estimate for (161.K4). They only prevent a many-frequency common-test
large sieve followed by a positive outer norm from being advertised as
the missing coupling.
