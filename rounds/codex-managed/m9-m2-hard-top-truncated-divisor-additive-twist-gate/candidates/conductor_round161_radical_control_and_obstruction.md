# Conductor Round-161 radical control and common-test obstruction candidate

## 1. Result and proposed decision

Close the proposed radical-frequency common-test route under
`hard_top_radical_frequency_coupling_no_go`, while retaining three exact
candidate advances for seam review:

1. the literal radical-incidence bijection;
2. a target-safe owner-complete long-channel sector
   \(D\le CL\), equivalently every fixed \(t\ge\tau\sqrt L\) sector;
3. an exact fixed-centre collision theorem, together with a route-scoped
   one-column obstruction showing why exact collision sparsity and a
   coefficient-uniform common-test/projective norm cannot close the
   remaining short channels.

No target bound or owner-complete strict polynomial range of \(L\) is
proved. The first physical survivor is already the literal
\(t=1,D\asymp L^2\) signed close-factor scalar.

## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses

Retain the complete Round-161 frozen owner. Thus

\[
 \mathcal T_L^{\rm ns}
 =\sum_{D>1\ {\rm sf}}\sum_{t\ge1}B_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D),
\qquad
 B_D(t)=L^{3/2}(Dt^2)^{-3/4}C_L(Dt^2),
\tag{161.J1}
\]

with the literal support indicator understood. There are fixed support
constants \(0<c_-<c_+\) such that

\[
 B_D(t)\ne0\Longrightarrow
 c_-L^2\le Dt^2\le c_+L^2.
\tag{161.J2}
\]

The inherited accepted bounds are

\[
 \sum_{D,t}|B_D(t)|^2\ll L^2\log(2L),
\qquad
 \sum_t|B_D(t)|\ll_\varepsilon
 (1+L/\sqrt D)L^\varepsilon.
\tag{161.J3}
\]

No positivity, lower envelope, common row vector, bounded variation in
\(D\) or \(t\), or Diophantine condition on the arbitrary fixed real
\(J\) is assumed.

## 3. Proof and derivation

### Literal radical incidence

For one original pair, put \(g=(h,m)\), \(h=ga\), \(m=gb\), so
\((a,b)=1\). Uniquely write

\[
 a=d_1u^2,\qquad b=d_2v^2,
\]

with squarefree \(d_1,d_2\). Then

\[
 d_1d_2=D,\quad t=guv,\quad(d_1u,d_2v)=1,
\quad d_2v^2\le d_1u^2\le4d_2v^2,
\tag{161.J4}
\]

and odd \(h\) is equivalent to \(g,d_1,u\) odd. Conversely every
tuple in (161.J4), with literal support, returns one original pair. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_D(t)=\mathbf 1_{Dt^2\asymp L^2}
\sum_{\substack{d_1d_2=D,\ guv=t\\(d_1u,d_2v)=1\\
g,d_1,u\ {\rm odd}\\d_2v^2\le d_1u^2\le4d_2v^2}}
&\chi_4(gd_1)\eta_L(gd_1u^2)
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{gd_1u^2}{H+1}\right)\\
&\times\left(\frac{L^2}{Dt^2}\right)^{3/4}
 W\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_Xd_1u^2}{4d_2v^2}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.J5}
\]

This retains even \(D\): its factor 2 lies in \(d_2\). It also retains
even \(t\): its 2-part lies in \(v\). The map is bijective and has no
hidden incidence multiplicity.

### Long-channel sector

For \(Z\ge2\), (161.J3) gives

\[
 \left|\sum_{D\le Z\ {\rm sf}}\sum_tB_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D)\right|
 \ll_\varepsilon (Z+L\sqrt Z)L^\varepsilon.
\tag{161.J6}
\]

Taking \(Z=CL\), with \(C\) fixed, proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\sum_{D\le CL\ {\rm sf}}\sum_tB_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D)\right|
 \ll_{C,\varepsilon}L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon.}
\tag{161.J7}
\]

By (161.J2), every fixed \(t\ge\tau\sqrt L\) sector lies in (161.J7),
with \(C=c_+\tau^{-2}\). Conversely \(D\le CL\) implies
\(t\ge\sqrt{c_-/C}\sqrt L\). The method stops sharply at this scale:
putting \(Z=L^{1+\delta}\) into (161.J6) costs
\(L^{3/2+\delta/2+\varepsilon}\). This last comparison is a capacity
of the positive argument, not a lower bound.

### Exact collision theorem

Square roots of distinct squarefree positive integers are linearly
independent over \(\mathbb Q\). In the multiquadratic field generated by
their prime factors, they are distinct character monomials; averaging a
putative relation against each sign character isolates every coefficient.

If two unequal base-frequency collision equations exist,

\[
 J(\sqrt{D_1}-\sqrt{D_2})=k,\qquad
 J(\sqrt{D_3}-\sqrt{D_4})=\ell,
\tag{161.J8}
\]

then eliminating \(J\) yields a rational linear relation among the
displayed radicals. Disjoint pairs and one-index overlaps are impossible;
two-index overlap is only repetition or reversal. Therefore the collision
graph of \(J\sqrt D\bmod1\) has at most one unequal unordered edge.

The same elimination applied to

\[
 J(t_1\sqrt{D_1}-t_2\sqrt{D_2})=k,
 \qquad D_1\ne D_2,\quad k\ne0,
\tag{161.J9}
\]

shows that all cross-channel atom relations use at most one unordered
radical pair. On that pair their coefficient triples are rational
multiples of one primitive relation. If \(D_1=D_2=D\), the equation is
\((t_1-t_2)J\sqrt D=k\): \(t_1=t_2\) is the trivial same-atom loop,
whereas a nontrivial repetition can occur only on a rational channel.
Also at most one channel has \(J\sqrt D\in\mathbb Q\). Such a channel
cannot share an endpoint with a cross-channel relation, since
\(J\sqrt{D_0}=r\in\mathbb Q\) and (161.J9) would give

\[
rt_1\sqrt{D_1}-rt_2\sqrt{D_2}-k\sqrt{D_0}=0,
\tag{161.J9a}
\]

contrary to square-root independence. Every channel in this exact classification is
target-safe by (161.J3). None of these qualitative identities gives a
positive lower bound for a nonzero modulo-one gap or controls a
near-resonant collar.

### One-column common-test obstruction

On \(t=1\), (161.J5) becomes

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_D(1)=\mathbf1_{D\asymp L^2}\left(\frac{L^2}{D}\right)^{3/4}
\sum_{\substack{d_1d_2=D\\d_1\ {\rm odd}\\d_2\le d_1\le4d_2}}
&\chi_4(d_1)\eta_L(d_1)
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{d_1}{H+1}\right)\\
&\times W\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_Xd_1}{4d_2}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.J10}
\]

This is a literal signed close-factor coefficient. Close semiprimes can
have one eligible incidence, so no coefficientwise divisor cancellation
is available; no family lower mass is asserted.

Let \(E_Jc(D)=\sum_tc(t)e(tJ\sqrt D)\). For a Hilbert-projective
decomposition \(A=\sum_rx_r\otimes c_r\), common-test Bessel plus
triangle gives

\[
 |\Lambda_J(A)|\le\|E_J\|_{2\to2}\|A\|_{S_1}.
\tag{161.J11}
\]

On a positive-relative-width set of squarefree
\(D\asymp L^2\), define only for the hostile method control

\[
 A_D(t)=1_{t=1}e(-J\sqrt D).
\tag{161.J12}
\]

There are \(\asymp L^2\) such squarefree \(D\). The array has the
coarse support, global \(L^2\) energy, and unit channel mass allowed by
(161.J3), while

\[
 \|A\|_{S_1}\asymp L,\qquad
 \|E_Je_1\|_2\asymp L,\qquad
 \Lambda_J(A)\asymp L^2.
\tag{161.J13}
\]

Thus the positive common-test/projective route is sharp at
\(L^{2+o(1)}\) already in one column and misses the target by
\(L^{1/2-o(1)}\). Equation (161.J12) is not the physical coefficient
(161.J10). It proves only that support, (161.J3), exact-collision
sparsity, and a coefficient-uniform common-test theorem do not imply the
target. A new theorem must use an exact property of (161.J10) that fails
for (161.J12).

following named-route conclusion. Montgomery--Vaughan Theorem 1 uses one
common coefficient vector. Bombieri--Iwaniec Lemma 2.4 uses separable
coefficients and charges absolute near-collision forms. Robert--Sargos

\[
 J^{1/4}L^{3/2+\varepsilon}+L^{7/4+\varepsilon}
 +L^{3/2+\varepsilon}+J^{-1/2}L^{3/2+\varepsilon},
\tag{161.J13a}
\]

so its best combination with triviality remains \(L^2\) for

be. These are exact no-matches for the four audited interfaces, not a
claim about all literature or future coefficient-sensitive theorems.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

After removing (161.J7) and the finitely many exact-collision channels,
the first unresolved physical statement in this decomposition is

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\sum_{D\asymp L^2\ {\rm sf}}
 B_D(1)e(J\sqrt D)\right|
 \ll_\varepsilon L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon.}
\tag{161.J14}
\]

There is no nontrivial \(t\)-sum on this face. Proving (161.J14) needs
signed cancellation across \(D\) for the actual close-factor coefficient,
or an equivalent alignment-sensitive theorem. The accepted energy gives
only \(L^{2+o(1)}\) after positive Bessel placement. No lower mass is
known, so (161.J14) remains a possible theorem. The rest of the
\(L\ll D\ll L^2\), \(t\ll\sqrt L\) sector would still require a
compatible joint estimate.
