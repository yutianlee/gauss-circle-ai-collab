## 3. Proof and equation-by-equation derivation

**Unfactored three-gradient determinant.**  On a fixed residue sheet, with \(\lambda\) fixed while differentiating the outer variables,

\[
 {2\over R}\nabla_{h,k,k'}\Theta
 =G_0=\left({1\over v}-u,\ v,\ -{1\over u}\right)
 =v(a,1,b).
 \tag{136.P2}
\]

Subtracting the first row from the second and third rows gives exactly

\[
 \det(\nabla\Theta_1,\nabla\Theta_2,\nabla\Theta_3)
 ={R^3\over8}v_1v_2v_3
 \bigl[(a_3-a_1)(b_2-b_1)-(a_2-a_1)(b_3-b_1)\bigr].
 \tag{136.P3}
\]

Thus blind (3.1) is correct, including its sign convention.  At \(\delta=L^{-1}\), a slope-triangle area \(\gg\delta^2\) gives

\[
 |\det(\nabla\Theta_1,\nabla\Theta_2,\nabla\Theta_3)|
 \gg R^3\delta^2\asymp L^9L^{-2}=L^7.
 \tag{136.P4}
\]

This certifies an unfactored geometric number and nothing more.

**Gauge reconciliation: gradients are not normals.**  Identity (136.H1) rewrites the same lattice atom as

\[
 e(\Theta)
 =-i(-1)^m\chi_4(d)\chi_4(h)
   e\!\left(R\sqrt{hk}-{Xk'\over2\lambda}\right).
 \tag{136.P5}
\]

Writing \(r=v^{-1}\) and \(z=u\), the two normalized gradient surfaces are

\[
 \Sigma_0(r,z)=(r-z,r^{-1},-z^{-1}),\qquad
 \Sigma_1(r,z)=(r,r^{-1},-z^{-1}).
 \tag{136.P6}
\]

The factored gradient vector is also

\[
 G_1=v(v^{-2},1,-c^{-1}),
\]

so \(D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{grad}}\) is the corresponding triangle-area determinant in \((v^{-2},-c^{-1})\) and is not identically zero.  In contrast,

\[
 \partial_r\Sigma_1=(1,-r^{-2},0),\qquad
 \partial_z\Sigma_1=(0,0,z^{-2}),\qquad
 N(r,z)\parallel(1,r^2,0),
 \tag{136.P7}
\]

and hence \(D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{nor}}=0\) for every triple, exactly as in (136.A16).  Therefore (136.A17) is correct for the discovery report's explicitly normal-based partition (136.A13), but it does not say that the blind cubic partition has no broad gradient triples.  Conversely, blind (3.1) cannot be called a gauge-invariant “physical determinant”: (136.P5) moves its \(-z\) coordinate into a literal arithmetic coefficient without changing the lattice sum.

**Fixed-\(Q\) ruling and both gates.**  The blind line pullback is also exact:

\[

 \tag{136.P8}

\(\alpha c-\beta v^2+\gamma cv^2=0\), so arbitrary line rulings are gauge-specific.  The horizontal ruling is exceptional and robust:

\[

 \Delta=hk{A^2-c^2\over c^2}.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first failed seam is not the algebra in blind (3.1).  It is the implication

\[
 |D^{(0)}_{\mathrm{grad}}|\gg L^7
 \quad\centernot\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{any power saving in }|\mathcal K_B^{\mathrm{bulk}}|.
 \tag{136.P15}
\]

At a fixed block, \(\mathcal K_B^{\mathrm{bulk}}\) is one complex scalar.  There is no external spatial integral, averaged block variable, multilinear restriction norm, or lattice theorem in the reports under which (136.P3) becomes a Jacobian or an orthogonality gain and which then returns to that scalar without an unpaid outer norm.  The exact gauge (136.P5)--(136.P7) further shows that a coefficient-modulation-invariant theorem cannot price \(D^{(0)}_{\mathrm{grad}}\): the same lattice sum has a factored normal surface with identically vanishing three-normal determinant.  This does not exclude a future Weyl/Poisson estimate for the specific literal coefficients; it excludes only the claimed automatic determinant route.

The first independent narrow seam is the absence of a signed estimate for (136.P13), jointly with \(b_B(h,k)\), the slanted profiles, both gates, and the outer \(\chi_4(h)\).  The full carrier is constant in the lift multiplier, so neither residue phases nor distinctness of the lifts supplies the missing cancellation.  Neither (136.P14) nor the unverified physical-amplitude premise behind the use of (136.A18) fills this seam.
