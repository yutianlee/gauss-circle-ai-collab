# Isolated finite and summable sequence problem

For a finite or absolutely summable family of real numbers x_i let Z=sum_i x_i, V=sum_i x_i^2, and let E_m be the sum of x_(i1)...x_(im) over all ordered m-tuples of pairwise distinct indices. For each fixed even integer m>=2 and delta>0, decide whether there is a constant C_(m,delta), independent of the family size and values, such that

    |Z^m-E_m| <= delta |Z|^m + C_(m,delta) V^(m/2).

Give a self-contained finite proof or counterexample, an explicit constructive constant if possible, and a rigorous infinite-family passage. Determine the resulting integral comparison on a finite measure space when the family varies measurably, including the fact that the signed integral of E_m may be negative. State sufficient convergence/dominating hypotheses for the integral statement. Do not assume the claimed inequality is true. Test zero families, fewer than m indices, equal positive values, opposite signs, m2 and large |Z|/sqrt(V). Distinguish fixed m from m growing with a parameter. Explain what information remains missing for any application to a bound on the signed integral.
