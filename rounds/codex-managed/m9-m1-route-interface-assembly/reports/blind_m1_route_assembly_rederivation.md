## 1. Result

**Conditional route-separation and outside-complement lemma (with a formal no-go).** Relative to the frozen reductions and owner menu, the direct blockwise route closes exactly after the two still-unowned classes in the lifted residual set are supplied: the hard-profile middle/lower signed-cone estimates and the literal smooth-profile residual estimates. Assuming the canonical first-band actual-symbol Gram theorem does not remove either class, because its statement supplies no reduction to a literal physical block. Thus, with \(A_{\rm dir}\) denoting all accepted direct owners,

\[
 A_{\rm dir}+H_{\rm cone}(\widehat{\mathcal U}_1)
 +S_{\rm lit}(\widehat{\mathcal U}_1)
 \Longrightarrow \mathrm{M9\!-\!M1}_{\rm blockwise}.
\]

Here “exactly” means necessary and sufficient for closure of this frozen owner tree, not that these particular estimates are logically necessary for every conceivable proof.

On the alternative route, the canonical theorem owns only the transition-flattened smooth nonaxial principal first-band packet. The smallest additional global hypothesis is one target-safe estimate for the actual-symbol residual left after subtracting that packet and every already accepted global packet. Its certified outside survivors are the lower-radial signed packets, upper conductors, axial and raw-transition pieces, cone/radial edges, the alpha transition, and sharp radial/top/interface samples. A bound for the full global angular-radial sum implies a **total active-M1** node after the accepted transform and tail errors, not the blockwise M9-M1 node. It can feed M9 only through a separate downstream assembly bridge.

Consequently, the canonical theorem alone has no certified implication to blockwise M9-M1. Likewise, a global total identity cannot imply all individual dyadic bounds: cancellation between distinct blocks gives an immediate abstract countermodel. Any edge asserting either implication without an additional bridge is invalid at this interface.

## 2. Exact statement and hypotheses

Let \(X\) be large and real, \(y=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\), and \(H_D=\lfloor DX^{-1/4}\rfloor\). All assertions below retain both frequency signs, the clipped terminal frequency block, every physical profile, floors, stars, character factors, half weights, support crossings, and real-\(X\) endpoints.

Put

\[
 \Omega=\{(\delta,\ell):1/4\leq\delta\leq1/2,\
          0\leq\ell\leq\delta-1/4\}.
\]

The following four sets are disjoint and cover \(\Omega\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf T&=\{(\delta,\ell)\in\Omega:\ell=\delta-1/4\},\\
 \mathsf W&=\{(\delta,\ell)\in\Omega:\ell<\delta-1/4,\
                   178\ell+1638\delta\leq463\},\\
 \mathsf P&=\{(1/2,0)\},\\
 \mathsf U&=\{(\delta,\ell)\in\Omega:\ell<\delta-1/4,\
                   178\ell+1638\delta>463\}\setminus\mathsf P.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\mathsf U=\mathcal U_1\). Lift \(\mathsf U\) from exponent labels to literal dyadic blocks with their actual physical profiles and all of the retained decorations; call the lift \(\widehat{\mathsf U}\). Let \(\widehat{\mathsf H}\) be the members on the unique profile containing \(d=y\), and let \(\widehat{\mathsf S}=\widehat{\mathsf U}\setminus\widehat{\mathsf H}\). This definition is profile-theoretic: it is not the condition \(\delta=1/2+o(1)\).

The accepted direct hypothesis \(A_{\rm dir}\) consists of the unexpanded bottom \(d\ll X^{1/4}\), all Fejer residuals through R5-Full, the frequency-first divisor owner on \(\mathsf T\), the full second-derivative owner on \(\mathsf P\), the TTY owner on \(\mathsf W\), and the accepted hard-profile Poisson reduction with its boundary/error/half-weight/real-even bookkeeping. The two additional direct hypotheses are:

- \(H_{\rm cone}\): for every still-unowned hard middle/lower frequency shell in \(\widehat{\mathsf H}\), the correctly signed transform satisfies
  \[
    \mathcal T_{\mathrm{M1},L}
    =\sum_{h\asymp L}\sum_{\substack{4h<n<16h\\n\ {\rm odd}}}
       \chi_4(n)a_{\mathrm{M1}}(h,n)e(\sqrt{Xhn})
    \ll_\varepsilon L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon .
  \]
  The terminal hard shell and the isolated point are not included again, since their direct owners are accepted.
- \(S_{\rm lit}\): uniformly for every literal smooth block in \(\widehat{\mathsf S}\),
  \[
       B_1(D,L;X)\ll_\varepsilon X^{1/4+\varepsilon}.
  \]

For the canonical hypothesis, set \(J=X^{1/2}\), \(Q=J^{2/5}\), and \(T=J^{3/5}\). Assume only the actual-symbol Gram estimate

\[
 |\mathcal E_{\rm hard}(U)|
 \ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\frac{U}{B}J^{14/5}
\]

for the single transition-flattened smooth nonaxial principal packet with \(J^{13/18}<C_{\rm cond}\leq J^{3/4}\), together with the packet's accepted preliminary owners and Toeplitz bridge. Denote the resulting owned packet by \(\mathsf F_{\rm can}\). No literal physical block, hard top cone, upper conductor, axis, raw transition, other radial sector, alpha interface, or endpoint estimate is part of this hypothesis.

On the global route, let \(\mathsf G_X\) denote the actual expression

\[
 \Re\!\left\{e(1/8)\sum_{n\leq16\sqrt X}
 \mathcal C_X^*(n)n^{-3/4}e(\sqrt{Xn})\right\}.
\]

After the accepted global decompositions, define \(\mathsf G_{\rm out}\) algebraically to be \(\mathsf G_X\) minus all accepted critical-interior and auxiliary packets and minus the packet \(\mathsf F_{\rm can}\), without replacing \(\mathcal C_X^*(n)\) except where a target-safe replacement is explicitly accepted. The minimal global outside hypothesis is

\[
                  \mathsf G_{\rm out}\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon.
\]

At the resolution certified by the statement packet, its survivor set is exhausted, with priority used to prevent overlap, by: sharp \(n=16\sqrt X\) and other top/support/interface samples; the alpha transition; cone edges and radial sectors outside the accepted compact critical interior; lower radial blocks \(n\asymp X^\nu\) for \(0<\nu<1/2\); axial and raw-transition terms; and smooth nonaxial principal upper conductors \(C_{\rm cond}>J^{3/4}\). For \(0<\nu\leq2/5\), the lower-radial leaf may use the accepted replacement by \(\mathcal D(n)\), but the signed \(\mathcal D\)-sum is still the required estimate. For \(2/5<\nu<1/2\), the signed limiting cone and its replacement correction are separate leaves. The hard-top scale \((\delta,\ell)=(1/2,\nu/2)\) survives for every such \(\nu\), while the TTY wedge owns only a low-\(D\) prefix when \(\nu\geq356/819\).

## 3. Proof or derivation

For the direct tree, first keep the bottom and every R5-Full residual outside the active Fourier triangle. Inside \(\Omega\), a point is either on the terminal line \(\mathsf T\) or lies strictly below it. A strict point satisfies exactly one of the two TTY inequalities. The weak inequality puts it in \(\mathsf W\); under the strict reverse inequality it is either the isolated point \(\mathsf P\) or lies in \(\mathsf U\). This proves the displayed disjoint partition and gives one-count ownership before any analytic estimate is invoked.

The direct owner tree is:

    blockwise M9-M1
    |-- unexpanded bottom                                      [accepted]
    |-- Fejer residuals through R5-Full                        [accepted]
    +-- active triangle Omega
        |-- terminal line T: frequency-first divisor owner     [accepted]
        |-- TTY interior W                                     [accepted]
        |-- isolated point P: full second derivative           [accepted]
        +-- residual U
            |-- unique profile containing d=y
            |   +-- signed Poisson cone, middle/lower shells    [H_cone]
            +-- every other full smooth profile                 [S_lit]

The accepted one-sided Poisson formula is a reduction on the hard leaf. With its target-safe errors restored, \(H_{\rm cone}\) supplies the required physical hard-block bound. The hypothesis \(S_{\rm lit}\) supplies the bound directly on every smooth residual block. The four-way partition then proves the direct conditional implication. Conversely, within this frozen tree, deleting either new leaf leaves at least one literal residual block without an owner. This is the owner-theoretic necessity claimed in Section 1.

The canonical packet \(\mathsf F_{\rm can}\) has no branch in this tree. Its hypotheses specify a transformed, smooth, nonaxial, transition-flattened conductor band, whereas a member of \(\widehat{\mathsf U}\) is a literal physical block. No equality, target-safe reduction, or uniform reverse bridge between the two is among the hypotheses. Formally, the packet statements may be regarded as independent predicates: a valuation in which the canonical predicate is true and either \(H_{\rm cone}\) or \(S_{\rm lit}\) is false satisfies all stated canonical hypotheses but does not close the direct tree. Hence the claimed direct implication cannot be derived from the frozen packet.

The alternative global tree is:

    total active M1
    +-- accepted global transform and inactive-tail errors
        +-- actual global angular-radial estimate G_X
            |-- compact smooth critical radial sectors          [accepted]
            |-- first-band smooth nonaxial principal packet     [canonical]
            +-- exact actual-symbol remainder G_out
                |-- lower radial signed packets                 [open]
                |-- upper conductors                            [open]
                |-- axes and raw transitions                    [open]
                |-- cone/other-radial edges                     [open]
                +-- alpha, sharp endpoint, top/interface terms  [open]

The accepted transform plus \(\mathsf G_X\ll X^\varepsilon\) proves the total active-M1 target. It cannot prove a uniform estimate for each \(B_1(D,L;X)\): abstractly, two physical block contributions can be \(A\) and \(-A\), so their global sum obeys the target while either block is arbitrarily large. Thus the correct immediate child above the global estimate is total-M1, not blockwise M9-M1. A later M9 conclusion requires a separately stated assembly bridge and all of that bridge's other hypotheses.

Radial, conductor, axial/transition, and alpha cuts are mandatory bookkeeping interfaces if the global remainder is proved packetwise. They are not dependencies of the direct proof. Conversely, \(H_{\rm cone}\) and \(S_{\rm lit}\) are direct-route leaves and are not additional hypotheses once the full global estimate is assumed. A hard-top cone can instead be chosen as an owner for part of a lower-radial global leaf, but then that actual term must be removed from the remaining global leaf rather than counted twice. “Near-product” and “product-wavelet” have no exact statement or support map in the permitted packet, so they cannot be promoted as mandatory owner nodes here; at most they are alternative refinements inside a transformed global leaf after an explicit one-count reduction is supplied.

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first failed step in any attempted inference from the canonical theorem to blockwise M9-M1 is the missing bridge from \(\mathsf F_{\rm can}\) to a literal member of \(\widehat{\mathsf U}\). After returning to the valid direct tree, the first analytic gaps are precisely \(H_{\rm cone}\) on the hard middle/lower shells and \(S_{\rm lit}\) on the smooth residual profiles.

For the global tree, the first analytic gap after the canonical and accepted critical packets are removed is the estimate for the actual-symbol remainder \(\mathsf G_{\rm out}\); the lower-radial signed sum already furnishes an explicit surviving subcase. There is also a formal granularity limit: the packet does not give disjoint support definitions for every conductor/axis/transition/alpha label, nor any definition of near-product or product-wavelet packets. Therefore the exact algebraic residual is well-defined, and the priority list above prevents conceptual double ownership, but a mechanically checkable finer global partition cannot be certified from the permitted context alone.
