## 3. Proof and hostile derivation

The complement calculation in (2.2) is immediate from the weak TTY
inequality, the strict residual inequality, and the terminal boundary.  The
only overlap among the displayed accepted main owners is the terminal/TTY
overlap already removed in the definition of \(\mathcal W\).  Refinement by
the actual profile containing \(y\) proves (2.3), whereas a refinement by
\(\delta\) would put smooth and hard profiles in the same class.

On \(\mathcal T\), the frequency-first divisor theorem gives the target for
every profile, including the hard one.  The singleton \((1/2,0)\) has its
accepted full second-derivative owner, and \(\mathcal W\) has the accepted
TTY owner.  The bottom and Fejer pieces have their separate accepted owners.
The hard-top transform together with (2.4) bounds precisely the remaining
hard physical blocks, and (2.5) bounds precisely the remaining smooth
blocks.  Hence every literal block has exactly one target-sized owner, which
proves (1.1) after the harmless logarithmic number of dyadic labels is
absorbed into \(X^\varepsilon\).  A whole-\(\mathcal U_1\) estimate such as
the stated cross-product odd-kernel target or RCS target would instead own
both pieces of (2.3); adding (2.4) and (2.5) to it would count the same
physical blocks twice.

The canonical statement itself proves only a child in the transformed
product-wavelet/conductor branch.  In that branch the accepted fixed-interior
owners reach \(C\leq J^{13/18}\).  In the first band, transition errors,
axes, the zero mode, collars, and the previously removed same-group/coarse/
good-prime/bad-prime packages have separate owners; the assumed canonical
theorem supplies only the residual smooth nonaxial principal packet.  For
\(J^{3/4}<C\leq J\), the principal main, transitions, and axes remain open.
Cone edges and other radial, alpha, and top sectors are not members of the
canonical packet.

This conductor branch is nested inside the global radial architecture; it
is not an additional summand beside its parent.  The global root is the
accepted exact angular recombination.  Fixed smooth critical sectors
\(n\asymp\sqrt X\), compactly supported inside \((0,16\sqrt X)\), are
closed.  Every \(0<\nu<1/2\) retains an open hard-top scale.  For
\(0<\nu\leq2/5\), replacement by the one-sided divisor coefficient has a
target-safe error but its signed radial sum is open; for \(2/5<\nu<1/2\),

\(n=16\sqrt X\) endpoint, the limit \(n/\sqrt X\to0\), the connector-complete

## 4. First doubtful or unproved step

The first invalid step in any proposed canonical-to-blockwise proof is the
inference

\[
 \text{canonical first-band transformed packet}
 \Longrightarrow \text{all smooth physical }\mathcal U_1\text{ blocks}.
\]

No such bridge is stated or proved.  It would have to preserve individual
\((D,L)\) localization, actual profiles, floors, stars, signs, support
crossings, and real-\(X\) endpoints while exhibiting a disjoint inverse owner
map.  The accepted global recombination deliberately removes the \(L\)
partition and therefore cannot provide that conclusion without new
information.

For the direct route, the first genuinely unproved inequalities are (2.4)
and (2.5).  For the global route after assuming the canonical theorem, the
unproved branches are the upper-conductor principal/transition/axis packet,
the lower-radial signed cone (and its correction above \(\nu=2/5\)), the
alpha transition, and the sharp radial/top/interface packets.  Before these
can be advertised as a sufficient complement, the missing global one-count
parent/child assembly must itself be proved.  This is an interface gap, not
an estimate supplied by the canonical Gram.
