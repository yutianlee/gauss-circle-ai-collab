## 1. Result and selection verdict

The UNBAL surface is mathematically live, but it is not the preferred
Round-181 objective unless the other fresh owners offer no smaller exact
interface. The reason is precise: after Rounds 107, 118, 123--125, 134--135,
143, and 160, there is no unspent scalar transform, scale variable,
positive-energy contraction, short spectral band, or low-projective-cost
reciprocity decomposition. The first lawful positive statement is already a
complete signed literal-matrix vector theorem for the whole strict-UNBAL
owner. It is high leverage, but substantially broader than a bounded local
kernel.

Thus the recommendation is:

1. **Do not select UNBAL merely to retry Poisson, reciprocity, scalar
   Kuznetsov, a positive Gram norm, or a large sieve.** Those routes have
   exact self-return or capacity obstructions.
2. **If Round 181 deliberately accepts a high-risk owner-complete attack,**
   freeze the exact theorem in Section 2 and require a genuinely joint
   matrix argument before every positive norm.
3. **Do not substitute a friendly sub-sector** such as (h=mj), a fixed
   (g), a short Linnik band, or a smooth interior. No accepted connector
   makes any one of those sectors sufficient for UNBAL.

This is a strategy conclusion, not an impossibility theorem. Round 160
explicitly leaves a bespoke signed vector theorem open.

## 6. No-repeat barriers and false controls

The following mechanisms are closed at their reviewed scopes and cannot be
the advertised novelty of Round 181.

1. Product regrouping followed by the matching (h)-process and
   (k)-Poisson summation returns the same width-(D/L) fixed-centre wave.
2. A second reciprocal B-process or the joint stationary Legendre transform
   returns the primal reciprocal phase and profile.
3. The exact shifted Gram zero alias and exact nonzero aliases are safe, but
   the nonzero nonexact aggregate remains; this reduction is not its bound.
4. Fejer positivity gives only the safe negative side. The needed upper
   bound is the actual-character energy, and separating character rows is
   coefficient-blindly false.
5. The optimal folded majorant pays every bandwidth contraction through
   its zeroth mass; lagwise modulus restores the full factor.
6. Smooth-weight-first completion gives Ramanujan/additive self-return;
   inverse-selector-first completion produces the unresolved joint matrix.
7. Complete (h\bmod n) summation reconstructs the original reciprocal
   row exactly. The shorter displayed (j)-modulus is not a gain.
8. Exact rank-one scalarization followed by triangle pays the nuclear norm
   (K^{1/2-o(1)}); grouping (h\bmod j) pays the sharp
   (Delta^{1/2}) compression norm.
9. The positive frequencies (h=mj<n) remain after the single
   (h=0\pmod n) deletion and have trivial short inverse phase. They may
   not be discarded or relabeled as the safe zero row.
10. Scalar fixed-index Kuznetsov, row Parseval, complete Kloosterman second
    moment, a short Linnik band, Mellin common tests, and the audited
    Bettin--Chandee/Wright-type scalar maps do not match the full moving
    weighted matrix at an owner-saving norm.
11. An arbitrary-coefficient vector or operator-norm theorem is stronger
    than required and encounters the exact full-rank controls. The target
    is only for the literal actual family; conversely, an unweighted
    capacity is not a lower bound for that family.
12. A flat-interior theorem, one (g)-stratum, one residue band, or the
    (h=mj) spine alone has no accepted implication to the complete UNBAL
    parent.

The first repeated barrier ends the route; the round should record the
smallest new no-go rather than continue through another invertible
reparameterization.

## 7. Only plausible genuinely new mechanism

The remaining mechanism is a **nonseparable actual-family vector estimate**
which treats the full ((g,n,j,h)) coefficient in (181.U7) before any
positive modulus, residue, frequency, or spectral norm. A concrete attack
would block (h=qj+s) while retaining both (q) and (s), and would use
cross-((n,j)) cancellation of the actual vectors

\[
 \chi_4(n)S(N_0,qj+s;n)
 e\!\left(\frac{(qj+s)\overline n_j}{j}
          -\frac{qj+s}{jn}\right)
 \tag{181.U12}
\]

together with the moving literal profile. The desired gain must arise from
joint incompatibility between the Kloosterman vector, inverse-residue
matrix, and physical support, not from the unitary inverse-residue matrix
alone. In operator language, one needs a literal-vector (TT^*) or
bilinear trace estimate whose off-diagonal kernel retains the outer
(chi_4(g)\chi_4(n)) signs and yields (X^{\mu(a)}) beyond (181.U9).

This is plausible only as a research direction. No accepted identity or
audited theorem presently supplies the first saving, and the hostile spine
(g=1, h=mj) forces the proof to obtain cancellation from
(S(N_0,mj;n)), the (n,j) variables, or the literal amplitudes.

## 8. Three orthogonal Round-181 tasks if UNBAL is chosen

### Task A: literal joint-vector attack

Starting from (181.U6)--(181.U7), derive one complete (TT^*), bilinear,
or trace kernel without scalarizing the inverse-residue matrix and without
taking absolute values in (g,n,j,h). Attempt (181.U6) uniformly over
(181.U3), with an explicit (X^{\mu(a)}) ledger. Stop at the first norm or
boundary cost that repays the gain.

### Task B: hostile spine and capacity audit

Test every proposed vector inequality on primitive (g=1), all
(h=mj<n), high additive frequencies, arbitrary ((N_0,n)), and moving
support endpoints. Determine whether the proposed theorem is genuinely
literal or silently uniform over adversarial coefficients. Prove either a
target-safe bound for every mandatory hostile component plus its exact
connector, or a route-scoped no-go for the proposed joint norm. Do not
infer a lower bound from capacity.

### Task C: statement-only normalization and owner rederivation

From the statement of (181.U1), independently rederive the flat centered
matrix, the (1/(gnj)) factor, character placement, exactly-once (h=0)
removal, and the implication back to the product-phase target. Audit the
profile, floor, star, transition, and endpoint terms which separate the
flat theorem from the complete parent. This task must not inherit the
discovery derivation.

These tasks are orthogonal: Task A seeks a positive theorem, Task B attacks
its sharpest false controls, and Task C owns the normalization and
promotion seam.

## 10. Comparison with the exhausted K17a and K26 routes

K17a, K26, and UNBAL now share one strategic fact: none has an automatic
coordinate left which supplies the missing power.

- **K17a after Round 179:** low conductor and fixed-proportion sectors are
  safe, but the high centered primitive defect self-returns. Positive
  capacity is (Lq\log(2q)), one square root leaves
  (L\sqrt q\log(2q)), and the target is (L).
- **K26 after Round 180:** the row diagonal and exact product collisions are
  safe, but restoring them makes the off-row estimate target-equivalent to
  the original local scalar concentration. Local positive capacity is
  (L^2) against (L), and the endpoint capacity is (L^4) against
  (L^3).
- **UNBAL:** scalar reciprocity, completion, positive energy, and spectral
  scalarization are parked. The missing factor is (X^{\mu(a)}) beyond
  the best accepted physical envelope, or (M^{1/4}) from raw dual
  positivity. Only a complete actual-family vector theorem remains.

UNBAL has greater immediate owner leverage: a proof closes one of the three
mandatory M2 parents, whereas either K17a or K26 closes only one sufficient
route inside a residual hard-TOP (t=1) channel. Its cost is breadth: it
quantifies over the full strict ((\delta,\ell)) region, long frequencies,
moving moduli and endpoints, and variable spectral levels. That breadth is
the rigorous reason to rank it below a genuinely smaller fresh owner
interface for a single analytic round, while retaining it above any repeat
of the automatic K17a/K26 mechanisms.
