# Isolated signed Fourier transport question

For any finite real signed measure (or complex measure with conjugate symmetry) mu_T on the frequency line, define F_T(R)=integral e(Rx)dmu_T(x) and L_T(mu)=integral w(R/T)F_T(R)dR=T integral w_hat(-Tx)dmu_T(x).

Determine rigorously the best useful elementary transfer from discrepancy of mu_T against a smooth reference density to L_T. In particular: (1) derivatives and signs in integration by parts with cumulative distribution discrepancy; (2) smooth continuum densities of form A_T L_T^(-1) q(x/L_T), fixed Schwartz q, L_T comparable sqrt(T), A_T<=T^3, and whether their contribution is negligible because the physical window avoids zero; (3) exact smoothing subtraction using a Schwartz kernel whose Fourier transform is supported near zero; (4) which local/global discrepancy norm and scale would suffice for |L_T|<=T^(29/25+eps), and whether this follows just from total mass O(T^3) and cumulative one-factor square-root error. Treat finite atoms, endpoints, nonzero total mass, constants and tails explicitly. Do not conflate signed integral with its absolute certificate.

Control: a modulated smooth density that has the same coarse density law but a Fourier peak at a physical point R~T. Quantify its mass/discrepancy/integrated contribution and the resolution necessary to detect it. This is an abstract information-content control, not an arithmetic counterexample. If useful, formulate products/convolutions of 12 measures, but do not assume probabilistic independence. No numerical experiments. Seven-section report.
