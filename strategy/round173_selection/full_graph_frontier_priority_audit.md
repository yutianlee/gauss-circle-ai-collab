### 3.2 Why the ranking selects K26 despite weaker leverage

The ranking uses four conjunctive tests.

1. **Exact round-sized interface.** K26 has one finite signed object
   (173.K26*) with no unresolved definition, multiplicity, parity, link, or
   ordinary-zero seam. UNBAL, all-label M1, remaining-label BAL, and GAR
   quantify over substantially wider families.
2. **Newly isolated first failure.** Round 172 determines precisely where
   a proof must depart from coefficient-uniform positivity. The surviving
   whole-chain and literal-coefficient possibilities have not yet received
   a dedicated identity-or-no-go round.
3. **Falsifiability.** The selected-pair zero-mass identity, the no-pair
   positive control, the dechirped \(L^4\) array, and the one-site
   recombination control give immediate tests. A false route can be
   terminated rigorously without drifting into another frontier.
4. **Bounded cost of failure.** Failure closes a natural residual route
   more sharply. Round 173 is immediately followed by the mandatory full
   strategy/current-literature checkpoint, so no competing frontier is
   silently postponed beyond review.

Greater downstream leverage does not override interface readiness. In
particular, the Round-169 aggregate would close more of hard TOP, UNBAL
would close a whole M2 parent, and GAR could replace the complete M1 side of
the alternative tree. None currently has a comparably new, exact,
round-sized mechanism gate.

> Does the selected-pair/no-pair decomposition create an exact signed

should be recorded as the Round-173 no-go, with K26 left open. The round
must not hide the failure by taking a positive norm or by moving to the
Round-169 collar, K17a, or BAL.
