# Round 148 squarefree reciprocal-dispersion strategy

## Selection

Round 147 closes the fixed-order \(t=1\) Voronoi route with two
sequential seams:

1. uniform growing-order and moving-prefix Bessel control;
2. signed squarefree-\(H\) resonance aggregation.

The first seam is necessary only for that particular transformed
presentation and, even if granted, the accepted absolute-channel ledger
still loses \(R^{1/3}\) at \(M=R^{4/3}\) and \(R^{1/4}\) at \(M=R^2\).
The arithmetic seam is therefore the higher-leverage next interface.
Round 147 also exposes it before the complex-order transform through a
one-sided reciprocal phase.

## Frozen mechanism

On \(d\asymp D\), \(e\asymp E\), \(DE\asymp M\), a lawful one-sided
transform in \(e\) has dual length

\[
Q\asymp D\sqrt{N/M},\qquad Q\le R^2\ll N,
\]

and reciprocal phase \(e(Nd/q)\). The bare estimate

\[
\sum_{q\asymp Q}\left|\sum_{d\asymp D}e(Nd/q)\right|
\ll_\varepsilon (Q+D)(NQ)^\varepsilon
\]

is accepted, while literal expansion of squarefreeness followed by
triangle gives only \(QD^{1/2}+D\). The transformed target scale is

\[
|\mathcal T_{D,Q}|\ll_\varepsilon RD\,X^\varepsilon .
\]

Round 148 must first derive the exact signed \(\mathcal T_{D,Q}\)
before estimating it. Its outer \(q\)-amplitude, B-process constants,
squarefree and coprime conditions, parity, \(\chi_4\), strict cone or
proved collar, radial profile, clipped prefix, and boundary terms cannot
be replaced by arbitrary bounded coefficients or by the absolute
\(q\)-average.

## Candidate analytical route

1. Derive the exact one-sided transform and split off every polar,
   endpoint, collar, and zero-frequency contribution.
2. Keep the Möbius square-divisor variables signed before any
   Cauchy--Schwarz step.
3. Parameterize the near-divisor relation by
   \(j=N-aq\), retaining its multiplicity and the actual transformed
   amplitude.
4. Test a dispersion step across \(q\), followed by Poisson or completion
   in the square-divisor/progression variables. Price the diagonal,
   common-divisor strata, \(q\mid N\), small \(j\), and terminal
   prefixes separately.
5. Compare any surviving bilinear complete sums with primary-source
   squarefree-progression, Kloosterman-correlation, or weighted
   large-sieve theorems only after an exact phase and modulus match.
6. Translate any gain back to the \(R,M,D,E\) ledger and to the signed
   \(h_z(k)A_{-z}(kN+j)\) interface. A top-block gain is not an all-scale
   result.

## Mandatory falsifiers

- The assertion with arbitrary bounded outer \(q\)-coefficients is false
  by phase alignment and is not a permitted target.
- Disjoint \(q\)- or \(k\)-supports do not imply scalar orthogonality.
- The squarefree \(Q+D\) absolute average must be proved, weakened, or
  rigorously refuted; it is not inherited from the bare lemma.
- The \(z=0\) recombination
  \(\mu^2(n)r_2(n)/4\ge0\) forbids a free random-sign heuristic.
- Exact divisors \(q\mid N\), \(D=1\), primes, even squarefree inputs,
  and the \(N=sL^2+1\) slow family remain controls.
- Cauchy may not discard the actual outer coefficient and then claim a
  coefficient-sensitive saving.

## Exit rule

- squarefree_reciprocal_target;
