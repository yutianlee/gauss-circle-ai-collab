# Recommended immediate strategy: one exact Round-131 gate

The proposed Round-131 direction in the repository is correct: **compute the exact induced determinant-residue zero mode before invoking another norm or transform**.

## Step 1: write the top shell in the determinant coordinate

For a fixed primitive outer ray ((a,b)), write
[
a'=a+p,\qquad b'=b+q,\qquad n=aq-bp.
]
For fixed (n),
[
bp\equiv -n\pmod{|a|},
]
and the physical support interval for (p) has length (O(|a|)). Hence there are (O(1)) admissible lifts (p=p_{a,b}(n)), after which
[
q=\frac{n+bp}{a}
]
is forced.

Substitute this chart into the exact physical scalar, not into a simplified atom. Define
[
\mathcal W_{i;a,b}(n)
]
to include all of the following:

[
\zeta_{i,\rho,\eta}(a+p),\quad
T_{i;r,p,\rho,D}(v),\quad
c_{i,t},\quad
\frac{\chi_4(g)}{g}P_{i,a+p}(g),
]
together with the unique owner, star, equality, sign-sector, and support conventions.

## Step 2: separate the arithmetic residue weight from the smooth phase

Because (\chi_4) has period (4), the correct arithmetic modulus is generally (4|a|), not merely (|a|). Write the arithmetic part as
[
\mathcal W^{\rm ar}_{i;a,b}(n)
==============================

\sum_{\ell\bmod 4|a|}
\widehat{\mathcal W}_{i;a,b}(\ell)
e!\left(\frac{\ell n}{4|a|}\right).
]

The first task is to identify the **physical resonant mode**
[
\ell=\ell_i(a,b),
]
which need not be the naïve Fourier zero mode. In M1 the quarter carriers (e(\pm b'/4)) translate the residue frequency; in M2 the numerator character (\chi_4(|a'|)) does the same. Round 130 already shows that these translations can align with reciprocal aliases rather than producing automatic cancellation.

The decisive computation is therefore:

[
\widehat{\mathcal W}_{i;a,b}\bigl(\ell_i(a,b)\bigr)
\quad\text{with the reciprocal phase and all threshold births included.}
]

There are three possible outcomes:

1. **Exact vanishing.** This would be major structural progress.
2. **A lower-order physical main term.** Quantify the saving and retain it.
3. **A natural-size coherent term.** This is a valid obstruction; stop this graded lane rather than hiding the term inside an energy norm.

## Step 3: price any zero-mode cancellation correctly

A bounded-partial-sum argument for (\chi_4),
[
\sup_M\left|\sum_{m\le M}\chi_4(m)\right|\ll1,
]
would give
[
\left|
\sum_{p}\chi_4(a+p)F(p)
\right|
\ll
|F|_\infty+\operatorname{Var}F
]
provided the **physically recombined** amplitude (F) has controlled variation. If this replaces the present (L)-term triangle by one variation norm, it would bank the full (L=Y^{1/6}) gain and reach (Y^{9/16}).

This argument must not be run on separated threshold atoms. The shell support statement itself is valid only after the Stieltjes thresholds have recombined, and the same ordering issue applies to any periodic-character cancellation.

## Step 4: attack nonresonant residue modes only after the zero mode

For (\ell\ne\ell_i(a,b)), seek a bound for the literal scalar
[
\sum_{(a,b)}A_i(a,b)
\sum_{\ell\ne\ell_i(a,b)}
\widehat{\mathcal W}*{i;a,b}(\ell)
\mathcal K*{i;a,b,\ell},
]
where (\mathcal K) is the remaining determinant/reciprocal kernel.

Do not prove a uniform operator norm. The required theorem is an **actual-vector matrix coefficient estimate** in the one physical coefficient direction.

# Cross-disciplinary approaches

## 1. Kloosterman sums, quadratic characters, and spectral theory

This is the strongest external direction, conditional on an exact transformation of the nonzero determinant modes.

Blomer and Pascadi’s July 2026 theorem applies to arbitrary moduli (c) and arbitrary coefficient sequences:
[
\sum_{\substack{m\in\mathcal I,\ n\in\mathcal J\(m,n,c)=1}}
\alpha_m\beta_n S(am,n;c)
\ll
|\alpha|_2|\beta|_2c^{1+o(1)}
\left(
\frac{N^{1/8}}{c^{3/32}}
+\frac{N^{5/16}}{c^{3/16}}
+\frac{N^{2/3}}{c^{7/18}}
\right).
]
At the square-root length (N=\sqrt c), this saves (c^{-1/32}) over the trivial bound. Their proof passes from Kloosterman fourth moments to quadratic-character sums, and it is valid for composite moduli. This is unusually well aligned with the project’s determinant-residue and (\chi_4) structure. ([arXiv][2])

The required compatibility audit is exact:

1. Can the completed determinant-residue mode be written as (S(am,n;c)), or as an explicitly controlled finite linear combination of such sums?
2. What are (c), (M), and (N) in terms of (Y,D,L,W)?
3. Does the interval length fall in the theorem’s nontrivial range?
4. Are the coprimality conditions literal or recoverable by Möbius inversion without losing the gain?
5. Does the center (c\asymp Y) need to be integerized, and is that integerization target-safe?

If the effective modulus is (c\asymp D=Y^{1/2}) and the square-root regime genuinely occurs, the theorem would offer only
[
c^{-1/32}=Y^{-1/64}.
]
That is not remotely the full (Y^{-11/48}) saving. But after a hypothetical (L=Y^{1/6}) zero-mode gain, an additional (Y^{-1/64}) would move strictly below (Y^{9/16}) and therefore bank a new graded exponent.

Pascadi’s earlier large-sieve results are also relevant because they are designed for sequences with concentrated Fourier transforms, including determinant-difference sequences of the form
[
b_n=
\sum_{h_1\ell_1-h_2\ell_2=n}1.
]
Theorem 2 treats exponential-phase sequences, and Theorem 3 treats dispersion coefficients. This is a better conceptual fit than a general spectral large sieve if the determinant-residue coefficient has sparse Fourier support. ([arXiv][3])

Milićević–Qin–Wu also prove power-saving bilinear estimates for Kloosterman sums modulo arbitrary (q), including ranges below Pólya–Vinogradov. Their theorem is a viable backup if the Blomer–Pascadi parameter window fails. ([arXiv][4])

**Recommendation:** assign an exact source-card and parameter-map audit now, but do not import any of these theorems until the literal top-shell kernel has been transformed into their stated form.

## 2. Algebraic geometry and trace functions

If a completed nonzero residue mode becomes a bounded-conductor trace function (K) modulo a prime (q), the 2026 Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin framework is relevant. Their Theorem 1.3 treats type-I and type-II sums
[
\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_n K(m^bn^c)
]
for “gallant and light” sheaves whose geometric monodromy has a simple connected component or an appropriate finite quasisimple core. The type-II estimate is
[
\ll q^\varepsilon
|\alpha|_2|\beta|_2(MN)^{1/2}
\left(
\frac1M+
\left(\frac{q^{3/4+7/(4l)}}{MN}\right)^{1/l}
\right)^{1/2}.
]
Their method combines quantitative sheaf theory, a robust Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet criterion, and stratification of products of trace functions. ([arXiv][5])

The algebraic-geometric route is eligible only if four facts are proved:

[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(i)}&\text{the residue kernel is genuinely finite-field periodic};\
\text{(ii)}&\text{its conductor is uniformly bounded};\
\text{(iii)}&\text{the relevant monodromy is nonexceptional};\
\text{(iv)}&\text{the real smooth and threshold factors can be separated without}\
&\text{turning the physical scalar into an arbitrary norm.}
\end{array}
]

The zero-mode computation is exactly the place where geometric triviality would reveal itself. If the completed sheaf contains a geometrically constant summand, that is the finite-field version of the coherent zero mode; it must be subtracted or shown to cancel physically.

**Verdict:** high-value conditional route for nonzero modes, but not yet an available theorem dependency.

## 3. Fourier analysis and hyperbolic-paraboloid decoupling

The exact phase before taking an outer norm is
[
\Phi_{i,\eta}(u,v)
==================

\frac{ca}{\kappa_i b}
-\frac{cu}{\kappa_i v}
+\vartheta_{i,\eta}\rho v.
]
Its Hessian satisfies
[
\det D^2_{u,v}\Phi_{i,\eta}
===========================

-\frac{c^2}{\kappa_i^2v^4}.
]
At (c\asymp Y) and (v\asymp D=Y^{1/2}), this determinant is of unit order and negative. Thus the local phase surface is hyperbolic, not elliptic. The determinant gate
[
0<av-bu<\frac{\kappa_i bv}{W}
]
places the support near a rational ruling. This explains both why a generic two-variable transform self-returns and why a **broad–narrow decomposition** is the natural decoupling formulation.

Demeter and Wu prove bilinear (\ell^2)-decoupling and refined bilinear decoupling for the hyperbolic paraboloid
[
\mathbb H={(\xi,\eta,\xi\eta)},
]
with (R^\varepsilon) constants for transverse caps. Their formulation explicitly identifies the linear rulings as the obstruction and requires separation in both coordinate directions. ([arXiv][6])

A legitimate project application would have to:

1. Rescale the top-shell phase to (\mathbb H).
2. Split same-ray or same-denominator packets into the narrow/ruling part.
3. Prove the remaining caps satisfy Demeter–Wu transversality at the active (W,D,L) scales.
4. Apply decoupling only to the broad part.
5. Retain the actual (\chi_4)-weighted coefficients inside each cap.
6. Prove the narrow part separately.

Decoupling alone supplies essentially (Y^\varepsilon), not the missing (Y^{11/48}). Its role would be to create orthogonality after the coherent residue mode has been removed. It cannot replace the zero-mode theorem.

**Verdict:** serious backup for the broad nonzero-mode component; not a stand-alone route.

## 4. Kloosterman fractions and determinant equations

The real phase
[
e!\left(-\frac{ca'}{\kappa_i b'}\right)
]
is naturally closer to a Kloosterman fraction than to a fixed-modulus finite-field trace function when the center (c) is not already integral. Bettin and Chandee’s trilinear theorem treats arbitrary coefficients in sums
[
\sum_{a,m,n}\nu_a\alpha_m\beta_n
e!\left(\frac{a\overline m}{n}\right)
]
and includes applications to determinant equations and equidistribution of their solutions. ([arXiv][7])

Thomas Wright’s 2026 extension treats trilinear Kloosterman fractions with a partially fixed factor in the denominator and improves certain unbalanced convolution ranges. This may be especially relevant to the endpoint M2 **unbalanced** parent, rather than to the current balanced top shell. ([arXiv][8])

One recent preprint that initially claimed stronger bilinear Kloosterman-fraction bounds, arXiv:2601.00292, currently carries an author correction stating that a missing factor invalidates the claimed improvement. It should not be used as a theorem dependency. ([ADS][9])

**Verdict:** audit Bettin–Chandee and Wright; explicitly exclude the corrected preprint.

## 5. Asymptotic analysis

Round 130 shows that a unit-Hessian transform followed by absolute values is an invertible reformulation, not a gain. The remaining asymptotic opportunity is more specific:

> Compute the leading stationary symbol of the **combined physical branch sum**, and determine whether its resonant coefficient vanishes.

This requires a stationary expansion in which the M1 (\pm1/4) branches or the M2 numerator character are combined before taking moduli. A useful theorem would have the form
[
\mathscr S_{i,r,a'}=
\mathscr S^{(0)}*{i,r,a'}+
\mathscr S^{(1)}*{i,r,a'}+
O(Y^{-A}),
]
where the leading term (\mathscr S^{(0)}) is shown to cancel in the determinant-residue sum. The next term might then carry an extra derivative or curvature factor.

The elementary version is discrete Abel summation in the character coordinate. The more technical version is a uniform stationary-phase expansion near the reciprocal aliases, with the character shift built into the saddle equation. The falsification test is immediate: if the exact leading symbols of the two M1 branches add rather than cancel, or if the M2 shifted alias is nonzero, there is no leading-symbol saving.

**Verdict:** high-priority part of the zero-mode gate.

## 6. Additive combinatorics and incidence geometry

The determinant graph has vertices given by primitive rays and edges indexed by
[
n=ab'-a'b.
]
Round 130 proves bounded multiplicity in one fibre, but the phase-aligned control demonstrates that degree bounds and marginal (\ell^2) information do not produce scalar cancellation.

The useful combinatorial target is therefore an **inverse theorem**, not another incidence count:

> If the literal top-shell scalar is (\gg D K_DY^{-\delta}), show that a positive proportion of its mass lies in a structured family: same-denominator packets, one rational ruling, one determinant residue class, or one reciprocal alias.

One could then estimate each structured family using its actual (\chi_4) weights. This is analogous to broad–narrow analysis or Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers, but it must be weighted and phase-sensitive.

Pascadi’s structured large-sieve work is particularly relevant here because it combines Cilleruelo–Garaev modular-hyperbola counting with spectral estimates for Fourier-concentrated sequences. ([arXiv][3])

General rational-point determinant methods can help classify exceptional algebraic fibres, but pure point-count bounds are unlikely to beat the already identified coefficient-blind capacity. Recent determinant-method and rational-points-near-curves results should therefore be viewed as narrow-branch tools, not primary cancellation theorems. ([arXiv][10])

## 7. Probability

The random model for lattice points near circles predicts square-root behavior only after coherent correlations have been identified and removed. Lester and Wigman prove that well-separated boundary lattice points have an independence property in their random-model framework, while also emphasizing nontrivial autocorrelations and exceptional pair structures. ([arXiv][11])

For this project, probability is useful for:

* estimating the expected scale of the nonresonant determinant modes;
* designing signed-versus-random-sign diagnostics;
* locating exceptional coherent classes;
* suggesting a deterministic inverse theorem.

It does not provide a pointwise theorem for the fixed physical Vaaler coefficients. Any probabilistic conclusion remains `diagnostic_only` until deterministic pseudorandomness is proved.

## 8. Homogeneous dynamics and automorphic geometry

A primitive ray ((a,b)) can be viewed through (\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)), and
[
ab'-a'b=n
]
is the symplectic pairing or unipotent displacement between two primitive vectors. Effective horocycle mixing or equidistribution of primitive vectors could potentially average the outer rays.

The limitation is that the project requires a very short, signed, reciprocal-phase-weighted correlation at a prescribed center, not merely equidistribution of primitive rays. Homogeneous dynamics is therefore more plausible as a way to control a broad outer average after zero-mode subtraction than as a direct top-shell theorem.

**Verdict:** low-priority backup.

## 9. Voronoi, Bessel, and radial asymptotics

Vaaler’s extremal Fourier approximation remains the exact truncation and residual-control infrastructure of the current proof route. It does not supply the missing signed determinant correlation. 

Voronoi/Bessel methods remain more natural for the M1 global angular-radial route. Popov’s 2024 survey develops classical and newer versions of Voronoi formulae for the circle problem, including long- and short-interval formulations. ([Math-Net][12])

If the Round-131 determinant zero mode is coherent, the best rotation is not another top-shell norm. It is either:

* the M2 unbalanced prescribed-center wavelet, where Kloosterman-fraction and spectral methods are a better fit; or
* the M1 lower-radial signed aggregate, where uniform Bessel/stationary asymptotics may be more effective.

## Final strategic judgment

The strongest message from Round 130 is not the numerical shell saving. It is that the remaining problem is now **one actual signed scalar with an explicit determinant-residue coordinate**. That is a substantially better research object than the previous outer Gram or two-variable transform.
