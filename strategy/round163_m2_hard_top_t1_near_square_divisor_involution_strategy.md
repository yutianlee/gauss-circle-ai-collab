## Preliminary conductor controls

For an odd prime $p\mid N$, let the single-coordinate divisor toggle be

\[
 T_p(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 pd,&p\nmid d,\\
 d/p,&p\mid d.
 \end{cases}
\]

If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, then
$\chi_4(T_p(d))=-\chi_4(d)$. However the physical divisor window
$I_N=[\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]$ has multiplicative width two, while $p\ge3$.
Thus

\[
 d\in I_N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_p(d)\notin I_N.
\]

So a one-prime sign-reversing toggle has no internal physical pair: every
term is window leakage. This does not rule out a multi-prime exchange,
an average identity across products, or a genuinely analytic signed
estimate, all of which must be tested independently.

Complementary divisors give

\[
 d\in[\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N/d\in[\sqrt N/2,\sqrt N].
\]

For odd squarefree $N$,
$\chi_4(N/d)=\chi_4(N)\chi_4(d)$, but the complementary term lies in the
excluded lower window and carries the swapped profile. If $N=2M$ with
$M$ odd, the physical factor swap $N/d$ is even. The character-preserving
odd-part complement is instead $M/d=N/(2d)$, and it maps the upper window
to $[\sqrt N/4,\sqrt N/2]$. Neither complement is a second physical
upper-window divisor.

Finally,

\[
 \sum_{d\mid N, d\ {\rm odd}}\chi_4(d)
 =\prod_{\substack{p\mid N\\p\ {\rm odd}}}(1+\chi_4(p)).
\]

This full-divisor sum vanishes when an odd
$p\equiv3\pmod4$ divides $N$, but the cancelling partner of every
physical-window divisor is outside the window. Full-divisor vanishing is
therefore not evidence that the truncated coefficient is small.
