# Isolated finite-cell oscillation problem

Read only protocol.md, this statement, and your assigned brief. No graph, history, siblings, or sources.

Let fixed 0<a<b, T->infinity, I_T={n integer:aT<=n<=bT}. Let c_n>=0 obey sum c_n comparable to T and max c_n<=C_eta T^eta for every fixed eta>0. Let v be fixed C^1 on [a,b] with 0<v_min<=v<=v_max. Define h_n(R)=T^(-3/4)v(n/T)cos(2pi R sqrt(n)-3pi/4). Partition the supported integers c_n>0 into K_T nonempty cells by any rule independent of R. Fix delta>0 and K_T<=T^(1-delta). Fix a compact interval J inside (1/sqrt(2),sqrt(3)) with positive length, and a fixed nonnegative smooth w equal to one on a neighborhood of J.

Prove or refute: there are real perturbations d_n supported on I_T and a radius R0/T in J, with |d_n|<=c_n/2 and sum_(n in C)d_n=0 in every cell C, for which the real wave D_T(R)=sum d_n h_n(R) has integral w(R/T)|D_T(R)|^12 dR >=c T^(5/2). All constants may depend on fixed data and delta. Explain how a sign choice yields a comparable peak and moment for one of two nonnegative coefficient vectors c_n+d_n and c_n-d_n, and what further bounds are needed to append an infinite baseline wave. Keep the literal square-root locations and the real phase. The result is about coefficient perturbations preserving cell totals, not exact arithmetic coefficients. No proof method is supplied.

Required controls: K=number of supported integers (singleton cells), zero coefficients, nonnegative perturbed vectors, cell endpoint conventions, fixed perturbation once R0 is chosen, and width of the physical peak. State any defect in the proposed hypotheses. Purely analytical proof; no numerical experiment needed.
