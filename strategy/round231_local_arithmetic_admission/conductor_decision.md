## 1. Result

The principal new reviewed control uses the actual represented-integer support and the complete original wave outside a finite shell. Exact coefficient totals in a sublinear collection of fixed cells can be preserved by nonnegative bounded perturbations while one resulting complete wave has

    M12 >= c T^(10beta),

and both possible perturbation signs obey the inherited pointwise ceiling T^(beta+epsilon) on the physical window. Thus those local totals and that ceiling alone cannot certify any power improvement below 10beta for this coefficient class. This is not a lower bound for the exact r2 wave: the perturbations need not preserve multiplicativity, the divisor-character identity, integrality, or all prefix discrepancies.

A second result concerns actual unperturbed coefficients. The specified first-order rational-demodulation procedure, when followed by a sum of absolute short-interval bounds, has an unavoidable certificate of size at least T^(1/6) on a required shell. This rejects that certificate, not the signed shell itself or every rational-phase method.

## 3. Derivation and admission comparison

The arithmetic report proves exact norm densities at odd and even prime powers, CRT reconstruction, and every reduced-conductor Fourier coefficient. For odd reduced conductor m the normalized coefficient is chi4(m)/m. Completing its phi(m) numerators and all conductors costs

    sum_(m|q)phi(m)/m = product_(p^k||q)[1+k(1-1/p)].

The small size of one mode is therefore not a free saving after completion. The continuous residue means sum exactly to the area mean; the signed errors sum exactly to the original arithmetic discrepancy. Elementary partial summation on n~N costs 1+R sqrt(N). At N~T the global error estimate gives T^(5/4), weaker even than the trivial T^(1/4) shell mass. These formulas supply no new complete inequality.

The signed-transfer report retains the actual discrepancy inside an exact Stieltjes transform. It also retains the rational main and remainder after local additive demodulation. Polynomial moduli can represent the local phase: patches of length H~N^(3/4)T^(-1/2) have bounded exact residual variation with q~max(1,H). Thus the small undemodulated phase width alone is not a reason to reject polynomial moduli.

But when N~cT^(2/3), with fixed sufficiently small c, H<1. Every nonempty patch contains one integer, so its absolute additive partial-sum certificate is exactly the coefficient mass. Summing gives N^(1/4)~T^(1/6), larger than beta and the target tail ceiling 13/125. The available norm-head bound T+X^(5/2)+TX^(5/13) cannot remove this shell at an improved complete exponent: b<10beta forces the cutoff power below 4beta<2/3. This is a rigorous failure of the stated absolute transfer plus this particular head estimate. Joint signed cancellation, nonlinear phase analysis or a stronger head theorem lies outside that result.

The conductor's cell theorem uses weighted projection and radius averaging. Its finite T-sized-shell lemma was independently proved from a statement-only brief using pairwise variance. A separate nonauthor review verifies the whole baseline, all tails and the later smaller-shell extension. At N=T^(4beta), the perturbation has height N^(1/4), width N^(-1/2), and moment N^(5/2)=T^(10beta). Convexity chooses a single sign with a comparably large complete moment, while both signs retain the actual baseline ceiling. No arithmetic moment lower bound is inferred from this adversarial coefficient family.
