## Candidate four-corner orbit

On a physical atom satisfying

\[
 (m,\beta)=(\alpha,m')=(m,m')=1,
\tag{197.S6}
\]

define the two allocation swaps

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \tau_0(g\alpha,m,g\beta,m')
   &=(gm,\alpha,g\beta,m'),\\
 \tau_1(g\alpha,m,g\beta,m')
   &=(g\alpha,m,gm',\beta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{197.S7}
\]

They preserve \(N\), \(N+r\), \(r\), the Fejer factor, and the radical
phase.  Conditions (197.S2) and (197.S6) are the exact candidate
conditions ensuring that the recomputed gcd is \(g\) at all four
corners.  The maps commute algebraically and generate a candidate
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\) orbit.

The physical defects are invariant:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |g\alpha-gm|&=|gm-g\alpha|,\\
 |g\beta-gm'|&=|gm'-g\beta|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{197.S8}
\]

Thus \(P_2\) is formally orbit invariant whenever every corner is
interpreted with the same recomputed gcd.  This assertion must be
checked against the exact opposing-source, cone, endpoint, and
zero-extension predicates; it may fail there and create boundary
commutators.

Put

\[
 s_0=\chi_4(\alpha m),\qquad
 s_1=\chi_4(\beta m').
\tag{197.S9}
\]

The lower and upper swaps multiply the physical character product by
\(s_0\) and \(s_1\), respectively.  Since

\[
 r=g(\beta m'-\alpha m)
\tag{197.S10}
\]

and \(g\) is odd, the candidate rectangular-sign sector is

\[
 \mathcal P_{\rm rect}:
 \quad P_2,\quad (197.S6),\quad
 s_0=s_1=-1.
\tag{197.S11}
\]

In particular \(4\mid r\).  On a complete four-corner orbit, the
character weights have the alternating pattern

\[
 (+,-,-,+).
\tag{197.S12}
\]

The sector is defined physically and allocation-independently by taking
the full orbit of every atom satisfying (197.S11), with zero extension
at every literal predicate.  No nonemptiness, density, or positive-mass
claim is built into the definition.

## Candidate mixed coefficient difference

For a corner whose only moving factors are the two endpoint
coefficients, write

\[
 F(d_0,d_1)=
 \lambda_{N+r,\sigma}(d_1)
 \overline{\lambda_{N,\sigma}(d_0)}.
\tag{197.S13}
\]

The alternating orbit sum is then the exact rectangular difference

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &F(g\alpha,g\beta)-F(gm,g\beta)
  -F(g\alpha,gm')+F(gm,gm')\\
 &\quad=
 \{\lambda_{N+r,\sigma}(g\beta)
    -\lambda_{N+r,\sigma}(gm')\}
 \overline{\{\lambda_{N,\sigma}(g\alpha)
    -\lambda_{N,\sigma}(gm)\}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{197.S14}
\]

The lower difference is geometrically close:

\[
 |g\alpha-gm|\le D_L.
\tag{197.S15}
\]

On one common literal smooth cell, the accepted normalized \(C^1\)
symbol calculus suggests the candidate gain

\[
 |\lambda_{N,\sigma}(g\alpha)
   -\lambda_{N,\sigma}(gm)|
 \ll_\varepsilon {D_L\over L}X^\varepsilon,
\tag{197.S16}
\]

while the upper difference is kept at its literal bounded size.  The
positive small-\(\kappa\) \(P_2\) outer envelope is
\(D_LL^2X^\varepsilon\).  Thus the exact desired ledger is

\[
 {D_L\over L}\cdot D_LL^2
 =D_L^2L\ll L^2.
\tag{197.S17}
\]

This is the only candidate power gain.  It is coefficient-sensitive and
fails for arbitrary bounded endpoint arrays.  It does not use the
Round-196 primitive-carrier shadow.

## Literal commutators that must be opened

Equation (197.S14) is available only on the common four-corner part of
the actual physical summand.  The round must form a first-failure
partition and price, without duplication:

1. failure of any cross-coprimality in (197.S6);
2. change of recomputed gcd or of the opposing-incidence orientation;
3. strict-cone, shell, height, profile, floor, star, half-weight,
   hard-sample, crossing, endpoint-trace, or zero-extension exits;
4. Round-184 residual-selector changes at either endpoint, including
   the exceptional case in which exactly one selected prime lies in
   \(g\);
5. squarefree, allocation-coprimality, parity, and divisibility changes;
6. loss of one or more of the four corners;
7. any failure of the character table (197.S12);
8. any conductor, anchor, band, carry, affine birth/death, mask,
   cell, crossing, Fejer, or square-root term introduced if the proof is
   pushed through the later Round-187--Round-192 core rather than proved
   directly in the physical Fejer correlation.

The preferred proof level is the exact physical correlation (197.S1):
there \(N,N+r,r\), the Fejer weight, and the radical phase are common to
the four corners.  A later-core proof is admissible only if both
\(T\)-branches, both orientations and frequency signs, the complete
anchor aggregate, all carries and births/deaths, and one outer real part
are restored exactly.

Normalized BV factors are not assigned pointwise smoothness.  On a
common cell use only the certified \(C^1\) part.  Charge a crossed BV
increment or literal face by an explicit collar count on the orbit-closed
active box.  If any boundary class retains the full
\(D_LL^2X^\varepsilon\) capacity, the mechanism self-returns unless that
class is placed in an exact complement or an already proved safe sector.

## Promotion and stop rules

A strict-sector promotion requires:

- a physical, orbit-invariant sector definition independent of coefficient
  nonvanishing;
- an exact disjoint complement;
- a complete four-corner orbit or an explicitly bounded exit term;
- the actual endpoint mixed difference, not an arbitrary-array theorem;
- the common-cell \(D_L/L\) gain with normalized BV and literal faces
  priced separately;
- an outer \(O(L^2X^\varepsilon)\) ledger with no positive power hidden
  in \(X^\varepsilon\); and
- independent algebra, orbit, power, owner-scope, and blind reviews.

Stop with a route-scoped no-go if:

1. the two independent swaps do not define a common physical orbit even
   after the exact cross-coprimality restriction;
2. the four literal character weights do not give (197.S12);
3. the full summand does not reduce to (197.S14) plus target-safe
   commutators;
4. an opposing-source, selector, core, or zero-extension boundary retains
   full \(D_LL^2X^\varepsilon\) capacity; or
5. the \(D_L/L\) gain is lost when the active-box multiplicity and outer
   ledger are restored.

Even complete success leaves the disjoint \(P_1\) complement, all other
original-\(t\) incidences, the hard small-\(t\) owner until every
complement closes, smooth M1, GAR, every M2 parent, endpoint uniformity,
M9, both bridges, the quarter theorem, and every exponent separate.
