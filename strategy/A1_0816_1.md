# Executive assessment

The repository has made **substantial structural and diagnostic progress**, but not theorem-level progress toward a new Gauss-circle exponent. The accepted architecture is still

[
\mathrm{H1!-!H3}+\mathrm{H4}+\mathrm{R5!-!Full}+\mathrm{M9}
\Longrightarrow
P(X):=N(\sqrt X)-\pi X\ll_\varepsilon X^{1/4+\varepsilon},
]

with `M9-M1`, `M9-M2`, and endpoint uniformity all open. The current proof draft itself describes the argument as conditional and does not claim a new exponent.

The strategy should be **modified, not replaced**:

* retain the balanced hyperbola, floor-compatible sawtooth, and finite Vaaler reduction;
* stop spending primary effort on repeated Poisson/B-process/Hankel transformations once the repository has proved that they return to the original signed energy;
* replace the current transform-chasing phase by a **sign-preserving resonance-energy program**, with an additive-combinatorial inverse theorem for exceptional primitive rays;
* retain an M1 spectral/Kloosterman route as the principal backup;
* use (\ell^2)-decoupling only after the exact signed frequency geometry has been established. Ordinary coefficient-uniform decoupling is unlikely to close the endpoint because it generally erases the essential (\chi_4)-structure.

The latest update is therefore best described as **high-quality obstruction and normalization progress**, rather than progress on the final exponent.

[

The phase has a rank-one Hessian in the radial lift direction. Poisson/B-process in (m), followed by the matching reciprocal transformation in (k), returns the original phase and amplitude: the Gaussian factors (e(1/8)) and (e(-1/8)) cancel, as do the adjoint Jacobians. Thus repeatedly applying the same stationary transform is circular.

This is a significant no-go theorem. It means the next saving cannot come simply from “one more B-process.”

# 5. Should the strategy change?

## Judgment

**Yes at the bottleneck; no at the reduction.**

The hyperbola–Vaaler architecture should remain the main framework. It has survived repeated endpoint, sign, normalization, and residual audits. Replacing it now would discard the strongest accepted part of the project.

The part that should change is the treatment of the terminal signed energies.

## 5.1 What should be stopped

The following should not remain primary routes:

1. **Repeated transform iteration.**
   Both M1 and M2 now have exact self-return results. A further Poisson/B/Hankel transform is only justified if it introduces a genuinely new arithmetic variable or nonconjugate averaging.

2. **Character-blind (L^2), Frobenius, Schur, or operator-norm bounds.**
   If (\chi_4) enters only through a diagonal unitary conjugation (U^*KU), an operator norm cannot exploit it.

3. **Absolute summation over primitive rays and reciprocal modes.**
   The upper-range absolute near-collision program has already produced false or overlarge control quantities.

4. **Generic decoupling before the frequency geometry is frozen.**
   A theorem valid for arbitrary bounded coefficients will not, by itself, explain why the special (\chi_4)-weighted sum is smaller than its unsigned adversarial analogue.

## 5.2 New primary route: inverse structure plus signed energy

For M2 define

[
\Lambda_{a,b}
=============

\frac{X(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)^2}{2}.
]

After endpoint-collar extraction, the exact odd-lift sum has the schematic form

[
\sum_{\substack{g\in\mathcal G_{a,b}\g\ {\rm odd}}}
\mathfrak B^\circ_{a,b,k}(g)
e!\left(
-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}{k}
\right).
]

The first required theorem is not yet the whole energy estimate. It is:

### Actual-symbol variation lemma

Prove uniformly that

[
\mathcal V_{a,b,k}
:=
|\mathfrak B^\circ(g_{\max})|
+
\sum_{\substack{g,g+2\in\mathcal G_{a,b}}}
|\mathfrak B^\circ(g+2)-\mathfrak B^\circ(g)|
]

has the expected stationary-phase scale, with:

* the exact (\Phi), spatial profile, floors, and (q_X);
* both endpoints and collar ownership;
* all transition and lower-order stationary corrections;
* no frozen-symbol substitution.

Only after that is the Abel bound legitimate:

[
\left|
\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_{a,b}}
\mathfrak B^\circ(g)
e!\left(-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}{k}\right)
\right|
\lesssim
\mathcal V_{a,b,k}
\min!\left(
G_{a,b},
\left|\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}k\right|^{-1}
\right).
]

### Resonance inverse theorem

Classify the triples ((a,b,k)) for which

[
\left|\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}k\right|
\le G_{a,b}^{-1}.
]

The desired conclusion should have two parts:

[
\mathscr R
==========

\mathscr R_{\rm structured}
\sqcup
\mathscr R_{\rm generic}.
]

For the generic part, prove a determinant/incidence/additive-energy bound. For the structured part, identify squarefree-kernel, perfect-square, or low-complexity families and estimate their **actual coefficient-weighted signed contribution**, not merely their cardinality.

The explicit ((81,121)) family must be one of the control models.

### Signed primitive-ray energy

The eventual theorem should retain cancellation over ((a,b,k)):

[
\begin{aligned}
\Bigg|
\sum_{\substack{a<b<4a\(a,b)=1\a,b\ {\rm odd}}}
\sum_{k\in K_{a,b}}
e(\alpha_{a,b,k})
\sum_{\substack{g\in\mathcal G_{a,b}\g\ {\rm odd}}}
\mathfrak B^\circ_{a,b,k}(g)
e!\left(-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}k\right)
\Bigg|
\ll_\varepsilon L^2X^\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
]

This is the right M2 target. Summing the Abel majorant absolutely over (a,b,k) is likely too strong.

# 7. Cross-disciplinary methods

## 7.1 (\ell^2)-decoupling and Fourier analysis

Small-cap decoupling is relevant, but only in a carefully limited role.

Guth–Maldague prove sharp small-cap decoupling and amplitude-dependent wave-envelope estimates for the cone in (\mathbb R^3); this is the input used in Li–Yang’s first-spacing argument. ([arXiv][4]) Demeter–Guth–Wang develop a broader small-cap toolbox for the parabola, moment curve, and cone, including exponential sums with small frequency separation. ([arXiv][5])

For the current M2 survivor, a direct application is obstructed by three facts:

1. the transformed phase has a rank-one radial direction;
2. the exceptional fourth-power rays are genuinely coherent;
3. coefficient-uniform decoupling does not exploit the (\chi_4)-weighted signed structure.

Therefore, decoupling should enter **after** primitive-ray packetization. A plausible route is:

* remove exact structured rays;
* decompose the remaining ((a,b,k))-set into caps according to
  [
  \delta=\sqrt b-\sqrt a,\qquad \frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}k;
  ]
* prove transversality for generic packets;
* apply a weighted (L^2) or (L^4) estimate to those packets;
* retain the signed units (e(\alpha_{a,b,k})) outside the norm wherever possible.

Kiyohara’s (\ell^2)-decoupling treatment of lattice points near nondegenerate curves is relevant because it incorporates minimal separation and the doubling constant of the underlying finite set. That suggests measuring additive structure of the resonant primitive rays before applying decoupling. ([arXiv][6])

Xuerui Yang’s 2025 Mizohata–Takeuchi-type weighted (L^2) estimate for quadratic exponential sums is also strategically relevant. Its proof combines (TT^*), superlevel-set analysis, and the circle method, and bounds a weighted (L^2) norm in terms of tube concentration. ([arXiv][7]) A local quadraticization of the M2 phase might fit this architecture, but only after proving a tube-mass estimate for the actual arithmetic frequency set.

**Verdict:** useful generic-packet module; not a direct endpoint solver.

## 7.2 Additive combinatorics and incidence geometry

This is the most promising new primary direction for M2.

Robert and Sargos showed in another three-dimensional exponential-sum problem that an unavailable optimal spacing lemma could be bypassed by a four-variable Diophantine count. ([De Gruyter Brill][8]) The conceptual lesson is directly applicable: do not insist on bounding each reciprocal-resonance fibre separately; recast the second moment or energy as a higher-dimensional incidence problem.

For the current survivor, one should study the additive/multiplicative energy of

[
\mathcal A
==========

\left{
\frac{X(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)^2}{2k}:
a<b<4a,\ (a,b)=1,\ a,b\ {\rm odd}
\right}
\pmod 1.
]

The needed program is:

1. count pairs with
   [
   \left|
   \frac{\Lambda_{a,b}}{k}
   -----------------------

   \frac{\Lambda_{a',b'}}{k'}
   \right|\le \eta;
   ]

2. identify when high multiplicity forces:

   * common squarefree kernels;
   * (a=u^2,b=v^2);
   * low-rank relations between (u,v,k);
   * small doubling in a suitable parameterization;

3. prove that outside these structured cases, the incidence count is near-diagonal;

4. estimate the structured cases using their actual signed profile.

Recent work on bilinear sums and additive energies involving modular square roots is potentially relevant if one can first modularize the reciprocal resonance without losing the actual amplitude. ([arXiv][9])

**Verdict:** highest near-term priority for M2.

## 7.3 Spectral and Kloosterman methods

The strongest recent external developments are more naturally aligned with M1.

Recent examples include:

* Pascadi’s non-abelian amplification for bilinear Kloosterman sums with composite moduli and square-root-length variables; ([arXiv][10])
* Blomer–Pascadi’s 2026 bilinear Kloosterman estimate, giving a power saving at the critical square-root length for all moduli; ([arXiv][11])
* Dong–Robles–Zeindler’s improved bilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions, including arbitrary coefficient sequences and Salié-type phases; ([arXiv][12])
* Kerr–Shparlinski–Wu–Xi’s incomplete Kloosterman bounds using additive combinatorics. ([arXiv][13])

These papers are **source-audit candidates**, not imported lemmas. The M1 kernel has a short moving numerator, local parity data, transition amplitudes, and a particular conductor corridor. The next literature campaign should build a table:

[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Project variable} & \text{External theorem variable} & \text{Required range}\ \hline
C & c & J^{32/45}<C\le J\
\text{numerator length} & M & \text{exact project length}\
\text{dual length} & N & \text{exact project length}\
\chi_4\text{ and local data} & \alpha_m,\beta_n & \text{must match}\
\text{actual profile} & W & \text{smoothness/BV hypotheses}
\end{array}
]

Terminate the route immediately if the numerator length or modulus structure lies outside the theorem.

**Verdict:** strongest backup program, with a realistic possibility of extending the accepted M1 conductor range.

## 7.4 Asymptotic analysis

Asymptotic analysis remains essential for exactness, but is unlikely to be the final source of cancellation.

The repo has correctly learned that all of the following must be retained:

* finite Poisson stars and two half-endpoint corrections;
* incomplete Fresnel transitions;
* moving stationary amplitudes;
* floor and ceiling parity;
* the distinction between step-two bulk variation and step-four sharp-endpoint variation;
* Gaussian constants and external normalization.

The next useful asymptotic theorem is the uniform actual-symbol variation lemma described above. Once that interface is established, asymptotic analysis should stop. The Round 76 Legendre self-return shows that applying another matched stationary transform recreates the original problem.

**Verdict:** indispensable interface certification; not the missing power-saving mechanism.

## 7.5 Probability theory and random models

Probability is useful as a diagnostic and as a guide to the correct deterministic theorem.

Lester–Wigman analyze the folklore i.i.d. square-root-cancellation heuristic for lattice points near circles, prove independence for well-separated configurations, and identify nontrivial correlation structure. ([arXiv][14]) This supports a decomposition into:

[
\text{generic quasi-independent configurations}
\quad+\quad
\text{structured correlated configurations}.
]

That is precisely the decomposition suggested by the current M2 resonance set.

However, probability alone cannot prove a worst-case pointwise bound. The explicit fourth-power coherent rays show why.

Xiao’s 2026 square-root-sum results are also instructive: diagonal-scale second and fourth moments are proved in substantial ranges, but discrepancy is obtained only after combining the moment estimate with a **separate pointwise exponential-sum input** and Erdős–Turán. ([arXiv][15]) This reinforces the project’s existing conclusion that a global fourth moment is not automatically a pointwise M9 theorem.

Probability should be used for:

* signed versus unsigned numerical controls;
* estimating the expected generic energy;
* predicting the correct large-value exponent;
* identifying exceptional deterministic families;
* designing a deterministic large-value theorem.

**Verdict:** valuable hypothesis generator and falsification model; not a proof route by itself.
