## Current primary literature and its actual applicability

The bounded fresh audit checked the recent Kloosterman, trace-function,
amplitude-dependent cone, and circle records, including withdrawals.
The accepted repaired Li--Yang exponent remains unchanged. No checked
theorem supplies either complete open source directly.

Blomer--Pascadi's critical all-moduli Kloosterman bound saves c^(-1/32)
under its actual kernel, product-coefficient, length, unit and gcd
hypotheses. An exact reduction from the physical source has not been
constructed. Pascadi's factorization theorem has a c^(-1/12) example
for prime squares as well as products of comparable distinct primes;
the prime-square case corrects the narrower wording of the supplied
memo. See [Pascadi, Theorem1.2 and Example1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08445v2).

bound for the exceptional terms. The audited candidate ledgers record

The cone theorem controls three-dimensional averages. A lift of the
circle sum to that setting still needs a proved estimate for its fixed
central-line trace. The trace-function approach still lacks an actual
identified sheaf and a priced exceptional set. The September numerical
circle paper supplies computational evidence, not an asymptotic upper
bound: [arXiv2609.04725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04725).

Detailed primary theorem locations, versions, restrictions, and cost
ledgers are in the Round202 `reports/current_primary_methods_audit.md`.
This is a dated bounded audit, not a claim that all literature or all
possible methods have been exhausted. No new external theorem is

## Why a lower cutoff does not finish the argument

The complete high-moment source can be split at U=T^(1/2047).
The full low-head twelfth moment is O(T): an algebraic separation
argument handles every nonzero twelve-root difference, and Round201
handles all exact configurations. This is a valid but very low cutoff.
It leaves the entire transition n~T and every higher tail in the
remaining signed sum.

Known residual-moment interpolation does not recover the missing
saving. Even granting a sharp residual q0=262/27 moment, its combination
with L2 tail decay and the available supremum estimate has optimized
T-exponent excess at least 2eta0, where
eta0=(62/27)(theta_LY-1/4)=0.148072478... . This remains above the needed
eta<0.088281287... . The statement concerns this specified combination
of inequalities; it does not rule out a stronger signed-tail theorem.

## Decision for the next analytic campaign

Keep the complete whole-discrepancy route, and test a new estimate that
preserves its oscillatory filter. Choose a fixed smooth chi supported
away from zero with chi=1 on the required radius window, and w=chi^2.
For the actual tail n>U define

\[
 \mu_{T,>U}=\frac12\sum_{n>U}a_n(T)
   \{e(-3/8)\delta_{\sqrt n}+e(3/8)\delta_{-\sqrt n}\},
 \quad K_T(\xi)=T\widehat\chi(T\xi).
\]

Plancherel identifies the complete tail twelfth moment with
||K_T*mu_(T,>U)^{*6}||_2^2. The kernel has every polynomial moment
zero, reflecting the window's separation from radius zero. It must
remain oscillatory. The next candidate analytic target is

\[
 \|K_T*\mu_{T,>U}^{*6}\|_2
       \ll_\epsilon T^{29/50+\epsilon},\qquad U=T^{1/2047}.
\]

This corresponds to eta=2/25 and would yield the strict intermediate
pointwise exponent 102/325=0.313846... after complete reconstruction.
It is unproved. Its formulation supplies no gain by itself. The next
campaign must attempt a coefficient-sensitive estimate for the full
signed convolution, such as a rigorously bounded smooth-density
subtraction, and account for the complete remainder. An approximation
of one r_2 summatory function does not establish that theorem.

The full goal remains the quarter exponent. The analogous filtered
k-fold measure represents every even moment; uniform target-sized
bounds at arbitrarily high fixed orders would reach one quarter via
persistence. The twelfth moment is a strict-improvement milestone,
not a redefinition of success. Preserve all internal routes and
accepted results, and reopen a modular import only when an exact
source transformation with affordable costs exists.
