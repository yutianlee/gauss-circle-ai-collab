## 1. Exact scoped result

Retain the literal nonsquare hard-TOP scalar

\[
 \mathcal T_L^{\rm ns}
 =\sum_{\substack{D>1\\D\ {\rm squarefree}}}\sum_{t\ge1}
 B_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D),
 \qquad
 B_D(t)=L^{3/2}(Dt^2)^{-3/4}C_L(Dt^2),
\tag{161.K1}
\]

with fixed support constants

\[
 B_D(t)\ne0\Longrightarrow
 c_-L^2\le Dt^2\le c_+L^2
\tag{161.K2}
\]

and the accepted bounds

\[
 \sum_{D,t}|B_D(t)|^2\ll L^2\log(2L),\qquad
 \sum_t|B_D(t)|\ll_\varepsilon
 \left(1+\frac L{\sqrt D}\right)L^\varepsilon.
\tag{161.K3}
\]

Then the following two conclusions are proved.

1. For every fixed \(C>0\),
   \[
    \boxed{
    \left|\sum_{\substack{D\le CL\\D>1\ {\rm squarefree}}}
    \sum_tB_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D)\right|
    \ll_{C,\varepsilon}L^{3/2}X^\varepsilon.}
    \tag{161.K4}
   \]
   This owns every fixed \(t\ge\tau\sqrt L\) sector. Exact base-frequency
   collisions and exact atom collisions have the algebraic classification
   in Section 4; all channels named by that classification are target-safe.
2. Support, (161.K3), exact-collision sparsity, and a
   coefficient-uniform common-test, large-sieve, Bessel, or Hilbert
   projective placement do not imply the target. The obstruction is exact
   already on the \(t=1\), \(D\asymp L^2\) one-column interface, where the
   evaluation norm and the phase-aligned diagnostic's nuclear norm are both
   \(\asymp L\), and their product is \(\asymp L^2\).

The first conclusion is a target-safe sector, not an owner-complete strict
polynomial range for \(\mathcal T_L^{\rm ns}\). The second is a
route-scoped coefficient-interface obstruction, not a physical lower bound.
No near-collision estimate, hard-TOP target, downstream M2 estimate, or
global exponent follows.

## 2. Literal radical-incidence dictionary

For a literal product pair \((h,m)\), put

\[
 g=(h,m),\qquad h=ga,\qquad m=gb,\qquad (a,b)=1.
\]

Write uniquely

\[
 a=d_1u^2,\qquad b=d_2v^2
\]

with \(d_1,d_2\) squarefree. Then

\[
 D=d_1d_2,\qquad t=guv,\qquad
 (d_1u,d_2v)=1,
\tag{161.K5}
\]

\[
 d_2v^2\le d_1u^2\le4d_2v^2,\qquad
 g,d_1,u\ {\rm odd}.
\tag{161.K6}
\]

Conversely, a tuple satisfying (161.K5)--(161.K6) and the literal support
and profile conditions reconstructs one allowed pair

\[
 h=gd_1u^2,\qquad m=gd_2v^2.
\tag{161.K7}
\]

The gcd and the squarefree kernels recover the tuple, so this map is
bijective. In particular, the factor \(2\) of an even \(D\) lies in
\(d_2\), and the \(2\)-part of an even \(t\) may lie in \(v\); neither
branch is deleted.

The exact coefficient is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_D(t)=\mathbf 1_{Dt^2\asymp L^2}
\sum_{\substack{d_1d_2=D,\ guv=t\\(d_1u,d_2v)=1\\
g,d_1,u\ {\rm odd}\\d_2v^2\le d_1u^2\le4d_2v^2}}
&\chi_4(gd_1)\eta_L(gd_1u^2)
\Phi\!\left(\frac{gd_1u^2}{H+1}\right)\\
&\times\left(\frac{L^2}{Dt^2}\right)^{3/4}
W\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_Xd_1u^2}{4d_2v^2}}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.K8}
\]

where the indicator abbreviates the original literal shell and zero
extension. No tensor separation or incidence cancellation is inferred
from (161.K8).

## 3. Long-channel sector

For \(Z\ge2\), (161.K3) and the triangle inequality give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\sum_{\substack{D\le Z\\D>1\ {\rm squarefree}}}
 \sum_tB_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D)\right|
 &\le\sum_{D\le Z}\sum_t|B_D(t)|\\
 &\ll_\varepsilon
 \left(Z+L\sum_{D\le Z}D^{-1/2}\right)L^\varepsilon\\
 &\ll_\varepsilon (Z+L\sqrt Z)L^\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.K9}
\]

Taking \(Z=CL\) proves (161.K4). From (161.K2),
\(t\ge\tau\sqrt L\) implies
\(D\le c_+\tau^{-2}L\), while \(D\le CL\) implies
\(t\ge\sqrt{c_-/C}\sqrt L\). Thus (161.K4) is exactly a fixed-constant
long-channel sector.

The same positive argument stops at that scale. Substituting
\(Z=L^{1+\delta}\), \(\delta>0\), into (161.K9) costs
\(L^{3/2+\delta/2+\varepsilon}\). This is a capacity of the method, not
a lower bound for the physical scalar.

## 4. Exact collision algebra

Square roots of distinct positive squarefree integers are linearly
independent over \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed, in the multiquadratic field
generated by their prime factors, the squarefree prime monomials are
distinct sign characters; averaging a rational relation against the
characters isolates every coefficient.

Suppose two unequal base-frequency collisions occur:

\[
 J(\sqrt{D_1}-\sqrt{D_2})=k,\qquad
 J(\sqrt{D_3}-\sqrt{D_4})=\ell,
\tag{161.K10}
\]

where \(D_1\ne D_2\), \(D_3\ne D_4\), and \(k,\ell\ne0\).
Eliminating \(J\) produces

\[
 \ell\sqrt{D_1}-\ell\sqrt{D_2}
 -k\sqrt{D_3}+k\sqrt{D_4}=0.
\tag{161.K11}
\]

Radical independence rules out disjoint pairs and one-index overlaps.
With two shared indices, the equations are repetitions or reversals.
Hence the graph of \(J\sqrt D\bmod1\) has at most one unequal unordered
edge globally.

For atoms, a cross-channel relation is

\[
 J(t_1\sqrt{D_1}-t_2\sqrt{D_2})=k,\qquad
 D_1\ne D_2,\quad k\ne0.
\tag{161.K12}
\]

Applying the same elimination to two such relations shows that all
cross-channel atom relations use one unordered radical pair, and their
coefficient triples are rational multiples of one primitive relation.
If \(D_1=D_2=D\), the equation is

\[
 (t_1-t_2)J\sqrt D=k.
\tag{161.K13}
\]

The case \(t_1=t_2\) is the trivial same-atom loop and forces \(k=0\).
A nontrivial same-channel repetition can occur only when
\(J\sqrt D\in\mathbb Q\). At most one such rational channel exists.
Moreover it cannot coexist with a cross-channel relation: if
\(J\sqrt{D_0}=r\in\mathbb Q\), then (161.K12) gives

\[
 rt_1\sqrt{D_1}-rt_2\sqrt{D_2}-k\sqrt{D_0}=0,
\tag{161.K14}
\]

which contradicts radical independence, including when \(D_0\) equals
one endpoint.

Every channel named in this exact classification has absolute mass
\(\ll_\varepsilon(1+L/\sqrt D)L^\varepsilon\), so a fixed finite
collection is target-safe. Equations (161.K10)--(161.K14) give no
positive lower bound for a nonzero modulo-one gap and no control of a
near-resonant collar.

## 5. Literal one-column survivor and projective obstruction

On \(t=1\), (161.K5) forces \(g=u=v=1\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_D(1)=\mathbf 1_{D\asymp L^2}
\left(\frac{L^2}{D}\right)^{3/4}
\sum_{\substack{d_1d_2=D\\d_1\ {\rm odd}\\d_2\le d_1\le4d_2}}
&\chi_4(d_1)\eta_L(d_1)
\Phi\!\left(\frac{d_1}{H+1}\right)\\
&\times W\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_Xd_1}{4d_2}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{161.K15}
\]

This is the literal signed close-factor coefficient. For
\(D=pq\), \(p<q\le4p\), the cone has only the nontrivial orientation
\((d_1,d_2)=(q,p)\), when its profiles are active. This exhibits a
singleton product fibre but proves no density or lower mass.

Choose fixed \(0<a<b\) inside the ambient support interval and let

\[
 \mathscr D_L=\{D\in(aL^2,bL^2]:D>1\ {\rm squarefree}\},\qquad
 Q_L=\#\mathscr D_L\asymp L^2.
\tag{161.K16}
\]

The asymptotic follows from
\(\sum_{n\le x}\mu^2(n)=x/\zeta(2)+O(\sqrt x)\). This is an ambient
diagnostic count, not a count of nonzero physical coefficients.

Define

\[
 (E_Jc)(D)=\sum_tc(t)e(tJ\sqrt D),\qquad
 \Lambda_J(A)=\sum_{D,t}A_D(t)e(tJ\sqrt D).
\tag{161.K17}
\]

For every Hilbert-projective decomposition
\(A=\sum_rx_r\otimes c_r\),

\[
 |\Lambda_J(A)|
 \le\|E_J\|_{2\to2}\sum_r\|x_r\|_2\|c_r\|_2.
\tag{161.K18}
\]

The infimum on the right is the nuclear norm \(\|A\|_{S_1}\).
On the one-column restriction,

\[
 \|E_J^{(1)}\|=Q_L^{1/2}\asymp L,
\tag{161.K19}
\]

independently of every spacing or collision property. For the hostile
method control

\[
 A_D(t)=\mathbf 1_{D\in\mathscr D_L}\mathbf 1_{t=1}
 e(-J\sqrt D),
\tag{161.K20}
\]

one has

\[
 \|A\|_{S_1}=Q_L^{1/2}\asymp L,\qquad
 \Lambda_J(A)=Q_L\asymp L^2.
\tag{161.K21}
\]

The array has squared energy \(\asymp L^2\) and unit row mass, so it
obeys the scales in (161.K3). A real-array version gives the same
capacity: writing \(\theta_D=2\pi J\sqrt D\), choose all
\(b_D=\cos\theta_D\) if \(\sum_D\cos^2\theta_D\ge Q_L/2\), and choose
all \(b_D=\sin\theta_D\) otherwise. Then \(|b_D|\le1\) and

\[
 \left|\sum_{D\in\mathscr D_L}b_De(J\sqrt D)\right|\ge Q_L/2.
\tag{161.K22}
\]

Thus the coarse coefficient interface plus exact collision sparsity
cannot imply an \(L^{3/2+o(1)}\) theorem uniformly over coefficients.
For the physical column, positive Cauchy and (161.K3) give only

\[
 \left|\sum_D B_D(1)e(J\sqrt D)\right|
 \ll L^2\sqrt{\log(2L)}.
\tag{161.K23}
\]

This leaves \(L^{1/2-o(1)}\) relative to the target. Equations
(161.K20)--(161.K23) are route capacities, not a lower bound for
(161.K15).

## 6. Audited source scope and restored powers

The audited primary-source interfaces have the following precise scope.

1. Montgomery--Vaughan Theorem 1 estimates a family of evaluations of
   one common coefficient vector. Its weighted “Moreover” clause retains
   the same restriction. Neither form accepts the \(D\)-dependent rows
   \(B_D(t)\) without a separately proved factorization.
2. Bombieri--Iwaniec Lemma 2.4 is bilinear with separable coefficients
   \(a(x)b(\eta)\) and charges absolute weighted near-collision forms.
   Applying it to a general \(B_D(t)\) therefore requires a projective
   decomposition and its full collision-adapted norm.
3. Robert--Sargos Theorem 2 counts a real four-root inequality, not the
   fixed-\(J\) modulo-one relation
   \(\|J(\sqrt D-\sqrt E)\|\le T^{-1}\). Even granting a cost-one
   separation of the literal cone coefficient, their Theorem 1 with
   \[
    H_0\asymp M_0\asymp L,\quad N_0=1,\quad
    \alpha=\beta=\tfrac12,\quad\gamma=1,\quad\Xi\asymp JL
   \]
   (the sole \(n=2\) term gives the exact choice \(\Xi=JL/2\))
   yields
   \[
    J^{1/4}L^{3/2+\varepsilon}
    +L^{7/4+\varepsilon}
    +L^{3/2+\varepsilon}
    +J^{-1/2}L^{3/2+\varepsilon}.
    \tag{161.K24}
   \]
   Since \(L\ll J^{1/2}\), the first term is at least the trivial
   \(L^2\) scale, with equality only at the boundary.
4. Miller Theorem 1.1 applies to the additive twists of Fourier
   coefficients of one fixed \(\mathrm{GL}(3)\) cusp form. No
   identification of the finite, \(D\)-dependent coefficient (161.K15)
   with that automorphic sequence is available.

These are no-match and restored-power statements for the named
interfaces only. They do not assert that no theorem in the literature,
or no future coefficient-sensitive theorem, can estimate (161.K15).
